# Supervised linear projections (2D/3D) that maximize visible separation of two classes when one class is small (n < d)

Context for all sections: 192-dim residual stream of a small ViT, 600 tokens/frame, ~18–20 "tool" tokens vs ~580 "background" tokens, 806 checkpoints of the same frame; data = (checkpoint, token) pairs, strongly correlated along checkpoints. Goal: an orthonormal 3-frame A ∈ R^{192×3} with measurable, held-out-valid, checkpoint-stable separation of tool vs background.

Notation used below: n_1 = minority class size, n = total, d (or p) = 192, S_B / S_W = between- / within-class scatter (sums of squares), Σ_B / Σ_W = corresponding covariances, μ_1, μ_0 = class means, Δ = μ_1 − μ_0, A = d×k projection with AᵀA = I_k.

Sources were gathered with an Undermind deep search (workspace "ViT: detekcja końcówki narzędzia w chirurgii zaćmy", search "supervised linear projection class separation small n large p", https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893?path=/supervised%20linear%20projection%20class%20separation%20small%20n%20large%20p), full-text reads of 9 PDFs via Undermind, and web lookups. Scite was not used.

## Key question 1 — Fisher LDA with two classes: the single discriminant direction and how to obtain axes 2–3 for a 3D view

### Takeaway
With two classes S_B has rank 1, so Fisher LDA yields exactly one direction w ∝ S_W^{-1}Δ; any further "LDA axes" must come from a different criterion. The best-supported options are (i) LOL / "mean-difference + PCA of the class-centered residual" (Vogelstein et al. 2021, with a theorem for p ≥ n), (ii) a regularized frame-level projection-pursuit index (PDA index, Lee & Cook 2010), or (iii) direct Stiefel/Grassmann optimization of a regularized frame objective (Cunningham & Ghahramani 2015). Taking "directions of minimum within-class variance" as axes 2–3 is exactly the data-piling failure mode when n_1 < d and should be avoided.

### Cited Findings
- Fisher's original two-class discriminant: Fisher, R. A. (1936), "The use of multiple measurements in taxonomic problems", Annals of Eugenics 7:179–188 (DOI 10.1111/j.1469-1809.1936.tb02137.x) — [Undermind record Fis36](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-1809.1936.tb02137.x). The Undermind deep-search summary states: "Two-class Fisher LDA has rank-one between-class scatter, so it provides one canonical discriminant axis—not principled second and third supervised axes" — [Undermind deep search summary](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893?path=/supervised%20linear%20projection%20class%20separation%20small%20n%20large%20p).
- Cunningham & Ghahramani (2015), "Linear dimensionality reduction: survey, insights, and generalizations", JMLR 16:2859–2900, write every linear DR method as "minimize f_X(M) subject to M ∈ 𝓜" with 𝓜 = {M ∈ R^{d×r}: MᵀM = I} (Sec. 2.1, Eq. 1). Their orthogonal LDA program (Sec. 3.1.3, Eq. 6) is the "quotient-of-traces" max tr(MᵀΣ_B M)/tr(MᵀΣ_W M) s.t. MᵀM = I; for r = 1 the solution is the top eigenvector of Σ_W^{-1}Σ_B; for r > 1 they call it "a common misconception" that the top r generalized eigenvectors solve it, since those eigenvectors "will not in general be orthogonal" and solve a different trace-of-quotient objective. Their solver maps the unconstrained gradient onto the Stiefel manifold (Sec. 4, App. A); optimizing on the Grassmann manifold instead "empirically mattered very little" — [ar5iv HTML of arXiv:1406.0873](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.0873); JMLR listing DOI 10.5555/2789272.2912091 — [JMLR PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume16/cunningham15a/cunningham15a.pdf).
- The trace-ratio problem max tr(VᵀAV)/tr(VᵀBV), VᵀV = I (orthonormal projection, unlike the generalized-eigenvector "ratio-trace") and its iterative eigenvalue algorithm: Ngo, Bellalij & Saad (2012), "The Trace Ratio Optimization Problem", SIAM Review 54(3):545–569, DOI 10.1137/120864799 — [Undermind record Ngo12](https://doi.org/10.1137/120864799).
- LOL (Linear Optimal Low-rank projection), two-class construction (Methods, "The geometric intuition of LOL"): compute class means, Δ = μ̂_1 − μ̂_0, center each observation by its own class mean, take the eigenvectors of the resulting class-centered (within-class) covariance, and form the p×d projection [Δ, u_1, …, u_{d−1}]. The paper does not state that the concatenation is orthonormalized. Main theorem: under a Gaussian model with p ≥ n, LOL is at least as good (Chernoff information) as reduced-rank LDA, and under weak extra conditions at least as good as PCA; condition for beating PCA is essentially that the pooled covariance's d-th eigenvector carries less class information than Δ. Complexity O(npd/(Tc)). Vogelstein et al., "Supervised dimensionality reduction for big data", Nature Communications 12, 2872 (2021), DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-23102-2 — [full-text read via Undermind, Vog17](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23102-2).
- LOL simulations: 100 training samples in 100 features ("LDA cannot solve this p ≥ n problem"), and a geometric illustration with 100 samples in 1,000 dimensions where LOL is close to Bayes-optimal; the paper reports the lowest average cross-validated error across 7 real benchmarks (Wilcoxon p = 0.008) — [Vog17](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23102-2).
- Two-class LDA with more than one direction via "removal of classification structure": transform the data along the found discriminant direction into data with no discriminant information, then iterate to obtain the next direction; directions may be oblique to each other. Aladjem, M. E. (1997), IEEE TPAMI 19(2):187–192, DOI 10.1109/34.574805 — [Undermind record Ala97](https://doi.org/10.1109/34.574805).
- Sliced inverse regression: Li, K.-C. (1991), "Sliced inverse regression for dimension reduction", JASA 86(414):316–327, DOI 10.2307/2290563 — [search result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliced_inverse_regression). (See Inferences for why SIR gives ≤ 1 direction with a binary label.)
- Class-sensitive PCA (CSPCA): "a compromise of PCA and MDP [maximal data piling], that seeks new direction vectors for better Class-Sensitive visualization", with HDLSS asymptotics of the MDP vector — Miao, D. (2015), PhD dissertation (UNC, advisors Marron & Fine) — [Undermind record Mia15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85b15b25460efc1141af2e1ae8d106ab6bb9d32b). Not peer-reviewed.
- ULCA (unified linear comparative analysis) unifies discriminant analysis and contrastive learning in one weighted linear DR objective with an interactive refinement optimizer: Fujiwara, Wei, Zhao & Ma (2021/2023), "Interactive Dimensionality Reduction for Comparative Analysis", IEEE TVCG, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2021.3114807 — [Undermind record Fuj21](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2021.3114807).
- Supervised PCA via manifold (Stiefel/Grassmann) optimization that jointly solves prediction and DR: Ritchie, Scott, Balzano, Kessler & Sripada (2019), IEEE DSW, DOI 10.1109/DSW.2019.8755587 — [Undermind record Rit19b](https://doi.org/10.1109/DSW.2019.8755587).

### Inferences
- Because rank(S_B) = 1 for two classes, tr(AᵀS_B A) ≤ ΔᵀS_W^{-1}… more precisely the numerator of the quotient-of-traces objective is maximized already by one column; adding columns 2–3 can only reduce the denominator tr(AᵀS_W A). Hence optimizing Cunningham's orthonormal LDA objective with r = 3 and two classes makes axes 2–3 the directions of *minimum within-class variance* orthogonal to axis 1. When n_1 = 20 < d = 192, the minority-class scatter has rank ≤ 19 and S_W (rank ≤ n − 2 if n < d, or with near-zero eigenvalues from the heavily correlated checkpoints) has a large near-null space; such axes show "perfect" training separation that is an artifact (see KQ5, data piling). Therefore the frame objective must be regularized (S_W + λI, PDA index) or axes 2–3 must be chosen by a criterion that does not reward small within-class variance (LOL residual PCA).
- LOL is literally the "LDA + PCA of the residual" recipe from the question, with a theorem that covers p ≥ n. For a 3D view: axis 1 = Δ (or the shrinkage-LDA direction), axes 2–3 = top-2 eigenvectors of the class-centered covariance after projecting out axis 1, then Gram–Schmidt. These axes display the dominant within-class variation (e.g., the checkpoint-to-checkpoint drift of the whole frame), so they tend to be stable and are not label-overfit, but they do not add separation beyond axis 1.
- SIR with a binary Y: E[X | Y] takes two values, so the SIR kernel cov(E[X|Y]) has rank 1 and its single direction is Σ_total^{-1}Δ, which (by the Sherman–Morrison relation between total and within covariance) is proportional to the Fisher direction. SIR therefore cannot provide axes 2–3 either; second-moment methods (SAVE-type, using differences of class covariances) could, but they need a well-estimated 192×192 minority covariance from 20 points, which is infeasible unless checkpoints are pooled.
- The Aladjem (1997) iteration is the two-class analogue of "deflate and repeat": after projecting out axis 1, re-fit a discriminant criterion. With two classes and a rank-1 S_B, after exact deflation the mean difference in the complement is zero, so the only remaining "discriminant" signal is in second moments or in non-Gaussian structure; this is why a frame-level projection-pursuit index (KQ2) is the natural tool for axes 2–3.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found that specifically prescribes a recipe for "two classes → 3D frame" beyond LOL and PDA-index pursuit; CSPCA is a dissertation only.
- I did not verify Cook & Weisberg's 1991 SAVE citation this session; the SIR-rank argument above is a derivation, not a quoted result.

## Key question 2 — Projection pursuit with class-separation indices (LDA index, PDA index, holes/entropy/Gini), guided tours, tourr / spinifex / classifly

### Takeaway
The LDA index I_LDA(A) = 1 − |AᵀWA|/|Aᵀ(W+B)A| works on k-dimensional frames directly but degenerates when n < p; the PDA index of Lee & Cook (2010) adds a ridge term nλI to both determinants and is the only index in this family designed for large p small n, with λ chosen by a within-class-scatter balancing rule (S(λ) = 1). All of these are implemented in tourr (guided_tour with lda_pp / pda_pp), and the tour/lineup literature provides the right visual diagnostic.

### Cited Findings
- LDA index (Lee, Cook, Klinke & Lumley 2005): for A ∈ R^{p×k} with orthonormal columns, I_LDA(A) = 1 − |AᵀWA| / |Aᵀ(W+B)A| (= 0 if the denominator vanishes), with B = Σ_i n_i(X̄_i − X̄)(X̄_i − X̄)ᵀ and W = Σ_i Σ_j (X_ij − X̄_i)(X_ij − X̄_i)ᵀ — as restated in Wu & Zhang (2023), Eq. (2) — [Wu23v full-text read](https://doi.org/10.6339/23-jds1096); original: Lee, Cook, Klinke & Lumley (2005), "Projection Pursuit for Exploratory Supervised Classification", JCGS 14(4):831–846, DOI 10.1198/106186005X77702 — [Undermind record Lee05b](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186005X77702).
- PDA index (Lee & Cook 2010), on standardized data X^s_ij = diag(Σ̂)^{-1/2}(X_ij − X̄) with B_s, W_s the corresponding scatter matrices (B_s + W_s = n R̂, the correlation matrix): for 0 ≤ λ < 1,
  I_PDA(A, λ) = 1 − |Aᵀ{(1−λ)W_s + nλI_p}A| / |Aᵀ{(1−λ)(B_s+W_s) + nλI_p}A|.
  λ = 0 recovers the LDA index; the identity term "help[s] prevent data piling in high dimensions". Lee & Cook's empirical rule: define S(λ) = tr(W_PP)/(nk) for the data projected by the PDA solution at that λ and pick λ with S(λ) = 1; in the leukemia example this gave λ = 0.95; simulations compare λ = 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 — [Wu23v full-text read](https://doi.org/10.6339/23-jds1096). Original: Lee, E.-K. & Cook, D. (2010), "A projection pursuit index for large p small n data", Statistics and Computing 20(3):381–392, DOI 10.1007/s11222-009-9131-1 — [Monash record](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/a-projection-pursuit-index-for-large-p-small-n-data/); abstract: "Most indexes will not work well when there are a small number of observations relative to the number of variables" — [Ewha record](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/a-projection-pursuit-index-for-large-p-small-n-data/).
- Wu & Zhang (2023) HDLSS simulations (n = 40, two classes of 20, p = 500; n/p from ≈0.02 to 0.20): when n/p > 15 % every method except LDA exceeded 95 % accuracy; as n/p fell PDA stayed ≥ 70 % while linear SVM fell to 20 % at the extreme; LDA never exceeded 85 % in the informative-variable experiment; the authors "advise against LDA in high-dimensional settings" and recommend PDA for large-p-small-n data. Implementation: R package classPP; PDAindex() also in PPtreeViz (default lambda = 0.1) — [Wu23v](https://doi.org/10.6339/23-jds1096); [PPtreeViz PDAindex docs](https://rdocumentation.org/packages/PPtreeViz/versions/2.0.4/topics/PDAindex).
- Projection-pursuit guided tour (combining grand tour and PP optimisation): Cook, Buja, Cabrera & Hurley (1995), JCGS 4(3):155–172, DOI 10.1080/10618600.1995.10474674 — [Undermind record Coo95](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.1995.10474674). Software: Wickham, Cook, Hofmann & Buja (2011), "tourr: An R Package for Exploring Multivariate Data with Projections", J. Stat. Softw. 40(2), DOI 10.18637/jss.v040.i02 (grand, guided and little tours into 1, 2, 3 or d dimensions) — [Undermind record Wic11](https://doi.org/10.18637/JSS.V040.I02). tourr's lda_pp(cl) documentation refers to Cook & Swayne (2007) for the equations; the pda_pp reference page was not retrievable in this session — [tourr lda_pp reference](https://ggobi.github.io/tourr/reference/lda_pp.html).
- Manual tours (control the coefficient of one variable to test sensitivity of a structure to that variable), designed to follow a guided tour: Spyrison & Cook (2020), "spinifex: An R Package for Creating a Manual Tour …", The R Journal 12(1):243–257 — [R Journal](https://journal.r-project.org/articles/RJ-2020-027); CRAN spinifex 0.3.10 (2025-09-18) — [CRAN](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/spinifex/index.html).
- Simplified manual controls for projection and slice tours with application to exploring classification boundaries in high dimensions: Laa, Cook, Lee & Valencia (2023), JCGS 32(4):1229–1236, DOI 10.1080/10618600.2023.2206459 — [Undermind record Laa22](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2023.2206459).
- classifly (Wickham, maintained by Cook), CRAN 0.4.3 (2026-03-15): "implements methods for understanding the division of space between the groups" of a classifier in p-dimensional space — [CRAN classifly](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/classifly/index.html).
- Desired properties of a PP index — smoothness, squintability, flexibility, rotation invariance, speed — and the finding that all tested new indexes fall short on some property: Laa & Cook (2020), Computational Statistics 35:1171–1205, DOI 10.1007/s00180-020-00954-8 — [arXiv:1902.00181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.00181). (2021), The R Journal 13(2):542 — [Undermind record H21](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893).
- Other PP-for-classification work surfaced: Espezúa et al. (2015), "A Projection Pursuit framework for supervised dimension reduction of high dimensional small sample datasets", Neurocomputing 149:767–776, DOI 10.1016/j.neucom.2014.07.057 — [Undermind record Esp15](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.NEUCOM.2014.07.057); Hou & Wentzell (2014), "Regularized projection pursuit for data with a small sample-to-variable ratio", Metabolomics 10:589–606, DOI 10.1007/s11306-013-0612-z — [Undermind record Hou14](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11306-013-0612-z); Barcaru (2019), "Supervised projection pursuit – A dimensionality reduction technique optimized for probabilistic classification", Chemometrics Intell. - Unsupervised cluster-finding PP (for comparison): Hofmeyr & Pavlidis (2019), "PPCI: an R Package for Cluster Identification using Projection Pursuit", The R Journal 11(1), DOI 10.32614/RJ-2019-046 — [Undermind record Hof19c](https://doi.org/10.32614/RJ-2019-046).

### Inferences
- For our frame A ∈ R^{192×3}, I_PDA is a smooth function of A and can be maximized by tourr's guided_tour(pda_pp(cl, lambda)) in 3D, or by any Stiefel optimizer (the index depends on A only through span(A), so the Grassmann manifold is the natural domain). λ should be chosen by the S(λ) = 1 rule on held-out checkpoints or by grouped CV (KQ5) rather than fixed at the PPtreeViz default 0.1; Lee & Cook's own example used λ = 0.95 at n/p ≈ 0.1, which suggests strong regularization is typical when the minority class is tiny.
- Since I_LDA = I_PDA(λ=0) and with n_1 = 20 the minority within-class scatter is rank-deficient, I_LDA → 1 for any 3-frame that contains a null-space direction of W — a projection-pursuit version of data piling. This is the concrete reason to use the PDA index (or an explicit ridge) for axes 2–3.
- The holes / central-mass / entropy / Gini indices in Lee et al. (2005) measure distributional shape rather than labelled separation; in our γ regime (KQ5) they can lock onto noise structure, so they are best used only as secondary diagnostics on the view found by PDA/LDA pursuit.

- The exact definitions of the Gini/entropy indices in Lee et al. (2005) were not retrieved.
- tourr's pda_pp documentation page was not fetched (the lda_pp page does not list it).

## Key question 3 — Regularized / shrinkage / penalized discriminant analysis (Friedman RDA, Ledoit–Wolf, Witten & Tibshirani penalized LDA, Hastie–Buja–Tibshirani PDA, DWD / maximal data piling)

### Takeaway
When n_1 < d the within-class covariance is singular and the Fisher objective is unbounded along any null-space direction; the standard fixes are convex shrinkage toward a scaled identity (Friedman's γ, Ledoit–Wolf's analytic ρ; sklearn shrinkage='auto'), a diagonal within-class estimate with an L1 penalty (Witten & Tibshirani), or a roughness/ridge penalty Ω (Hastie–Buja–Tibshirani). For the axis-1 direction itself, DWD was designed to avoid HDLSS data piling, and weighted DWD handles class imbalance.

### Cited Findings
- Friedman (1989) RDA: Σ̂_k(λ) = S_k(λ)/W_k(λ) with S_k(λ) = (1−λ)S_k + λS, W_k(λ) = (1−λ)W_k + λW (Eq. 16; λ = 0 QDA, λ = 1 LDA), then Σ̂_k(λ,γ) = (1−γ)Σ̂_k(λ) + (γ/p)·tr[Σ̂_k(λ)]·I (Eq. 18). λ, γ chosen jointly by leave-one-out CV of misclassification on a grid of 25–50 points (5×5 grid λ ∈ {0, .125, .354, .650, 1}, γ ∈ {0, .25, .5, .75, 1} in simulations). When N_k ≤ p the class sample covariance is singular with p − N_k + 1 zero eigenvalues; LDA is "ill- or poorly posed" when total N is less than or comparable to p. Friedman, J. H. (1989), JASA 84(405):165–175, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1989.10478752 — [full-text read via Undermind, Fri89](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1989.10478752).
- Ledoit & Wolf (2004): a distribution-free estimator that is "the asymptotically optimal convex linear combination of the sample covariance matrix with the identity matrix" under quadratic loss, as n and p go to infinity together; it is both well-conditioned and more accurate than the sample covariance. J. Multivariate Anal. 88(2):365–411, DOI 10.1016/S0047-259X(03)00096-4 — [Undermind record Led04](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0047-259X(03)00096-4).
- scikit-learn LinearDiscriminantAnalysis: shrinkage='auto' uses the Ledoit–Wolf lemma; shrinkage only with solver='lsqr' or 'eigen' (not 'svd'); a float in [0,1] fixes the amount; a custom covariance_estimator may be passed instead — [sklearn docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.discriminant_analysis.LinearDiscriminantAnalysis.html).
- Witten & Tibshirani (2011) penalized LDA: with a diagonal within-class estimate Σ̃_w = diag(σ̂_1², …, σ̂_p²), the k-th discriminant vector solves
  max_β { βᵀ Σ̂_b^k β − λ_k Σ_j |σ̂_j β_j| } s.t. βᵀ Σ̃_w β ≤ 1 (LDA-L1, Eq. 17),
  with Σ̂_b^k = (1/n) XᵀY(YᵀY)^{-1/2} P_k^⊥ (YᵀY)^{-1/2} YᵀX projecting out previous vectors in class-score space (so later vectors are sequentially orthogonalized without imposing Σ_w-orthogonality on penalized vectors); λ_k = λ·‖Σ̃_w^{-1/2} Σ̂_b^k Σ̃_w^{-1/2}‖ and λ is chosen by cross-validation; the number of discriminant vectors is a further tuning parameter. When p > n "a vector in [the] null space [of Σ̂_w] but not in the null space of the between-class covariance can make the Fisher objective arbitrarily large" (their reason for the diagonal estimate). JRSS-B 73(5):753–772, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00783.x — [full-text read via Undermind, Wit11](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00783.x).
- Hastie, Buja & Tibshirani (1995) penalized discriminant analysis: LDA recast as optimal scoring (regression) so that any penalized regression (e.g. a spatial smoothness penalty for pixel features) can be used; designed "for situations in which there are many highly correlated predictors". Annals of Statistics 23(1):73–102, DOI 10.1214/aos/1176324456 — [Undermind record Has95](https://doi.org/10.1214/AOS/1176324456).
- Sparse LDA by thresholding with p ≫ n theory: Shao, Wang, Deng & Wang (2011), Annals of Statistics 39(2):1241–1265, DOI 10.1214/10-AOS870 — [Undermind record Sha11e](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-AOS870). Direct sparse discriminant analysis in ultra-high dimensions: Mai, Zou & Yuan (2012), Biometrika 99(1):29–42 — [Undermind record Mai12](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893). Sparse LDA for HDLSS: Qiao, Zhou & Huang (2009) — [Undermind record Qia09](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/029ae5240b472145661e65e818f54de0acaf8597).
- Naive-Bayes/independence rule beats Fisher LDA when p > n (rigorous HDLSS theory; Fisher's rule degrades because of diverging spectra and the low-rank Moore–Penrose inverse of the sample covariance): Bickel & Levina (2004), Bernoulli 10(6):989–1010, DOI 10.3150/bj/1106314847 — [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bernoulli/volume-10/issue-6/Some-theory-for-Fishers-linear-discriminant-function-naive-Bayes-and/10.3150/bj/1106314847.full).
- Maximal data piling (MDP): in HDLSS settings there are directions onto which "projections of data … take on only two distinct values, one for each class"; infinitely many such directions exist in the data subspace, and the MDP vector maximizes the distance between the two piles. Ahn & Marron (2010), Biometrika 97(1):254–259, DOI 10.1093/biomet/asp084 — [Undermind record Ahn10](https://doi.org/10.1093/BIOMET/ASP084). Follow-up: a negatively-ridged discriminant vector can achieve data piling of independent test data under a spiked-covariance model ("double data piling"): Chang, Ahn & Jung (2021), Electron. J. Statist., DOI 10.1214/21-EJS1945 — [Undermind record Cha21o](https://doi.org/10.1214/21-ejs1945).
- DWD for HDLSS and class imbalance: Qiao, Zhang, Liu, Todd & Marron (2010), "Weighted Distance Weighted Discrimination and Its Asymptotic Properties", JASA 105(489):401–414, DOI 10.1198/jasa.2010.tm08487 — [Undermind record Qia10](https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2010.tm08487); sparse DWD: Wang & Zou (2015), JCGS 25(3):826–838 — [Undermind record Wan15i](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893). DiProPerm's authors note that DWD "better avoids the data-piling problem exhibited by SVM" — [Wei13c full-text read](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773).

### Inferences
- For axis 1 the practical estimator is w_1 ∝ (Ŝ_W + λI)^{-1} Δ with λ from Ledoit–Wolf (analytic, no CV) or from grouped CV; this is Friedman's γ-shrinkage with λ_Friedman = 1 (pooled), and is exactly what sklearn LDA(solver='eigen', shrinkage='auto') computes. Because the pooled S_W is dominated by the ~580 background tokens, the shrinkage is mostly controlling background anisotropy, which is what the view needs.
- Witten–Tibshirani's diagonal Σ̃_w is attractive here only if a sparse (few-neuron) axis is wanted for interpretability; sparsity also makes the Bickel–Kur–Nadler "sparse projections are asymptotically Gaussian" result applicable (KQ5), i.e. sparse axes are far less able to manufacture fake structure.
- The MDP/Ahn–Marron results explain precisely what an unregularized 3-frame does with 20 tool tokens: there exist directions in span of the data where all 20 tool tokens project to a single point; any objective rewarding low within-class variance (LDA index, quotient-of-traces, silhouette) will find them. "Perfect separation" of training data in n_1 < d is therefore not evidence.

### Gaps
- No source found that compares Ledoit–Wolf-LDA vs PDA-index vs LOL on the same HDLSS visualisation task.
- The Bickel & Levina 2004 paper was seen only through secondary descriptions.

## Key question 4 — Neighbourhood / metric-learning and PCA-flavoured supervised methods (NCA, LMNN, LFDA, supervised PCA, contrastive PCA, PLS-DA)

### Takeaway
NCA, LMNN and LFDA optimize local (kNN) structure and can output a 3D embedding directly, but with 20 minority points in 192 dimensions they have far more parameters (d×3 or d×d) than minority observations and must be early-stopped / validated on held-out checkpoints. Supervised PCA (HSIC) with a two-class delta kernel reduces to one informative direction; contrastive PCA is explicitly not a discriminant method and needs a well-estimated 192×192 target covariance; PLS-DA's first component is essentially the mean-difference direction and is the best-documented over-optimism trap in the n < p regime.

### Cited Findings
- NCA: learns a Mahalanobis metric / linear transformation A by "directly maximizing a stochastic variant of the leave-one-out KNN score on the training set"; "the same method can learn a low-dimensional linear embedding of labeled data that can be used for data visualization"; non-parametric. Goldberger, Roweis, Hinton & Salakhutdinov (2005), NIPS 17:513–520 — [NIPS metadata](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2004/file/42fe880812925e520249e808937738d2-Metadata.json); [NYU copy of the paper](https://cs.nyu.edu/home/people/in_memoriam/roweis/papers/ncanips.pdf). Standard form of the objective (as in the paper): maximize Σ_i Σ_{j∈C_i} p_ij with p_ij = exp(−‖Ax_i − Ax_j‖²)/Σ_{k≠i} exp(−‖Ax_i − Ax_k‖²), A ∈ R^{r×d}.
- LMNN: metric "trained with the goal that the k-nearest neighbors always belong to the same class while examples from different classes are separated by a large margin"; convex (hinge loss), solved as a semidefinite program over M = LᵀL. Weinberger & Saul (2009), JMLR 10:207–244 — [JMLR page](https://jmlr.org/papers/v10/weinberger09a.html); NIPS 2005 version with Blitzer — [Undermind record Wei05b](https://doi.org/10.5555/1577069.1577078).
- LFDA: combines Fisher discriminant analysis with locality-preserving projection using an affinity matrix over same-class pairs; closed form via a generalized eigenvalue problem; handles multimodal classes; kernelizable. Sugiyama (2007), JMLR 8:1027–1061 — [JMLR page](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/sugiyama07b.html). A secondary source states LFDA "does not suffer from the restriction that FDA's embedding dimension be less than the class count, in general" — [search result (semi-supervised LFDA paper)](https://paperity.org/p/7496502/semi-supervised-local-fisher-discriminant-analysis-for-dimensionality-reduction). Weighted-scatter form (unlocalized weights 1/n_ℓ within class; 1/n − 1/n_ℓ same-class and 1/n cross-class for between) — [JSAI 2007 note](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2007/data/pdf/100294.pdf).
- Supervised PCA (HSIC-based): maximize tr(Uᵀ X H L H Xᵀ U) s.t. UᵀU = I, with L the label kernel (delta kernel for classification); Barshan, Ghodsi, Azimifar & Zolghadri Jahromi (2011), Pattern Recognition 44(7):1357–1371, DOI 10.1016/j.patcog.2010.12.015 — [Undermind record Bar11d](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2010.12.015); description of the HSIC formulation from secondary sources — [search results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03148).
- Contrastive PCA: v* = argmax_{‖v‖=1} vᵀ(C_X − αC_Y)v, cPCs = top eigenvectors of C_X − αC_Y; α = 0 is PCA of the target; default α selection: 40 log-spaced α in [0.1, 1000], spectral clustering of the resulting subspaces with affinity = product of cosines of principal angles, medoid α per cluster; sample cPC convergence O_p(√(d/min(n,m))); the paper states cPCA "is not for classifying or discriminating" and the enriched subspace "is not necessarily the subspace optimal for target-versus-background classification". Abid, Zhang, Bagaria & Zou (2018), Nature Communications 9:2134, DOI 10.1038/s41467-018-04608-8 — [full-text read via Undermind, Abi18](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04608-8).
- Supervised PCA by feature screening then PCA: Bair, Hastie, Paul & Tibshirani (2006), "Prediction by supervised principal components", JASA 101(473):119–137 — surfaced by the Undermind deep search as Bai06b with the summary's caveat that "Supervised PCA and PLS-DA are further options, but their tuning and apparent separation are particularly vulnerable to overfitting in small samples" — [Undermind deep search summary](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893?path=/supervised%20linear%20projection%20class%20separation%20small%20n%20large%20p). (DOI 10.1198/016214505000000628 from memory; not verified this session.)
- PLS-DA vs LDA: "for two equal class sizes, PLS-DA using one partial least squares (PLS) component provides equivalent classification results to Euclidean distance to centroids", and using all non-zero components is equivalent to LDA; PLS-DA has "no significant advantage over traditional procedures for classification" and is "an algorithm full of dangers", though weights/loadings are useful for exploration. Brereton & Lloyd (2014), J. Chemometrics 28:213–225, DOI 10.1002/cem.2609 — [abstract via scholarhub](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/:slug/e36860e3-6211-43f4-85ce-361d9ed0eb13).
- PLS-DA over-optimism (synthetic random labels, true accuracy 50 %): internal CV gives overly optimistic estimates; overfitting peaks when the training-subset size approaches the feature dimensionality minus one, strongest for LOO; beyond that point extra noisy features act as regularization; ranking by overfitting: bootstrap best, then bootstrapped Latin partitions, random subsampling, K-fold, LOO worst. Rodríguez-Pérez, Fernández & Marco (2018), Anal. Bioanal. Chem. 410(23):5981–5992, DOI 10.1007/s00216-018-1217-1 — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s00216-018-1217-1&format=json&resultType=core).
- PLS-DA review: PLS-DA can produce a "good model even with null data" and the probability of unrealistic separation increases with the number of variables; a permutation test is "essential"; preprocessing must be estimated inside each training fold; LOOCV unreliable for complexity tuning, prefer Monte-Carlo / double / repeated double CV. Lee, Liong & Jemain (2018), Analyst 143:3526–3539, DOI 10.1039/c8an00599k — [full-text read via Undermind, Lee18g](https://doi.org/10.1039/c8an00599k). The review describes the PLS weight as "estimated by maximizing the covariance between X and y" and does not state PLS-DA ≡ LDA — [Lee18g](https://doi.org/10.1039/c8an00599k).
- Further PLS-DA validation literature: Westerhuis et al. (2008), "Assessment of PLSDA cross validation", Metabolomics 4:81–89 — [Springer link from search](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11306-007-0099-6); Gromski et al. (2015), Anal. Chim. Acta 879:10–23, DOI 10.1016/j.aca.2015.02.012 — [Undermind record Gro15](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aca.2015.02.012); Triba et al. (2015), Mol. BioSyst. 11:13–19, DOI 10.1039/c4mb00414k (row-permutation changes K-fold conclusions) — [Undermind record Tri15b](https://doi.org/10.1039/c4mb00414k); Westerhuis et al. (2008) DQ² — DOI 10.1007/s11306-008-0126-2 — [Undermind record Wes08c](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11306-008-0126-2).
- Supervised visualisation by a neural centroid-encoder (non-linear, for comparison): Ghosh & Kirby (2022), JMLR 23(20):1–34 — [arXiv:2002.11934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11934). Discriminative DR to visualise classifiers (Fisher-metric-weighted t-SNE): Schulz, Gisbrecht & Hammer (2015), Neural Process. Lett. 42:27–54, DOI 10.1007/s11063-014-9394-1 — [Undermind record Sch15k](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11063-014-9394-1).

### Inferences
- Supervised PCA with a two-class delta kernel: H L H has rank 1 (L = YYᵀ, centered), so X H L H Xᵀ ∝ (n_0 n_1/n)·ΔΔᵀ is rank 1 — the first supervised PC is the (total-covariance-unwhitened) mean-difference direction and further "supervised" components carry no label information. It therefore gives at most axis 1, and that axis is Δ rather than S_W^{-1}Δ.
- cPCA with target = tool tokens: C_X from 20 points in 192 dims has rank ≤ 19 and the stated rate √(d/min(n,m)) ≈ √(192/20) ≈ 3.1 means the sample cPCs are essentially unestimated unless tool tokens are pooled across many checkpoints (then m, n ≫ d but the pooled samples are correlated along checkpoints, see KQ5). With background = background tokens, cPCA answers "where do tool tokens vary more than background", not "where are they separated".
- NCA/LMNN/LFDA in 3D: a rank-3 NCA transformation has 576 free parameters; with 20 minority points the leave-one-out soft-kNN objective can be driven to ~1 on training data. Use them only with (a) early stopping on held-out checkpoints, (b) initialization from the regularized LDA/LOL frame, and (c) comparison to a permutation null. LFDA has the advantage of a closed-form eigen-solution and of not being limited to C − 1 = 1 dimension, so it can give 3 axes; its local scatter matrices need the same ridge as LDA when n_1 < d.
- PLS-DA's first weight vector is Xᵀy ∝ Δ (for centered X and ±1 coding), i.e. LOL's first axis; later PLS components are deflated covariances with y, so in the n < p regime they will chase label noise (Rodríguez-Pérez's peak of overfitting at n_train ≈ p − 1).

### Gaps
- The exact affinity-weighted local scatter formulas of LFDA were not retrieved from the JMLR PDF; the NCA and LMNN objectives are written from standard knowledge, with the source papers cited for their verbal statements.
- Bair et al. (2006) DOI not verified this session.

## Key question 5 — Direct optimization of a separation criterion on the Grassmann/Stiefel manifold, and measuring visible separation in a 2D/3D view

### Takeaway
Any smooth separation criterion (quotient-of-traces, PDA index, soft-silhouette, soft-kNN hit rate, projected t-statistic) can be maximized over orthonormal 3-frames with Stiefel projected-gradient or Grassmann methods; what matters is that the criterion is regularized and validated, because in n_1 < d the unregularized ones have degenerate maxima. For *measuring* separation in the view, the vis literature offers DSC (distance consistency) and neighbourhood-graph class-purity measures (SepMe), while the statistics literature offers permutation-calibrated projected statistics (DiProPerm, PDC) that are valid in HDLSS.

### Cited Findings
- Manifold formulation and solver: see Cunningham & Ghahramani (KQ1) — Stiefel projected gradient with line search (Sec. 4, App. A); Grassmann equivalent when f depends only on span(M) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1406.0873). Trace-ratio solver: Ngo, Bellalij & Saad (2012), SIAM Review, DOI 10.1137/120864799 — [Undermind record Ngo12](https://doi.org/10.1137/120864799). Manifold optimization toolboxes referenced in the search: Manopt (Boumal & Mishra) — [Undermind record Bou13b](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893); trivializations for gradient-based optimization on manifolds: Lezcano-Casado (2019), NeurIPS — [Undermind record Cas19h](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893).
- Distance Consistency (DSC) and an entropy-based "class consistency" measure for ranking scatterplot views of labelled data; validated against user-chosen "good views" with good precision/recall. Sips, Neubert, Lewis & Hanrahan (2009), Computer Graphics Forum 28(3):831–838, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01467.x — [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v28i3pp0831-0838); [Konstanz page](https://graphics.uni-konstanz.de/publikationen/Sips2009SelectingGoodViews/index.html).
- Existing separation measures "failed in over half the cases overall, and in over two-thirds of the cases involving real datasets" when used to pick DR views; taxonomy of visual cluster-separation factors (scale, point distance, shape, position; within- vs between-cluster) from 75 datasets, 4 DR techniques, 3 encodings. Sedlmair, Tatu, Munzner & Tory (2012), Computer Graphics Forum 31(3pt4):1335–1344 — [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/items/c5481850-330a-4fd3-a7e2-a72bad21328a).
- SepMe: 2002 measures = 17 neighbourhood graphs × 14 class-purity functions; >58 % of them beat DSC at predicting human separation judgements; best reaches 92.9 % accuracy (+11.7 % over DSC). Aupetit & Sedlmair (2016), IEEE PacificVis — [PDF](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf); [project page](https://sepme.vda.univie.ac.at/papers.php). Visual analysis of class-separation measures: Bernard et al. (2021), Computers & Graphics 96:48–60, DOI 10.1016/j.cag.2021.03.004 — [Undermind record Ber21e](https://doi.org/10.1016/J.CAG.2021.03.004); quality measures for projections of labelled data: Benato, Falcão & Telea (2023), Computers & Graphics 116:287–297, DOI 10.1016/j.cag.2023.08.023 — [Undermind record Ben23c](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2023.08.023).
- DiProPerm (Direction–Projection–Permutation): fit a binary linear direction (MD, Fisher LD, SVM, DWD, MDP), project to 1D, compute a two-sample statistic (t, mean difference, scaled MD, median/MAD, AUC); permute labels keeping group sizes and *refit the direction for every permutation*; typically 1,000 permutations; report empirical p (fraction of permuted statistics ≥ observed), Gaussian-fit p and z-score. Figure 1: two groups of 50 from the same N(0, I_1000) look well separated on a DWD direction fitted to their labels, and a 100-permutation test finds this non-significant. FLD + mean-difference statistic is a scaled Hotelling T², not computable in the usual way when the covariance is rank-deficient; PCA is unsuitable as the direction because its statistic is invariant under relabelling. Exact for equality of distributions; MD-MD exact for equality of means under normality with balanced sizes; MD-t invalid under heterogeneous covariances; a scaled-MD variant proposed for unbalanced sizes. Wei, Lee, Wichers, Li & Marron (2016), JCGS 25(2):549–569, DOI 10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773 — [full-text read via Undermind, Wei13c](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773). R package: Allmon, Marron & Hudgens (2021), "diproperm", The R Journal 13(1):266–272, DOI 10.32614/RJ-2021-072 — [Undermind record All20d](https://doi.org/10.32614/rj-2021-072).
- Population Difference Criterion (PDC) = (C − E_0 C)/√Var_0 C with C the projected mean difference along an MD or DWD direction, null moments from label permutations that refit the direction; *balanced* permutations (≈ equal numbers of swapped labels) recommended in high-signal cases because unbalanced relabellings retain real signal and make the all-permutation PDC non-monotone; bootstrap CI over the permutation set (B = 100); simulation n = m = 100, d ∈ {1, 10, 100}; cancer data n = 1,523, d = 12,478. Yang, Hannig, Hoadley, Carmichael & Marron (2023), JCGS, DOI 10.1080/10618600.2023.2276113 — [full-text read via Undermind, Yan21v](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2023.2276113).
- Permutation tests for classification error (theory via Rademacher averages; neuroimaging/microarray examples): Golland, Liang, Mukherjee & Panchenko (2005), COLT, DOI 10.1007/11503415_34 — [Undermind record Gol05c](https://doi.org/10.1007/11503415_34).

### Inferences
- A practical Stiefel objective for our case: f(A) = −log I_PDA(A, λ) or −(projected separation on axis 1 + weighted soft-silhouette in 3D) with Σ_W replaced by Σ_W + λI; optimize with projected gradient + QR retraction (Cunningham) or on the Grassmannian; initialize from the LOL frame (KQ1) so that the optimizer only needs to refine axes 2–3. Early-stop on a held-out block of checkpoints.
- For "measurable separation" report two numbers: (1) PDC/DiProPerm z-score of the projected tool-vs-background difference along axis 1 on *held-out* checkpoints with the full pipeline refit under each (balanced) permutation; (2) a view-level measure in the 3D scatter — DSC (fraction of points whose nearest class centroid in the view is their own) or a SepMe-style kNN class-purity, plus silhouette. DSC is cheap and standard but Sedlmair et al. show such measures disagree with humans in >50 % of real cases, so pair it with a lineup (KQ6).
- Because the direction is refit per permutation, the permutation null for the whole 3-frame pipeline costs 1,000 × (one shrinkage-LDA + one 192×192 eigendecomposition), i.e. seconds on GPU — feasible for every checkpoint window.

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly optimizes silhouette or DSC on the Stiefel manifold for labelled visualisation; this is an inference from the general framework.
- The SepMe measures were seen only via the project page and search snippets; which specific measure is "best" was not retrieved.

## Key question 6 — Overfitting and spurious structure when n < d: perfect separation, data piling, projection pursuit on noise, and the controls (permutation nulls, lineups, grouped CV, held-out tokens/frames)

### Takeaway
Three independent results say that with 20 tool tokens in 192 dimensions *any* labelling is linearly separable and *any* 1D/2D empirical distribution of the minority class can be manufactured by choosing the projection: Cover's counting theorem, Ahn–Marron data piling, and Bickel–Kur–Nadler's theorems (for the minority class the relevant ratio is p/n_1 ≈ 10 > 1). The required controls are: refit-everything label permutations (DiProPerm/lineups), cross-validation that holds out whole checkpoints (grouped/blocked CV, not LOO over tokens), and reporting separation only on held-out data.

### Cited Findings
- Cover's function-counting theorem: for N points in general position in R^d the number of linearly separable dichotomies is C(N, d) = 2 Σ_{k=0}^{d−1} binom(N−1, k); all 2^N dichotomies are separable when N ≤ d + 1, and the capacity (half of dichotomies separable) is N = 2d. Cover, T. M. (1965), IEEE Trans. Electronic Computers EC-14(3):326–334 (DOI 10.1109/PGEC.1965.264137, from memory) — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover%27s_theorem); [lecture note](https://www.cns.nyu.edu/%7Eeorhan/notes/covers-theorem.pdf).
- Data piling: Ahn & Marron (2010) — see KQ3 — [Ahn10](https://doi.org/10.1093/BIOMET/ASP084). Witten & Tibshirani: when p > n a null-space vector of Σ̂_w "can make the Fisher objective arbitrarily large" — [Wit11](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2011.00783.x).
- Bickel, Kur & Nadler (2018), "Projection pursuit in high dimensions", PNAS 115(37):9151–9156, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1801177115 (PMC6140545), for i.i.d. x_i ~ N(0, I_p), with ‖·‖_∞ the sup-norm between cdfs:
  - Theorem 1 (p/n → ∞): for any target cdf G there are data-dependent unit projections z_n with ‖Ĝ_{z,n} − G‖_∞ → 0; Remark 2 extends this to k-dimensional projections (explicit for two orthonormal directions and any bivariate G).
  - Theorem 2 (p/n → γ > 1): if μ_2(G) < γ − 1 such projections exist; if μ_2(G) > (1 + √γ)² they do not; Corollary 1: for γ > 1 any distribution is achievable up to scale and shift.
  - Theorem 3 (0 < γ ≤ 1): for any L > 1 and G with second moment < L − 1, projections exist converging to (γ/L)G + (1 − γ/L)N(0,1).
  - Theorem 4 (0 < γ < 1): if ‖G − Φ‖_∞ > C√(γ log(1/γ)) no projection sequence converges to G.
  - Theorem 5 (p/n → 0): all projections are uniformly asymptotically Gaussian.
  - Theorem 6 (p/n → γ > 0, s/n → 0): all s-sparse projections are uniformly asymptotically Gaussian.
  - Practical conclusion quoted: "in the 'small n, large p' setting, unless sparsity is enforced, and regardless of the chosen projection index, projection pursuit may detect an apparent structure that has no statistical significance." — [full-text read via Undermind, Bic18](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1801177115); [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1073/pnas.1801177115&format=json&resultType=core).
- Classical precursor: for p/n → 0 most projections are nearly Gaussian — Diaconis & Freedman (1984), "Asymptotics of graphical projection pursuit", Ann. Statist. 12(3):793–815 — [Undermind record Dia84](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893).
- Visual inference / lineups: the real plot is placed among null plots generated under H_0 (e.g. permuted labels); if a viewer can pick out the real plot, the structure is significant; the "Rorschach" protocol calibrates the analyst's sense of noise. Wickham, Cook, Hofmann & Buja (2010), "Graphical inference for infovis", IEEE TVCG 16(6):973–979, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2010.161 (InfoVis best paper) — [Monash record](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/graphical-inference-for-infovis/); R package nullabor — [CRAN readme](https://ftp.fau.de/cran/web/packages/nullabor/readme/README.html). Lineups applied to *random class separations in HDLSS data* (projection-pursuit views of permuted-label data can look as separated as real ones): Roy Chowdhury, Cook, Hofmann, Majumder, Lee & Toth (2015), "Using visual statistical inference to better understand random class separations in high dimension, low sample size data", Computational Statistics 30:293–316, DOI 10.1007/s00180-014-0534-x — [Undermind record Cho14d](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-014-0534-x) (title and deep-search summary only; abstract unavailable).
- DiProPerm's demonstration that two N(0, I_1000) samples of 50 look separated on a label-fitted direction, and its rule that the direction must be refit under each permutation — [Wei13c](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773). Balanced permutations for high-signal cases — [Yan21v](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2023.2276113).
- Cross-validation with dependent observations: random splitting of temporally/spatially/hierarchically structured data gives over-optimistic error estimates; hold out whole blocks/groups ("block CV"). Roberts et al. (2017), Ecography 40(8):913–929, DOI 10.1111/ecog.02881 — [Undermind record Rob17](https://doi.org/10.1111/ECOG.02881). hv-block CV for dependent data: Racine (2000), J. Econometrics 99(1):39–61 — [Undermind record Rac00](https://app.undermind.ca/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893).
- Over-optimism quantification and CV ranking (bootstrap > … > K-fold > LOO; peak overfit at n_train ≈ p − 1): Rodríguez-Pérez et al. (2018) — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s00216-018-1217-1&format=json&resultType=core). Double CV + permutation protocol (inner k = 7 for #components, outer k = 8 for assessment, repeated 20–30×, 2,000–3,000 permutations; NMC and AUROC more sensitive than Q²/DQ²; NMC = 0 or Q² = 0.99 can arise by chance): Szymańska, Saccenti, Smilde & Westerhuis (2012), Metabolomics 8:S3–S16, DOI 10.1007/s11306-011-0330-3 — [full-text read via Undermind, Szy11](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11306-011-0330-3).
- Montanari & Zhou (2022) extend projection-pursuit-in-noise to k-dimensional projections and interpolation in two-layer networks (arXiv) — [Undermind record Mon22](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893). Not peer-reviewed as retrieved.

### Inferences
- Which γ applies to us: per checkpoint, n = 600 tokens and p = 192 gives γ = p/n = 0.32 (Theorem 3/4 regime: mixtures with non-Gaussian weight up to γ/L and sup-norm deviations up to ≈ C·0.60 are reachable in pure noise). For the minority class alone, p/n_1 = 192/20 ≈ 9.6 > 1 (Theorem 2 regime): any empirical distribution of the 20 tool tokens with second moment < 8.6 (in standardized units) can be produced by some direction in pure noise, and by Remark 2 any 2D/3D joint shape too. Hence the *visual shape* of the tool cluster in a label-fitted 3D view carries no evidence by itself; only its position relative to a permutation null does.
- Pooling checkpoints raises nominal n (806 × 20 ≈ 16k tool tokens) but the tokens of the same frame across adjacent checkpoints are nearly duplicates; the effective sample size is unknown and probably close to the number of "independent" checkpoint epochs. The right CV unit is therefore the checkpoint (or a contiguous block of checkpoints), with blocked folds (Roberts et al.), and additionally a *token*-held-out test (e.g. leave out spatial token rows) to check that separation is not driven by a few token positions.
- Label-permutation null must preserve the structure: permute the tool/background labels *within each checkpoint* (keeping ~20 tool labels per checkpoint), refit the whole pipeline (shrinkage, λ choice, LOL/PDA frame), recompute the separation statistic on the held-out checkpoints. Use balanced permutations (Yang et al.) if the signal is strong, otherwise the permutation null collapses to p = 0 and loses resolution; the PDC z-score is then the "measurable separation".
- Sparsity as a defence: Theorem 6 says s-sparse directions with s/n → 0 cannot show fake structure; a sparse axis (Witten–Tibshirani L1) with s ≈ 10–20 neurons is a cheap robustness check — if the sparse axis shows similar separation to the dense one, the separation is unlikely to be a noise artifact.

### Gaps
- The Roy Chowdhury et al. (2015) paper's quantitative findings were not accessible (no abstract/PDF); it is cited by title.
- No source gives an effective-sample-size estimate for token embeddings across training checkpoints; this must be measured (e.g., autocorrelation of the projected statistics along the checkpoint axis).

## Prioritized recipe for our case (192-d residual stream, 20 tool vs 580 background tokens, 806 checkpoints)

### Takeaway
Use a regularized two-stage frame — axis 1 = shrinkage-LDA (or DWD) direction, axes 2–3 = PCA of the class-centered residual (LOL), optionally refined by PDA-index pursuit on the Grassmannian — fitted on a window of late checkpoints, validated with blocked-by-checkpoint CV and a refit-everything permutation null, and reported with PDC z-scores and a view-level DSC/kNN purity plus a lineup.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences (the recipe, in priority order)
1. Data layout. Standardize each of the 192 dimensions using the pooled within-class SD computed on the *fit* checkpoints only (Witten–Tibshirani weighting; PDA index also assumes standardized data). Choose a fit window of late checkpoints (e.g. the last 100–200) and a disjoint held-out block (e.g. interleaved blocks of 20 checkpoints), never random token splits.
2. Axis 1 (discriminant). w_1 = (Ŝ_W + λI)^{-1}Δ with Ŝ_W pooled over the fit window and λ from Ledoit–Wolf ('auto' in sklearn LDA, solver='eigen'); alternative that is designed for HDLSS and imbalance: weighted DWD. Cross-check with the plain mean-difference Δ (LOL's axis): if the whitened and unwhitened axes disagree strongly in held-out PDC, trust the one with the higher held-out PDC, not the one with better training separation.
3. Axes 2–3 (context). Default = LOL: project out w_1, take the top-2 eigenvectors of the class-centered covariance of the fit window, Gram–Schmidt to an orthonormal 3-frame. This shows where the whole frame's representation moves between checkpoints and is label-robust. Optional refinement = maximize I_PDA(A, λ_PDA) over 3-frames (tourr guided_tour(pda_pp) or a Grassmann projected-gradient from the LOL start), with λ_PDA by Lee–Cook's S(λ) = 1 rule evaluated on held-out checkpoints; accept the refined frame only if its held-out PDC / kNN purity improves. Never use pseudo-inverse LDA, the λ = 0 LDA index, or minimum-within-variance directions for axes 2–3 (data piling).
4. Regularization of any direct Stiefel objective (soft-silhouette, soft-kNN, NCA-in-3D): always replace Σ_W by Σ_W + λI, initialize from the LOL frame, early-stop on the held-out block, and prefer the LOL/PDA frame unless the gain is confirmed by the permutation test.
5. Measurable separation (held-out only). (a) Along axis 1: projected two-sample t and the PDC z-score; (b) in 3D: DSC (nearest-centroid consistency), silhouette, and 5-NN class purity of tool tokens; (c) Bayes-style summary: separation ratio ‖Δ_proj‖/√(tool within-SD² + background within-SD²).
6. Null calibration. For B ≥ 1,000 label permutations within each checkpoint (balanced permutations if PDC > ~5), refit steps 1–3 completely and recompute (5) on the held-out block; report empirical p and PDC. Add a 20-panel lineup of the 3D view (real vs permuted-label fits) for the thesis figure.
7. Stability across checkpoints. Fit the frame on sliding windows of W checkpoints; report principal angles between consecutive frames (and between each frame and the final one) and the held-out PDC as a function of checkpoint index. A "stable" 3D view = principal angles that plateau in the late window; if axes 2–3 keep rotating, show only axis 1 vs a fixed PCA background.
8. Sparse sanity check. Fit Witten–Tibshirani LDA-L1 (penalizedLDA R package) with ≈10–20 non-zero neurons; if the sparse axis reproduces the held-out separation, the dense axis is not a noise artifact (Bickel–Kur–Nadler Theorem 6 logic).
9. Avoid: PLS-DA components beyond the first, NCA/LMNN without early stopping, cPCA with tool tokens as target (rank-19 covariance), SIR/supervised-PCA for axes 2–3 (rank-1 with two classes), LOO over tokens as the validation scheme.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end benchmark of these choices on transformer token embeddings; the ordering above follows the theory and the HDLSS simulations cited, not a direct empirical comparison on our data type.
- The effective number of independent checkpoints (hence the correct block size for CV and the number of permutations needed) must be measured on our data.
