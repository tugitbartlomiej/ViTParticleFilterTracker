# Class-separation measures for 2D/3D views: definitions, biases, human agreement, use as search objectives, and validation

Context for these notes: 192-dimensional token states of a small ViT; 600 tokens per frame, ~18–20 "tool" tokens (≈3 %) vs ~580 "background"; 806 training checkpoints of the same frame. Goal: choose 3 axes that separate tool from background most clearly, report a number, and show it is not an artefact of fitting ~20 points in 192 dimensions.

Research tools used: Undermind paper search + two deep searches (workspace "ViT: detekcja końcówki narzędzia w chirurgii zaćmy"; the validation deep search completed, the perception deep search was still running when these notes were written: https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893?path=/Class%20separation%20measures%20in%202D%203D%20scatterplots%20and%20agreement%20with%20human%20perception), full texts of Sips et al. 2009, Tatu et al. 2009, Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015, Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016 (SepMe), Wei et al. (DiProPerm), Bae et al. CHI 2025, and Undermind full-text readers for Bickel–Kur–Nadler 2018, Shen et al. 2023, Pavlopoulos et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2016, Maindonald & Burden 2005, Bevilacqua & Bro 2020, Varma & Simon 2006, Vabalas et al. 2019. Scite was not used.

Notation used below: a view is a 2D (or 3D) projection of labelled points; `k` = number of points, `m` = number of classes; "target class" = the class whose separation from everything else is being judged (for us: tool tokens).

## Key Question 1 — Which quantitative measures of class separation in a 2D/3D view exist, and what are their formulas?

### Takeaway
The visualization literature has converged on "neighbourhood purity" measures (how pure the local neighbourhood of each point is in the view), of which Distance Consistency (DSC, nearest-centroid error) and the SepMe family (class proportion of same-class neighbours in a proximity graph, averaged over the *target* class) are the reference points; classical clustering indices (silhouette, Dunn, Calinski–Harabasz, Davies–Bouldin, Fisher/LDA ratio) and density/entropy measures (DC, HDM, CDM) are the other two families, and all three are routinely compared in the same frameworks.

### Cited Findings

**Distance Consistency (DSC) and Distribution Consistency (DC) — Sips, Neubert, Lewis & Hanrahan 2009, Computer Graphics Forum 28(3):831–838 (EuroVis 2009).**
- Centroid-distance property CD: for a point `x` with label `i`, `d(x, centr(c_i)) < d(x, centr(c_j))` for all `j ≠ i`; DSC is "the classification error of class members using CD": `DSC = |{x' ∈ v(X) : CD(x', centr'(c_label(x))) ≠ true}| / k`, where `x'` is the 2D projection of `x` and `centr'(c_i)` the 2D projection of the class centroid; the score is normalised to 0–100 (in the paper's convention a *high* DSC = good view, e.g. "DSC=90" vs "DSC=49" for the wine data), computed in O(k). The authors state DSC "can be used as a very efficient method … if the class structure C(X) describes convex classes" — [Sips et al. 2009, PDF](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/Pdfs/Sips.etal-2009-SGVoHDDUCC.pdf); DOI [10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01467.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01467.x).
- Distribution Consistency DC (entropy-based, for non-convex classes): per screen region, `H(x,y) = −Σ_c (p_c/Σp_c) log2(p_c/Σp_c)` (0 if region pure, `log2 m` if all classes mixed equally); `DC = 100 − (1/Z) Σ_{x,y} p(x,y) H(x,y)` with `p(x,y) = Σ_c p_c` (regions weighted by the amount of data) and `Z = 100 / (log2(m) Σ_{x,y} Σ_c p_c)`; `p_c` is computed under a kernel of width σ (they report rankings for σ = 0.5 % and 5 % of image width; interleaved "chessboard" patterns are only penalised at the larger σ). DSC and DC correlated at 0.70–0.86 on Wine/Iris for σ = 0.03–0.05 — [Sips et al. 2009](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/Pdfs/Sips.etal-2009-SGVoHDDUCC.pdf).
- Sips et al. explicitly note the centroid property is "always violated" if two class centres differ by only a small ε in the view, and that "with increasing number of dimensions or clusters it is harder to find views that are highly consistent" — [Sips et al. 2009](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/Pdfs/Sips.etal-2009-SGVoHDDUCC.pdf).

**Class Density Measure (CDM) and Histogram Density Measure (HDM) — Tatu, Albuquerque, Eisemann, Schneidewind, Theisel, Magnor & Keim 2009, IEEE VAST 2009, pp. 59–66, DOI [10.1109/VAST.2009.5332628](https://doi.org/10.1109/VAST.2009.5332628).**
- CDM: one density image per class, density at pixel p = `1/r` with `r` the radius to the k-th nearest same-class point (k = 4 "eliminates the influence of outliers"); `CDM = Σ_{k=1}^{M−1} Σ_{l=k+1}^{M} Σ_{i=1}^{P} | p_i^k − p_i^l |` (M classes, P pixels), range 0..P when pixel values are normalised to [0,1]; "large if the densities at each pixel differ as much as possible"; it rewards "not only … well separated but also dense clusters" — [Tatu et al. 2009, PDF](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/4eecf78e-858a-4bf7-9c29-f049d39b71fa/download).
- HDM: per histogram bin, `H(p) = −Σ_c (p_c/Σ_c p_c) log2(p_c/Σ_c p_c)`; `1D-HDM = 100 − (1/Z) Σ_x Σ_c p_c H(p)`, `Z = 100/(log2 M · Σ_x Σ_c p_c)`; axis rotations by θ = 9m° (m∈[0,20)) are tried and the best kept; 2D-HDM uses a 2D grid where each bin's counts are pooled with its 8 neighbours; range 0–100, 100 = every bin single-class — [Tatu et al. 2009](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/4eecf78e-858a-4bf7-9c29-f049d39b71fa/download).

**SepMe family — Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016, "SepMe: 2002 new visual separation measures", IEEE PacificVis 2016, pp. 1–8, DOI [10.1109/PACIFICVIS.2016.7465244](https://doi.org/10.1109/PACIFICVIS.2016.7465244).**
- Framework: (1) build a proximity graph on the unlabelled 2D points (17 base graphs incl. Delaunay, Gabriel, RNG, MST, SIG, ε-ball, kNN, KNCG, β-skeleton, α-shape, γ-observable-neighbour graph GONG; 143 parametrisations), (2) apply a class-purity function (12 neighbourhood-based: Class Proportion CP, Class Entropy CE, Majority Vote MV, Weighted Vote WV, each over All points (A) or Target class only (T), with optimistic/pessimistic tie-breaks; 2 component-based: LTCC, MCEC) → 2002 measures, all scaled 0–100 — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016, PDF](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).
- Class Proportion: `m_CP(S, G) = Σ_{x_i∈S} p_i^{c_i} / |S|`, `p_i^ν = |N_i^ν|/|N_i|` (same-class fraction of graph neighbours; set to 1 if `N_i = ∅`); S = all points or only the target class. Class Entropy: `m_CE = Σ n_i h_i / Σ n_i`, `h_i = −q_i^0 log2 q_i^0 − q_i^1 log2 q_i^1`, `n_i = |N_i|+1`. Majority Vote: `m_MV = Σ δ(c_i, z_i)/|S|` with `z_i` the neighbourhood majority label — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).
- GONG (γ-observable neighbour graph): `x_i → p` is an edge if the intermediate point `p_i = γp + (1−γ)x_i` has `p` as its nearest neighbour in `s \ x_i`; γ = 0 gives the NN graph, γ = 0.5 the Gabriel graph; tested γ ∈ {0.25,…,0.5} — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).
- MCEC (Mixed-Class-Edges-Cut) is itself a *label-permutation* statistic: count mixed-class edges `n_G` in the graph, compare to the distribution of that count over `m` random label permutations (class sizes preserved), `m_MCEC = |{i : n_{G_rand_i} > n_G}|/m` — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).
- Multi-class problems are decomposed into k one-vs-rest ("target class vs union of others") problems, justified by earlier evidence that separation "can be judged for each class separately" — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).

**The 15 "state-of-the-art" measures compared by Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015 (Table 1), Computer Graphics Forum 34(3):201–210, DOI [10.1111/cgf.12632](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12632):** ABTN (between-class average distance), AWTN (within-class average distance), ABW (ratio), WII (between/within weighted by class sizes), CAL (Calinski–Harabasz: between-class centroid squared distances over within-class point-to-centroid squared distances), LDA (same ratio after the optimal linear transform), DUNN (ratio of max within-class distance to min between-class distance), GAM (Baker–Hubert gamma: normalised count of within-class distances smaller/greater than between-class distances), SIL (silhouette), HM (hypothesis margin: average over points of distance-to-nearest-other-class minus distance-to-nearest-same-class), CS (same-class proportion of MST neighbours), DSC, CDM_K (K = 1..10), DC_σ (σ = 0.1 %–20 % of max inter-point distance), HDM_Nb (Nb = 5..80 cells) — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015, PDF](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).

**Neighbourhood hit (projection literature).** `NH(k) = (1/N) Σ_i |{j ∈ kNN_2D(i) : label_j = label_i}| / k`, in [0,1]; introduced with the LSP projection by Paulovich et al. 2008, IEEE TVCG 14(3):564–575, DOI [10.1109/TVCG.2007.70443](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2007.70443), and used as a standard projection-quality metric in the large benchmark of Espadoto et al. 2019, IEEE TVCG, DOI [10.1109/TVCG.2019.2944182](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2019.2944182); the explicit formula above is restated in [Kim et al. 2021, arXiv:2110.00317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.00317).

**Classical cluster-validity indices (established, textbook):** silhouette `s(i) = (b_i − a_i)/max(a_i,b_i)` with `a_i` mean intra-class distance and `b_i` smallest mean distance to another class (Rousseeuw 1987, J. Comput. Appl. Math. 20:53–65, DOI [10.1016/0377-0427(87)90125-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-0427(87)90125-7)); Dunn 1974 (J. Cybernetics 4(1):95–104, DOI [10.1080/01969727408546059](https://doi.org/10.1080/01969727408546059)); Calinski–Harabasz 1974 (Comm. Stat. 3(1):1–27, DOI [10.1080/03610927408827101](https://doi.org/10.1080/03610927408827101)); Davies–Bouldin 1979 (IEEE TPAMI 1(2):224–227, DOI [10.1109/TPAMI.1979.4766909](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.1979.4766909)). A broad comparative study of 30 indices is Arbelaitz et al. 2013, Pattern Recognition 46(1):243–256, DOI [10.1016/j.patcog.2012.07.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2012.07.021).

**Classifier-based measures.** Benato, Falcão & Telea 2023 (Computers & Graphics 115:287–297, DOI [10.1016/j.cag.2023.08.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2023.08.023)) measure visual separation in a projection by the Cohen's κ of a semi-supervised optimum-path-forest classifier propagating labels in the 2D view, and report it "consistently scores values in line with the perceived visual separation, surpassing existing projection-quality metrics" on 18 datasets × 39 projections — [abstract via Undermind](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cag.2023.08.023).

### Inferences
- All families reduce to one of: (a) nearest-centroid error (DSC; a 1-NN-centroid classifier), (b) local neighbour purity (SepMe CP/CE/MV, CS, NH, kNN accuracy), (c) global dispersion ratios (CAL/LDA/Fisher, Dunn, DB, silhouette), (d) density/entropy on a pixel grid (DC, HDM, CDM). Fisher's ratio and Bhattacharyya distance belong to (c) as parametric (Gaussian) versions; they were not in the vis-community comparisons except via CAL/LDA.
- For our 2-class, 3 % minority case, the only measures that are defined *per target class* by construction are SepMe-T variants, per-class silhouette, per-class NH, and MCEC; DSC, CAL, DUNN and overall NH pool both classes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found that lists a Bhattacharyya-distance or Fisher-ratio evaluation against human scatterplot judgments; the CAL/LDA entries of Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015 are the closest.
- Lehmann & Theisel's TVCG paper (see Q3) defines a projection dissimilarity but I could not access its quality-measure details.

## Key Question 2 — Which measures agree best with human judgment, and how are they biased by class imbalance, outliers and density? What normalisations are recommended?

### Takeaway
Human-ground-truth studies rank local neighbour-purity measures highest: DSC was the best of 15 classical measures (AUC 82.5 %), and the SepMe measure GONG 0.35 DIR CPT raised this to 92.9 %, with "average over the *target class only*" being the single feature that significantly improved agreement; existing measures nevertheless fail in roughly half of real-data cases, and the 2025 CHI study shows class count/imbalance, density, shape and non-Gaussianity all shift human judgments in ways current measures miss. Micro-averaged silhouette is dominated by the large class; macro (per-class) averaging is the recommended fix.

### Cited Findings

**Sips et al. 2009 user study.** 10 participants (Stanford graphics lab) each chose 5 good views per scatterplot matrix (4–30 dimensions); DSC "correlates to over 50 % with people's judgement of good views" (recall) and humans "have little preference when shown views [that] differ in consistency by about 5 % or less" — [Sips et al. 2009](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/Pdfs/Sips.etal-2009-SGVoHDDUCC.pdf).

**Tatu et al. 2010 (AVI 2010, DOI [10.1145/1842993.1843002](https://doi.org/10.1145/1842993.1843002))** found DSC (named "CCM" there) and HDM the best-matching measures in a user study; this is reported second-hand in Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015 — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).

**Sedlmair, Tatu, Munzner & Tory 2012, "A taxonomy of visual cluster separation factors", CGF 31(3):1335–1344, DOI [10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03125.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03125.x).** Two measures (DSC and DC, per Bae et al. 2025's summary) were tested on 75 real+synthetic datasets × 4 DR techniques × 3 encodings (816 plots inspected); "the measures failed in over half of the cases, and in about two-thirds of the cases involving real datasets"; the failure taxonomy has four ordered categories Scale → Point Distance → Shape → Position, each with within-cluster factors (density, curvature, isotropy, clumpiness, …) and between-cluster factors (count, variance of count, variance of density, split, outliers, inner/outer position, overlap, variance of shape) — abstract via [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/j.1467-8659.2012.03125.x); factor list from the authors' slides ([konstanz2012.pdf](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/talks/konstanz2012.pdf)), which also show a DSC false-negative example (score 29 for a visibly separated but "stringy"-shaped cluster) — [slides](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/talks/konstanz2012.pdf).

**Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015 (CGF 34(3):201–210, DOI [10.1111/cgf.12632](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12632)).**
- Ground truth: the 2012 corpus re-coded; two expert coders rated separability of every class on a 1–5 scale (Krippendorff's α = 0.858); after removing 3D/multi-D plots, occluded points, datasets with >14 classes or >1000 visible points, and ambiguous ratings, 828 one-vs-all 2D scatterplots remained (408 synthetic, 420 real, 56 datasets); each measure is scored by the bootstrapped (10 000 samples) area under the ROC curve for predicting the binary human "separable / not" label — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).
- Winner: DSC, AUC bootstrap average 82.5 %, "closely followed by HM, CAL, LDA, WII, SIL and GAM … all … around 80 %"; HDM_10 reached 76.4 %; "almost all of the separation measures have a higher AUC … for synthetic datasets than for real datasets, with differences up to 36.6 % … for DC_20% (53.1 % on real and 89.7 % on synthetic)"; on 30 "highly synthetic" sanity datasets 26/35 measure instances scored a perfect 100 %. Parametric measures (CDM, HDM, DC) were strongly parameter-dependent (best CDM K = 4, HDM Nb = 10, DC σ = 10 %), contradicting the insensitivity claims in the original papers — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).
- Their reading: the two best measures "both involve a non-parametric Nearest Neighbor approach and give a distinct role to between and within class distances" — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).

**Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016 (SepMe).** On 768 of those 828 plots: 1170/2002 (58.4 %) new measures beat DSC (AUC 83.2 % on this subset); best = GONG 0.35 DIR CPT at 92.9 % (+9.7 points, +11.7 %); a cheap alternative with similar performance is KNNG 2 DIR CPT ("average Class-Proportion of the 2-Nearest-Neighbors of each point in the Target class", O(K n log n) vs O(n² log n)). Feature analysis: RNG has the highest median AUC and lowest spread (a "conservative option"); RNG, KNCG, KNNG, GONG, CBSG significantly beat SIG and ε-ball graphs; CP-over-target-class (CPT) is significantly better than all purity functions except MV-T and CE-T; **"Averaging over the target class (T) is significantly better than over all classes (A)"**; directedness and tie-breaking rules make no significant difference; global statistics (MCEC) and graphs with possibly distant neighbours (DG, GG, MST, SIG, AS, EBG) "did not perform as well". Case study: for a fixed DSC of 64, GON ranged 40→79 on the Wisconsin cancer data, i.e. the two measures rank views differently — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://www.vis.uni-stuttgart.de/documentcenter/staff/sedlmaml/papers/aupetit2016sepme.pdf).

**Bae, Fujiwara, Tseng & Szafir 2025, "Uncovering how scatterplot features skew visual class separation", CHI 2025, DOI [10.1145/3706598.3713976](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713976).** 150 valid MTurk participants, 294 two-class scatterplots sampled from 6 947 scatterplots built from real-world datasets, pairwise "which is more separated" tasks (15 participants per pair, 20 s each); 70 scatterplot features. Using GONG 0.35 DIR CPT as proxy ground truth, participants' choices matched it only 67.6 % of the time (σ = 31.2, 95 % CI [65.1, 70.1]) — "a 32.4 % mismatch"; the GONG score difference within a pair correlated only weakly with the vote count (r = 0.36). Feature groups significantly related to perceived separation include non-Gaussianity (Clumpy r = 0.446, DistributionOverlap r = −0.41), classification-complexity overlap measures (C_LSC r = −0.503, C_F3 −0.481, C_F4 −0.465), **point Count and Variance of Count (N_points_max r = −0.430; Points/Classes r = −0.429 to −0.639; σ_N_points r = −0.429) — "humans perceive VCS differently if there is a class imbalance or a large number of points"**, Skinny (−0.635), Sparse (+0.515), Convex, C_Density. A linear-SVR composite of 26 features reaches r = 0.696 vs GONG alone r = 0.439 and DSC alone r = 0.465; adding C_F1v (Fisher-type per-axis overlap) to GONG raises r to 0.554. Features are computed after MST-based outlier removal (Wilkinson's procedure) because "features are highly influenced by outliers" — [Bae et al. 2025, PDF](https://ivis.itn.liu.se/pubs/docs/fujiwara-chi25a.pdf).

**Silhouette aggregation under imbalance — Pavlopoulos, Vardakas & Likas 2024, "Revisiting silhouette aggregation", DOI [10.1007/978-3-031-78977-9_23](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-78977-9_23) (arXiv [2401.05831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05831)).** Micro-averaging (mean over points) "gives larger clusters proportionally more influence"; synthetic example: four equal Gaussian clusters, micro = macro = 0.623; adding 5 000 points to one distant cluster (min/max size ratio 0.02) moves micro to 0.879 while macro stays ≈ 0.622; on real data (Covertype, ratio 0.03) macro 0.25 vs micro 0.08, Glass (0.12) macro 0.20 vs micro 0.31 — the micro score "follows the dominant cluster's quality". Recommendation: macro-average (mean of per-cluster mean silhouettes), with per-cluster sampling for large data — [Undermind full-text summary of Pav24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05831).

**External indices and imbalance — de Souto et al. 2012, DOI [10.1109/SBRN.2012.25](https://doi.org/10.1109/SBRN.2012.25):** on highly imbalanced data, F-measure, NMI, AMI, Rand, ARI and BCubed "are not able to deal properly with the problem of class size imbalance"; a class-size-normalised Rand index is proposed — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/SBRN.2012.25).

**Outliers and noise in internal indices — Guerra et al. 2012, Intelligent Data Analysis 16(4):703–715, DOI [10.3233/IDA-2012-0545](https://doi.org/10.3233/IDA-2012-0545):** standard indices compared on databases with outliers and noisy dimensions, with index rankings changing across scenarios — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.3233/IDA-2012-0545).

**Quality metrics are necessary but not sufficient — Machado, Behrisch & Telea 2025, CGF, DOI [10.1111/cgf.70101](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70101):** projections can be generated that "score similar or even higher PQM values than projections created by well-known techniques, but show different, often confusing, visual patterns"; "low values usually mean poor projections — the converse does not always hold"; not all metrics can be fooled equally — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70101).

**Human judgments vs clustering-quality measures (Lewis, Ackerman & de Sa 2012, CogSci):** "some highly natural CQMs are in sharp contrast with human evaluations while others correlate well"; clustering-evaluation skill is present in the general population — [abstract](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9058288b07de70f4f9275b5bdeacf82dfe7d2dd). Tatu et al. 2010, Lewis et al. 2012 and the 0.858 inter-coder α all "indicate little variance in class separability judgments among humans" — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://sepme.cs.univie.ac.at/SRC/sedlmair2015ddeval.pdf).

### Inferences
- For a 3 % minority class, any measure averaged over *all* points (DSC, overall NH, micro-silhouette, overall kNN accuracy, MCEC) is dominated by the ~580 background tokens: a view in which the tool tokens sit inside the background cloud but the background is self-consistent can still score ≥ 0.95. The SepMe evidence that target-class averaging is *significantly* closer to human judgment plus the Pavlopoulos silhouette result justify reporting every measure **per class (tool-class value and background-class value) and never the pooled value**.
- DSC is a nearest-centroid rule, so it is blind to the minority class's spread: with 20 tool tokens far from their own centroid but still nearer to it than to the background centroid, DSC = 100 even though the tool cloud may visually overlap the tail of the background. Local-neighbourhood measures (GONG/kNN CPT, per-class NH) catch exactly this.
- Outlier handling: CDM's k-th-nearest-neighbour density (k = 4) and Bae et al.'s MST outlier pruning are the two explicit outlier-robustness devices in this literature; for 20 points, pruning is not advisable (each point is 5 % of the class), so prefer measures that degrade gracefully (CP with k = 2–5, silhouette) over Dunn (min/max distances, driven by a single outlier).
- Normalisation: all vis measures are reported in 0–100 or [0,1]; the comparative studies additionally rely on ROC/AUC against human labels, so "which measure is best" is a statement about *ranking* views, not about absolute values.

### Gaps
- No study was found that varies the minority-class fraction systematically (e.g. 1–10 %) and measures bias of DSC/GONG/NH; Bae et al. 2025 show count/variance-of-count correlates with human judgments but do not isolate imbalance.
- No published 3D-scatterplot ground truth for these measures: Sedlmair & Aupetit explicitly excluded 3D plots; Tian et al. 2021 (DOI [10.3390/info12060239](https://doi.org/10.3390/info12060239)) compare 2D vs 3D projections with quality metrics but not with separation-specific human judgments.

## Key Question 3 — How are separation measures used as objectives to rank or search views (scagnostics, projection pursuit, best-view selection, optimal projection sets)?

### Takeaway
The standard pattern is "generate many candidate views → score each with a separation measure → show the top-k (or a diverse top-k)"; axis-aligned SPLOM ranking (Sips; Tatu), ranking rotated/PCA views (Tatu HDM), VizRank-style classifier accuracy ranking, and dissimilarity-aware selection of minimal projection sets (Lehmann & Theisel; Grassmannian-based tools) are the main instances; projection pursuit with a supervised index is the continuous-optimisation version of the same idea.

### Cited Findings
- Sips et al. rank all `n(n−1)/2` SPLOM views by DSC/DC, let users threshold (e.g. DSC ≥ 80 fades out "nearly 97 % of the scatterplots" of the 159-D WHO data, >12 000 views), and explicitly leave "class consistency … as a measure of goodness in projection pursuit to find general projections" as future work; they contrast this with cluster-preserving transforms (Koren & Carmel 2004; Dhillon, Modha & Spangler 1998) that generate a *single* optimised view — [Sips et al. 2009](https://www.scribblethink.org/Work/Pdfs/Sips.etal-2009-SGVoHDDUCC.pdf).
- Tatu et al. rank axis-aligned scatterplots by CDM, and for HDM first rank 1D projections (with rotations θ = 9m°), pick the best-separating dimensions, run PCA on every subset and rank the resulting PC1–PC2 views by 2D-HDM; limitation noted: "it is not always possible to find good separating views, due to a growing number of classes" — [Tatu et al. 2009](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/4eecf78e-858a-4bf7-9c29-f049d39b71fa/download).
- VizRank (Leban et al. 2005, Bioinformatics 21(3):413–414, DOI [10.1093/bioinformatics/bti016](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/bti016)) ranks projections of labelled data by a machine-learning (classifier accuracy) score — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/bti016).
- Lehmann & Theisel 2016, "Optimal sets of projections of high-dimensional data", IEEE TVCG 22(1):609–618, DOI [10.1109/TVCG.2015.2467132](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2015.2467132): a data-driven method to find minimal sets of projections that "uniquely show certain data patterns", using a dissimilarity measure between projections that is invariant to affine transformations of the view so that the same pattern is not shown twice; yields complete "data tours" of about n/2 projections and optimal paths through the space of projection matrices — abstract via [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TVCG.2015.2467132).
- Liu et al. 2015 (CGF 34(3):271–280, DOI [10.1111/cgf.12639](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12639)) estimate linear subspaces by clustering, use their bases as 2D viewpoints and connect them with animated "dynamic projections" via a view-transition graph — [OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/cgf.12639). The Grassmannian Atlas (Liu et al. 2016, CGF, DOI [10.1111/cgf.12876](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12876)) samples the space of 2D linear projections, scores each with a quality measure, and organises them by the Grassmannian geodesic distance between subspaces (formula in Q5) — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12876).
- Projection-pursuit significance: Sun 1991, Biometrika 78(4):759–769, DOI [10.1093/biomet/78.4.759](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/78.4.759), derives an analytic approximation to the P-value of Friedman's projection-pursuit index under a null hypothesis, because "it is useful to have a significance test to help us decide whether apparent structure is real or just the effect of noise" and Monte Carlo is expensive — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/78.4.759).
- Liu & Gillies 2016, Pattern Recognition 53:73–86, DOI [10.1016/j.patcog.2015.11.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2015.11.015): "the conventional wisdom of improving classification performance by maximising inter-class discrimination is not valid for high-dimensional datasets, and can lead to severe overfitting … we prove the theoretical existence of perfectly discriminative subspace projections" — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patcog.2015.11.015).
- Machado et al. 2025 show that optimising a projection against a quality metric can produce high-scoring but misleading views, i.e. the metric is exploitable as an objective — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.70101).

### Inferences
- Searching 3 axes among 192 by a separation score is a selection among a very large family (C(192,3) ≈ 1.15 million axis-aligned triples, or a continuum for LDA/projection-pursuit directions); the maximum of any score over such a family is upward-biased even under the null, which is the "multiple comparisons / max statistic" issue addressed in Q4.
- The vis literature treats the measure as a *ranking* device checked by a human, not as a reported effect size; in our case the number must survive a null model (Q4), precisely because the same measure is also the search objective.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the body of Lehmann & Theisel 2016 (which quality measure they optimise and the exact dissimilarity formula); the Thiagarajan, Ramamurthy & Bremer 2016 "optimal set of axis-aligned projections" report has no accessible abstract.
- No vis paper found that combines view ranking with a built-in null model beyond SepMe's MCEC (which is itself a permutation statistic).

## Key Question 4 — How should a claimed separation be validated: label-permutation null models, cross-validation of supervised axes, the double-dipping problem, and small-sample/high-dimension effects?

### Takeaway
The established answer is: (i) never report the in-sample separation of axes chosen on the same points (circular analysis); (ii) obtain the null by permuting class labels **and re-running the whole axis-selection procedure inside each permutation** (DiProPerm); (iii) estimate the out-of-sample separation by fitting axes on training points and projecting held-out points (cross-validated score plots / looCV-bgPCA), nesting any model selection; (iv) expect spurious separation when the minority-class size is far below the dimension — pure Gaussian noise admits a projection separating any chosen subset when p ≥ n, and bgPCA/LDA/PLS-DA on noise routinely show "huge" group separation.

### Cited Findings

**Double dipping.** Kriegeskorte, Simmons, Bellgowan & Baker 2009, Nature Neuroscience 12:535–540, DOI [10.1038/nn.2303](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2303): circular analysis = using the same data for selection and for the selective analysis; of 134 fMRI papers in 5 journals in 2008, 42 % contained at least one non-independent selective analysis and 14 % more could not be judged; simulations with no true effect: pattern-classifier accuracy "significantly above chance" on Gaussian noise when voxels were selected on the same data; ROI test significant in 20/100 simulations at p < 0.05 and 9/100 at p < 0.01 with a true effect of zero; remedies: select on independent data, split data (e.g. odd/even runs), use selection statistics independent of the test statistic by construction, and prefer a non-selective whole-volume analysis with multiple-comparison correction — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2841687/).

**Spurious separation in high dimension / small samples.**
- Bickel, Kur & Nadler 2018, PNAS 115(37):9151–9156, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1801177115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1801177115): for `x_1..x_n ~ N(0, I_p)`, if p/n → ∞ then for any target cdf G there are unit projections z with `||Ĝ_{z,n} − G||_∞ → 0` (Theorem 1; proof solves the linear system `Xz = a`); for p/n → γ > 1, any G with second moment < γ − 1 is attainable and any G with second moment > (1+√γ)² is not, and every distribution is attainable *up to scale and shift* (Corollary 1); for 0 < γ < 1 there is a universal C such that no projection can be farther than `C·sqrt(γ log(1/γ))` from the Gaussian cdf in sup norm (Theorem 4); at γ = 0 all projections are Gaussian. Figures show a bimodal projection (mixture of N(±2, 1/4)) constructed from n = 100, p = 1000 noise and apparent two-cluster 2D structure at p = 5000, n = 400 — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1801177115).
- Cover 1965, IEEE Trans. Electron. Comput. EC-14:326–334, DOI [10.1109/PGEC.1965.264137](https://doi.org/10.1109/PGEC.1965.264137): a family of separating surfaces with d degrees of freedom "has a natural separating capacity of 2d pattern vectors", and "the probability of ambiguous generalization is large unless the number of training patterns exceeds the capacity" — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/PGEC.1965.264137).
- Ahn & Marron 2010, Biometrika 97(1):254–259, DOI [10.1093/biomet/asp084](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asp084): in HDLSS settings there exist infinitely many directions on which the projected data "take on only two distinct values, one for each class" (data piling); the maximal-data-piling direction has the longest gap — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asp084).
- Wei, Lee, Wichers, Li & Marron, JCGS 25(2):549–569 (2016), DOI [10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773): two samples of 50 from N(0, I_1000) are "very well separated" on the DWD direction trained on the labels, "despite the fact that the samples arise from the same underlying distribution" — [arXiv 1304.0796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0796).
- Maindonald & Burden 2005, ANZIAM J. 46(E):C59–C74, DOI [10.21914/anziamj.v46i0.947](https://doi.org/10.21914/anziamj.v46i0.947): random normal data with 4 190 features and 24 samples in 4 groups (sizes 6, 11, 1, 6); selecting the 9 features with the largest F-statistics and plotting discriminant scores "shows striking apparent separation"; resubstitution accuracy 96 % vs ≈ 0.5 under proper cross-validation; remedy: repeat feature selection and discriminant fitting inside every CV fold, align fold coordinate systems, and plot only held-out test scores — the random-data plot then "shows no discrimination" — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.21914/anziamj.v46i0.947).
- Bookstein 2019, Evolutionary Biology 46:271–302, DOI [10.1007/s11692-019-09484-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-019-09484-8): on the patternless model (p i.i.d. Gaussians, equal group sizes) bgPCA and its equivalent PLS-against-group "necessarily generate the appearance of huge equilateral group separations that are fictitious"; **"when specimen counts by group vary greatly or when any group includes fewer than about ten specimens … the smaller the group, the more likely a bgPCA is to fictitiously identify that group as the end-member of one of its derived axes"** — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-019-09484-8). Cardini, O'Higgins & Rohlf 2019, Evol. Biol. 46:303–316, DOI [10.1007/s11692-019-09487-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-019-09487-5): bgPCA captures the g−1 between-mean dimensions but only a fraction of the Σn_i − g within-group dimensions when p > g−1, so within-group spread is under-represented; "most obvious when sample sizes are small relative to the number of variables … but the distortion is present even for large samples"; strong covariance among variables reduces it — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-019-09487-5).
- Cardini & Polly 2020, Evol. Biol. 47:85–95, DOI [10.1007/s11692-020-09494-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-020-09494-x): leave-one-out cross-validated bgPCA scores (each specimen projected onto axes computed without it) "reduce the distortion of group means but do not remove it entirely"; cross-validation "largely fixes spurious separation", agrees largely with distance-based permutation tests, and scatterplots "must therefore be interpreted with great caution"; implemented as `RRPP::looCV` (documented as "a diagnostic tool rather than a data transformation") and `ade4::loocv` (which also reports a "spuriousness index") — [search summary](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/RRPP/versions/2.0.3/topics/looCV).
- Bevilacqua & Bro 2020, Metabolites 10:278, DOI [10.3390/metabo10070278](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo10070278): simulated 360 variables × 40 calibration samples; fitted PLS-DA score plot separates classes while cross-validated and test score plots overlap (R² ≈ 0.90, CV Q² ≈ 0.26, test Q² ≈ 0.23); recommendation: plot cross-validated scores (held-out samples projected onto rotated-aligned loadings), "calibrated score plots should generally not be used unless R² and Q² are comparable" — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo10070278).

**Permutation tests with re-fitting (DiProPerm).** Wei et al.: Direction (normal of a linear classifier: mean difference MD, Fisher LD, SVM, DWD, maximal data piling) → Projection (univariate two-sample statistic on the projected values: mean difference, t, scaled MD, median difference, AUC) → Permutation: "(a) pool the two samples and permute the class labels; (b) take the normal vector to the binary linear classifier **retrained on the permuted class labels**; (c) project data onto this direction and re-calculate the univariate two-sample statistic"; reject at level α if the original statistic is among the 100α % largest. Any DiProPerm is an exact level-α test of equality of *distributions* (exchangeability); for equality of *means* the MD-MD version is exact under normality only with balanced sizes (Theorem 1) and the MD-t version is invalid under unequal covariances because the denominator scales as d in the permutation world vs √d in the original (Theorem 2); for unbalanced sizes an "MD-scaled MD" statistic `T/{s_x²/m + s_y²/n}^{1/2}` is asymptotically valid; the FLD-direction + mean-difference statistic equals the Hotelling T² up to a constant; PCA directions are excluded because the statistic would be invariant to relabelling; report empirical p, Gaussian-fit p and z-score. Real example: a visually "non-overlapping" DWD projection (UNCGEO, Luminal A vs B) had empirical p = 0.31, while a visibly overlapping one (UNCUP) had p ≈ 0 — [arXiv 1304.0796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0796).

**Permutation tests for classifiers / CV.** Golland, Liang, Mukherjee & Panchenko 2005, COLT, DOI [10.1007/11503415_34](https://doi.org/10.1007/11503415_34) (permutation test for classification accuracy; concentration governed by a Rademacher average) — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/11503415_34). Ojala & Garriga 2010 (ICDM 2009 / JMLR) "Permutation tests for studying classifier performance" is the standard reference for label-permutation vs feature-permutation nulls (surfaced by the deep search; not read). Hsing, Attoor & Dougherty 2003, Machine Learning 52:11–30, DOI [10.1023/A:1023985022691](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023985022691), relate permutation p-values to classifier error estimates — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023985022691). Jamalabadi et al. 2018, PLoS Comput. Biol., DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486): when classes contain nested subclasses (e.g. multiple data points from the same subject), classification rates are systematically inflated; "parametric statistical tests as well as typical trial-wise permutation fail", and the fix is a permutation that respects the subclass structure — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486).

**Cross-validation and nesting.**
- Varma & Simon 2006, BMC Bioinformatics 7:91, DOI [10.1186/1471-2105-7-91](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91): on null data (no class difference) non-nested CV used for both tuning and error reporting gave mean CV error 37.8 % (shrunken centroids) and 41.7 % (SVM) vs true 50 %; nested CV gave 54.2 % vs 50.0 % — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91).
- Vabalas, Gowen, Poliakoff & Casson 2019, PLoS ONE, DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0224365](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224365): on balanced Gaussian-noise data, ordinary K-fold CV reports ≈ 95–100 % accuracy at N ≈ 20 and ≈ 75–85 % at N ≈ 50 (visual reading of Fig. 3) when feature selection is done on all data; nested CV and train/test split were not significantly different from 50 % at 96.5 % of sample sizes; nesting feature selection mattered more than nesting hyper-parameter tuning — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224365).
- Ambroise & McLachlan 2002, PNAS 99(10):6562–6566, DOI [10.1073/pnas.102102699](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.102102699) (selection bias when gene selection is outside CV) and Cawley & Talbot 2010, JMLR 11:2079–2107 (over-fitting in model selection and selection bias) are the standard references surfaced by the deep search — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.102102699).
- Varoquaux et al. 2017, NeuroImage 145:166–179, DOI [10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.10.038](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.10.038) (CV caveats for decoders; small-sample CV estimates are highly variable) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.10.038).

**Significance of clusters seen in an embedding.** Shen, Bhamidi & Liu 2023, JCGS 32(1):219–230, DOI [10.1080/10618600.2023.2219708](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2023.2219708): MDS-SigClust computes the 2-means cluster index `CI_2` (within-cluster SS / total SS, minimised over MDS coordinates and an LDA direction) in the embedding and calibrates it against `N(0, Σ_Y)` samples generated *in the embedding space* with the estimated covariance; they warn that a t-test between two clusters forced from one Gaussian is significant, so a cluster index must be calibrated against a single-cluster null rather than testing group differences — [Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2023.2219708). Selective inference for testing after clustering: Chen & Gao 2023/2024, Biostatistics, [arXiv 2311.16375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16375).

### Inferences
- Our regime: total n = 600 > p = 192, so a pooled within-class covariance is estimable; but the *minority* mean is estimated from n₁ ≈ 20 points in 192 dimensions, so any supervised direction (LDA `w = S_w^{-1}(μ̂₁ − μ̂₀)`, mean-difference, PLS-DA, bgPCA, or "best of 1.15 M axis triples") will partially fit the noise in `μ̂₁`. Bookstein's "fewer than about ten specimens" warning for bgPCA is the closest published threshold; we are just above it, not safely beyond it.
- The published remedies map directly: (a) DiProPerm-style null with the axis search re-run inside each label permutation (preserving the 20/580 split); (b) if the reported number is the *maximum* over candidate views, the null must also be the maximum over the same search (max-statistic); (c) looCV / cross-validated score plots: fit axes without token *i*, project token *i*, report measures on these out-of-sample coordinates (Maindonald; Cardini & Polly; Bevilacqua & Bro); (d) a cluster-index null in the view itself (Shen et al.) is weaker than (a)–(c) because it does not re-run the projection.
- Exchangeability caveat (DiProPerm, Jamalabadi): tokens within one frame are not i.i.d. (spatial correlation; the "tool" tokens are spatially contiguous) and the 806 checkpoints are serially correlated; label permutations should be *within a checkpoint* (so the 806 checkpoints are 806 correlated replications, not 806 independent tests) and spatially structured nulls (e.g. permute the *location* of a contiguous 20-token blob rather than scatter 20 random labels) are a stricter and more honest null for "tool vs background" than i.i.d. label shuffling.

### Gaps
- No source gives a closed-form bias of DSC/GONG/silhouette for a 20-vs-580 split in 192 dimensions; the magnitude must be obtained by simulation (the permutation null does exactly this).
- Ojala & Garriga 2010 and Cawley & Talbot 2010 were identified only by the deep search summary and not read.

## Key Question 5 — Separation that persists over a trajectory (consecutive checkpoints) and stability of a chosen view across time

### Takeaway
The only widely used, well-defined tool is the set of principal angles between the subspaces spanned by the axes chosen at different times (Grassmann geodesic distance); dynamic-projection systems in visualization use exactly this geometry to animate between views; for the statistical question "does the separation persist?", the validation literature's advice is that correlated snapshots are not independent replications and the dependence must be preserved in the null.

### Cited Findings
- Principal angles / Grassmann distance (as used by the Grassmannian Atlas): for orthonormal bases `A, B ∈ R^{n×r}`, compute the SVD `AᵀB = UΣVᵀ`; principal angles `θ_i = arccos σ_i`; geodesic distance `d_Gr(A,B) = (Σ_{i=1}^{r} θ_i²)^{1/2}` (r = 2 for 2D views; r = 3 for us); it compares subspaces, "rather than the particular bases used to represent them", and is used to build a kNN graph over sampled projections — Liu et al. 2016, CGF, DOI [10.1111/cgf.12876](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12876) ([Undermind full-text summary](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12876)). The numerically careful treatment of angles between subspaces is Zhu & Knyazev 2013, J. Numer. Math. 21(4):325–340, DOI [10.1515/jnum-2013-0013](https://doi.org/10.1515/jnum-2013-0013) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jnum-2013-0013); the classical algorithm is Björck & Golub 1973, Math. Comp. 27:579–594, DOI [10.1090/S0025-5718-1973-0348991-3](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1973-0348991-3).
- Dynamic projections: Liu et al. 2015 (DOI [10.1111/cgf.12639](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12639)) connect 2D linear views with "smooth animated transitions between pairs of projections" and a view-transition graph — [OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/cgf.12639). Lehmann & Theisel 2016 provide "optimal paths through projection matrices for interactive exploration" — [OpenAlex abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/TVCG.2015.2467132).
- Bootstrap stability of PCA subspaces: Daudin, Duby & Trecourt 1988, Statistics 19(2):241–258, DOI [10.1080/02331888808802095](https://doi.org/10.1080/02331888808802095) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02331888808802095); bootstrap confidence sets for spectral projectors: Naumov, Spokoiny & Ulyanov 2019, Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 174:1091–1132, DOI [10.1007/s00440-018-0877-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-018-0877-2) (surfaced by the deep search; not read).
- The Undermind validation deep-search summary (synthesising 134 abstracts) states: "Persistent separation across adjacent snapshots is not independent replication when snapshots are correlated; preserve temporal dependence in validation and null construction" and recommends "bootstrap independent units and compare selected subspaces using principal angles, rather than signed axes" — [deep search](https://app.undermind.ai/projects/40294fa6-c9a3-4469-9a61-d3eddeaee893?path=/Validating%20class%20separation%20in%20supervised%20projections%20null%20models%20cross%20validation%20double%20dipping) (synthesis, not a primary source).
- Jamalabadi et al. 2018 (DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486)) is the primary source for the inflation caused by nested/correlated subclasses and for subclass-aware permutations — [abstract](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486).

### Inferences
- For 806 checkpoints: compute the 3-axis subspace `A_t` at each checkpoint (or on a sliding window), report `θ_max(A_t, A_{t+Δ})` and `d_Gr` as a function of Δ, and compare against the same quantity computed between two *label-permuted* fits — that gives a null for "the chosen view is stable because of the tool signal, not because of the background covariance".
- Temporal hold-out is a natural cross-validation: fit axes on checkpoint t (or on checkpoints ≤ t), evaluate the separation measure on checkpoint t+Δ; because the frame is fixed and the states drift with training, this tests whether the view generalises along the trajectory rather than fitting one snapshot's noise.
- Treat the 806 checkpoints as one correlated trajectory: a single p-value per checkpoint is fine as a *profile*, but any "fraction of checkpoints significant" statement must acknowledge the serial correlation (e.g. use a block bootstrap over time or report effective sample size).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found that defines a specific test for "class separation persisting along a trajectory of snapshots"; the recommendations above are inferences from the principal-angle and subclass-permutation literature.
- Could not retrieve Lehmann & Theisel's projection-dissimilarity formula to compare with the Grassmann distance.

## Key Question 6 — Concrete recommendation for the 192-D token case (20 tool vs 580 background, 806 checkpoints)

### Takeaway
Report three per-class (tool-class) separation numbers computed on **out-of-sample** coordinates, with a **label-permutation null that re-runs the full axis search**, and show stability of the chosen 3-D subspace across checkpoints via principal angles.

### Cited Findings
(The recommendation rests on the findings in Q1–Q5; key anchors restated.)
- Target-class neighbourhood purity is the best human-aligned measure and target-class averaging is significantly better than all-class averaging — [Aupetit & Sedlmair 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/PACIFICVIS.2016.7465244); KNNG-2 CPT is its cheap proxy — same source.
- DSC is the best classical measure (AUC 82.5 %) but a centroid rule — [Sedlmair & Aupetit 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12632); [Sips et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01467.x).
- Macro/per-class silhouette is the imbalance-robust aggregation — [Pavlopoulos et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05831).
- Null must re-fit the direction on permuted labels — [Wei et al. (DiProPerm)](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2015.1027773); selection must be inside the validation loop — [Kriegeskorte et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2303), [Varma & Simon 2006](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91), [Vabalas et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224365); plot only held-out scores — [Maindonald & Burden 2005](https://doi.org/10.21914/anziamj.v46i0.947), [Cardini & Polly 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-020-09494-x), [Bevilacqua & Bro 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo10070278); groups < ~10 and p ≫ n produce fictitious end-member groups — [Bookstein 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11692-019-09484-8); noise admits any projection when p ≥ n — [Bickel, Kur & Nadler 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1801177115).
- Principal angles for subspace comparison — [Liu et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12876); correlated subclasses need structured permutations — [Jamalabadi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006486).

### Inferences (the recommendation)
1. **Measures to report (all for the tool class, in the chosen 3-D view, on held-out coordinates):**
   - **GONG 0.35 DIR CPT** (or **KNNG-2 CPT** as the fast proxy): mean fraction of same-class (tool) neighbours among the γ-observable / 2 nearest neighbours of each tool token. Range 0–1; the human-aligned headline number. For 3D, the GONG/kNN graph is defined in any metric space, so the 2D definition carries over unchanged (not validated against humans in 3D — state this).
   - **DSC** reported as *per-class* nearest-centroid accuracy: fraction of tool tokens nearer the tool centroid than the background centroid, and the same for background; the pooled DSC must not be used (580/600 = 0.967 floor).
   - **Per-class silhouette** (mean `s(i)` over the 20 tool tokens, and separately over background), plus the held-out **AUC of the 1-D LDA/Fisher score** (the DiProPerm statistic that is insensitive to the 20/580 imbalance and to unequal variances; avoid the plain t-statistic per Wei et al. Theorem 2).
2. **Null model:** label permutation *within each checkpoint* preserving the 20/580 counts, with the **entire pipeline** (standardisation, axis search over all candidate triples or LDA/projection-pursuit optimisation, and the final measure) re-run on each permuted labelling; use ≥ 1000 permutations; report the empirical p and z-score of each measure against this null. Because the reported view is the best of many candidates, the null statistic is the **maximum over the same candidate set** (max-statistic correction). As a stricter secondary null, permute the *spatial position* of a contiguous 20-token blob (same shape as the real tool mask) so that spatial autocorrelation among tokens is preserved under H₀.
3. **Cross-validation:** leave-one-tool-token-out combined with K-fold on background (or stratified 5-fold repeated), fitting the axes on the training fold only and projecting the held-out tokens; compute all three measures on the pooled held-out coordinates after aligning fold subspaces (Procrustes/rotation as in Maindonald and Bevilacqua & Bro), i.e. a cross-validated score plot. Any choice among candidate axis sets or hyper-parameters (γ, k) goes inside the outer loop (nested CV). Report the in-sample vs held-out gap explicitly; a large gap is the signature of fitting 20 points in 192 dimensions.
4. **Time:** (a) temporal hold-out — fit axes at checkpoint t, evaluate at t+Δ for several Δ; (b) principal angles `θ_1..θ_3` and `d_Gr` between the 3-D subspaces chosen at consecutive checkpoints, compared with the same angles between permutation-null fits; (c) when summarising over the 806 checkpoints, treat them as a serially correlated trajectory (block bootstrap over time), not as 806 independent tests.
5. **Reporting:** give the three measures as "in-sample / held-out / null 95-th percentile" triples at a few representative checkpoints, and the full held-out profile over training; state that human-alignment evidence for all measures is for 2D scatterplots and that 3D is an extrapolation.
