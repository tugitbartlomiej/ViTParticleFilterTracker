## Q1. Which physical systems are the closest analogues, and what quantitative signatures does each predict?

### Takeaway
No single physical system fits all six observations. The closest *quantitative* description is not a glass. It is a set of distinguishable tracer particles, each tied to its own input, sitting in noise-driven cages of a non-equilibrium steady state at fixed "temperature" (constant AdamW learning rate). Two things are superimposed on that picture: a species-selective, label-driven field that separates tool from background (neural-collapse-like), and a slow, noise-driven simplification of the structure (dimensional collapse). "Colloidal glass with phase separation" is a legitimate *hypothesis*, but it is not yet supported. The measurements that would show it (non-Gaussian displacements, cage-escape anticorrelation that decays, dynamic heterogeneity, waiting-time dependence) have not been computed. One published DNN study using Langevin training explicitly found **no caging** ([Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) [AB]).

### Cited Findings
**Colloidal glass / supercooled liquid signatures**
- In the 80:20 binary Lennard-Jones glass former, the MSD has three regimes: ballistic (slope 2), a cage plateau (at the lowest T from t ~ 1 to ~10³), and diffusive (slope 1). The binary mixture was chosen to suppress crystallization. The minority small B particles are more mobile than the A particles, and only B shows a late secondary van Hove peak near r = 1 (jumps) — [Kob95](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.51.4626) [FT].
- Kob–Andersen define the non-Gaussian parameter α2(t) = 3⟨r⁴⟩/(5⟨r²⟩²) − 1. Its maximum grows on cooling and sits at the end of the β (plateau) regime; the paper gives no numerical peak values. Mode-coupling fits give T_c = 0.435 and D ∝ (T − T_c)^γ with γ = 2.0 (A) and 1.7 (B) — [Kob95](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.51.4626) [FT].
- Self van Hove: G_s(r,t) = ⟨(1/N) Σ_i δ(r − [r_i(t) − r_i(0)])⟩. Supercooled liquids show fat, approximately exponential tails G_s ~ exp[−r/λ(t)] instead of the Gaussian. Single trajectories are long vibrations in a cage interrupted by rapid jumps — [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT].
- χ4(t) = N[⟨C(t)²⟩ − ⟨C(t)⟩²] peaks near τ_α, and its peak grows and shifts later on cooling — [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT]. The dynamic correlation length ξ4 extracted from S4(q,t) grows from < 1 to > 9 particle diameters approaching the mode-coupling temperature — [Lac03](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1605094) [AB].
- Aging: after a quench, C(t, t_w) ≈ C_eq(t − t_w) + C_aging(t/t_w). In many polymeric glass formers τ_α(t_w) ~ t_w^μ with μ ∈ [1/2, 1] (μ < 1 is "subaging"). An effective temperature is defined by T_eff = T/X(t, t_w) from the FDT violation; aging silica gives X ≈ 0.51 — [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT].
- A fixed temperature alone does not imply stationarity: an aged glass held at constant T keeps changing with t_w, while an equilibrated supercooled liquid at fixed T is stationary even if slow — [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT].
- In confocal studies of PMMA colloids, the fastest particles move cooperatively in clusters. Cluster size grows approaching the glass transition, then drops at it; clusters are largest near the α-relaxation time — [Wee00](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.287.5453.627) [AB].
- Cage anticorrelation, as measured by Weeks & Weitz: a displacement Δr01 tends to be followed by an opposite Δr12. ⟨x12⟩ is linear in r01 for small r01, with slope −c (example c = 0.26), and breaks from linearity at r_cage. In the middle of the MSD plateau c ≈ 0.5, and it decreases at larger Δt as cages break. The local MSD exponent γ(Δt) can be estimated from c(Δt) and tracks the measured slope. r_cage is relatively insensitive to Δt (a static property) and shrinks toward φ_g ≈ 0.58. Only 5–10% of particles move during a cage rearrangement — [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5) [FT].
- Near the colloidal glass transition, α2 is ≈ 0 at short and long lags and peaks at Δt*, which defines the cage-rearrangement time and coincides with the end of the MSD plateau — [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5) [FT].
- Cage sizes shrink and cage lifetimes grow approaching the glass transition. Rearrangements are more common in lower-density, more disordered regions — [WW02prl](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.89.095704) [AB].
- Aging of a shear-melted colloidal glass (φ ≈ 0.62): the MSD plateau extends to longer lags with age. At fixed lag, ⟨Δx²⟩ ∝ t_w^(−a) with a = 0.23, 0.29, 0.31 for lags of 5, 10 and 20 min, and a = 0.05–0.5 across samples. Aging proceeds intermittently ("aging events"). The 10% most mobile particles are spatially clustered at all ages, and cluster size does not grow strongly with age — [Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349) [FT].
- Bond-breaking correlation: a bond exists if r_ij(t0) ≤ A1·σ and breaks if r_ij(t0 + Δt) > A2·σ (A1 = 1.1, A2 = 1.6 in 2-D; A1 = A2 = 1.5 in 3-D). τ_b is the 1/e time of surviving bonds, with τ_α ≈ 0.1·τ_b, and the decay is stretched (exponent ≈ 0.6) at low T. Broken bonds cluster, giving a correlation length ξ from the Ornstein–Zernike fit S_b(q) = S_b(0)/(1 + ξ²q²) — [Yam98b](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.3515) [FT].

**Field-driven binary mixtures (lane formation)**
- When half of the particles in a Brownian binary mixture are pushed along a field and half against it, there is a nonequilibrium transition to lanes of like particles parallel to the field. Lanes speed up transport along the field and strongly suppress it perpendicular to the field. Oscillating fields above a critical frequency restore disorder — [Dzu02](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.021402) [AB].
- In experiments on oppositely charged PMMA colloids, the lane fraction rises continuously with field strength and Brownian dynamics reproduces it. The mechanism is enhanced *lateral* mobility from collisions with counter-moving particles, which drops sharply once lanes form ("locked-in" state) — [Vis11](https://doi.org/10.1039/C0SM01343A) [AB].

**Active matter**
- In dense active (AOUP) systems, glassy slowdown, cage-like localization and heterogeneous displacements persist. The isolated-particle MSD is 6(T_eff/ξ0)[τ_p(e^(−t/τ_p) − 1) + t]: ballistic for t ≪ τ_p, diffusive for t ≫ τ_p. T_eff is not a thermodynamic temperature. Equal-time velocity correlations with nontrivial q-dependence are a purely nonequilibrium signature; slowdown and caging alone do not distinguish active from thermal glasses — [Ber19h](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5093240) [FT].
- Active matter is a broad framework (flocks, swimmers, cytoskeleton) built on hydrodynamic theories of polar and nematic order — [Mar13d](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.85.1143) [AB].

**Rough-potential diffusion**
- A 1-D Brownian particle in a smooth potential plus Gaussian roughness of RMS ε has D_eff = D·exp[−(ε/k_BT)²], valid on scales much larger than the roughness. The paper makes no MSD power-law or transient-subdiffusion claim — [Zwa88](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.85.7.2029) [FT].
- In the initial high-lr phase, the distance of the weights from initialization grows logarithmically with the number of updates. Hoffer et al. model this as a "random walk on a random landscape" with "ultra-slow" diffusion — [Hof17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08741) [AB].

**Optical binding**
- Optical binding is the optically mediated interaction *between* assembled objects. It can be attractive or repulsive and organizes colloids into arrays — [Dho10](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.82.1767) [AB].

**Jamming**
- Deep networks show a jamming-like transition between under-parametrized and over-parametrized regimes. With cross-entropy loss, the dynamics never stop in the over-parametrized regime — [Spi18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ab4c8b) [AB]. Jamming is the transition from flowing to rigid with disordered structure in both phases — [Liu10b](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-conmatphys-070909-104045) [AB].

### Inferences
- **The particles are distinguishable, input-pinned tracers, not interchangeable colloids.** Each token's position is a deterministic function of its fixed image content and of the weights. The dominant collective "interaction" over training time is therefore the *shared* weight update (the common "wind" 0.58R), not pairwise forces. Physically this is closer to many tracers coupled to a common, slowly fluctuating external field (ranks 1–2) than to a dense suspension. This weakens the glass analogy (rank 4), which needs neighbour-made cages.
- **Static vs dynamic heterogeneity.** Quenched input heterogeneity (some tokens sit in stiffer "traps") can by itself produce α2 > 0 and frame-level fluctuations. A glass claim needs *dynamic* heterogeneity, meaning that mobile tokens change identity over a finite time. Test this with the rank correlation of per-token mobility between successive windows: it stays ≈ 1 forever for static heterogeneity and decays for dynamic heterogeneity.
- **Constant short-lag speed after epoch 30 argues against simple aging.** Colloid aging gives fixed-lag mobility ∝ t_w^(−a) with a = 0.05–0.5 ([Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349)). Our a ≈ 0 between epochs 30 and 200 is consistent with a stationary NESS (rank 1) or an equilibrated supercooled liquid, not with an aging glass. Aging could still show up at long lags (two-time test, Q4).
- **Neighbour persistence 0.78 → 0.71 needs clarification.** If it is measured at a fixed 10-epoch lag as a function of t_w, the falling value means restructuring *speeds up* with age. That is the opposite of aging and may come from the ongoing PR collapse, which redefines kNN neighbourhoods in fewer effective dimensions. If it is a function of lag, it is the decay of a bond-breaking-like function ([Yam98b](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.3515)), and τ_b ≫ 200 epochs.
- **Mapping the measured exponents onto Weeks–Weitz caging.** Assume stationary increments, and define c = −⟨Δr01·Δr12⟩/⟨|Δr01|²⟩ for consecutive equal intervals. Then ⟨|Δr02|²⟩ = 2⟨|Δr01|²⟩(1 − c), so the local exponent over a lag doubling is γ ≈ 1 + log2(1 − c). This is my derivation, consistent with the approximation described in [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5). Our γ = 0.20 (background) implies c ≈ 0.43; γ = 0.34 (tool) implies c ≈ 0.37. These are predictions to check directly.
- **The OU and glass pictures predict different shapes of c(Δt).** For a stationary OU process with autocorrelation e^(−Δt/τ), c(Δt) = (1 − e^(−Δt/τ))/2 rises monotonically to 0.5 (my derivation). For a glass, c peaks at ≈ 0.5 in the plateau and then *decreases* toward 0 at cage escape ([WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5)). This is the cleanest single discriminator available from the existing checkpoints.
- **"Cooling" is a relaxation after a quench at fixed T, not a temperature schedule.** Because lr is constant after warmup, the 100× drop in speed over the first ~30 epochs is relaxation from a random initial condition at fixed noise. This is the standard aging protocol (quench at t = 0, then wait t_w). The [Bai18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ab3281) result (aging while the loss decreases, stationary afterwards) is therefore the right template.
- **Lane formation and optical binding are metaphors.** Lane formation contributes at most one test: perpendicular mobility of tool tokens should be enhanced before separation and suppressed after. Optical traps are fully restoring, whereas our "traps" are saddle-like, so the optics vocabulary would mislead reviewers.

## Q2. Which ML-physics papers already model tokens or representations as interacting particles, and do they predict our observations?

### Takeaway
The token-as-particle literature (Geshkovski–Letrouit–Polyanskiy–Rigollet and its successors) is rigorous but describes evolution over **depth** in attention-only models with fixed weights. It predicts clustering and metastable multi-cluster states over layers. It says nothing about training time, and the analysed models exclude the MLP. Neural collapse, the SGD/AdamW stationary-state papers (Mandt, Jastrzębski, Kunin, Adhikari, Yaida, Kosson) and the simplicity-bias papers (stochastic collapse, weight-decay rank, large-step sparsification) together cover most of our training-time observations: phase separation, constant temperature, continued dimensional collapse, and constant laser rotation. **Caging of representations** is predicted by none of them, except trivially as bounded fluctuations normal to the solution manifold ([Pas23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.02563)).

### Cited Findings
**Tokens as interacting particles (time = depth)**
- Tokens are modelled as particles on S^(d−1) moving by ẋ_i = P_{x_i}(Σ_j e^(β⟨Qx_i, Kx_j⟩)/Z_{β,i} · Vx_j). β is an inverse temperature. LayerNorm becomes the tangent projection P — [Ges23b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10794) [FT].
- For Q = K = V = I in d ≥ 3, tokens converge to a single cluster for every β ≥ 0 (Theorem 6.1). Convergence is exponential for d ≥ n with uniform random initial tokens, but the rate degenerates at large β. The token matrix in the limit has rank ≤ 1 — [Ges23b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10794) [FT].
- Multi-cluster metastable states are seen numerically, for example 2 clusters at β = 4 and 3 at β = 9 on the circle; their lifetime is posed as an open problem. A link to Kuramoto synchronization holds at β = 0 on the circle. **The analysed model excludes feed-forward/MLP layers, and "time" is depth, not training** — [Ges23b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10794) [FT].
- Metastability is proven for tokens initialized in well-separated caps: the lifetime T2 ≥ (ε(1−α)/n)·e^((1−α)β), exponential in β. Within each cap, the squared pairwise distance drops below 2e^(−λβ) after time T1. A staircase energy profile (plateaux followed by rapid mergers) is proven for a modified merging dynamics on the circle. The multi-cluster states are not proven to be saddles; the paper calls the region a "slow manifold". Time is again depth — [Ges24b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.06833) [FT].
- The type of limiting object (a single point, polytope vertices, hyperplanes, or subspaces) depends on the spectrum of the value matrix V, and cluster locations are set by the initial tokens ("leaders") — [Ges23](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-2493) [AB]. Mean-field (PDE / Wasserstein-flow) versions show meta-stable clustered phases whose structure depends on β — [Bru24](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.23228) [AB]. A synthesis covers global clustering after long multi-cluster metastable states, exact clustering rates for an equiangular reduction, and how normalization changes contraction speed — [Rig25](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.01868) [AB].

**Rank collapse / oversmoothing (time = depth)**
- Without skip connections or MLPs, pure self-attention output converges *doubly exponentially* to rank 1 ("token uniformity"). Skip connections and MLPs prevent this degeneration — [Don21d](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03404) [AB].
- Rank collapse at initialization makes query and key gradients vanish. It can be avoided by depth-dependent scaling of the residual branches — [Noc22](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2206.03126) [AB].
- In ViTs, self-attention acts as a low-pass filter. Deep stacks keep mostly the DC (common) component of the feature maps, which shows up as patch uniformity — [Wan22as](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2203.05962) [AB].
- In trained ViTs, along depth, patch tokens show strong late-stage coherence toward their mean direction, and updates collapse to low rank in late layers — [Jac25](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.19941) [AB].

**Neural collapse (time = training)**
- NC1–NC4: within-class variability collapses (Σ_W → 0, measured by Tr(Σ_W Σ_B⁺)/C); centred class means form a simplex ETF with cosines −1/(C−1); the classifier aligns with the class means (self-duality); and the decision reduces to the nearest class centre. NC1 falls by often several orders of magnitude on a log axis and **continues well after the terminal phase begins** (≥ 99.9% train accuracy). Test accuracy improves only modestly during that phase (median +0.35 points) and drops in some runs. Measurements are on **last-layer** features with stepwise lr decay (wd 5·10⁻⁴; 1·10⁻⁴ for ImageNet) — [Pap20](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2015509117) [FT].
- In ViTs (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100), separation fuzziness D = Tr(SS_w SS_b⁺) decays exponentially with depth after a spike at layer 1, D^(l) = D^(1)e^(−γl) (R² ≥ 0.97). The pattern breaks for embedding dimensions 256 and 512. Final-layer D decays exponentially over epochs and then levels off. **No numerical γ is given and no foreground/background patch analysis is done** — [Var25](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3602733) [FT].
- A "law of data separation": separation improves at an approximately constant geometric rate across layers in modern vision networks — [He22g](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2221704120) [AB]. Within-class covariance falls relative to between-class covariance with depth; in top layers the class-mean subspace aligns with the leading singular vectors of the weights — [Ran23b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92b8437f0fe8524b7904b0cf51014d2a3e8adc77) [AB]. Most of the reduction in intra-class variance happens in shallower layers, and angular separation grows with depth — [Par23e](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.02760) [AB].

**SGD as Langevin / NESS (time = training)**
- Constant-lr SGD simulates a Markov chain with a stationary distribution, which can be tuned to approximate a posterior — [Man17e](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04289) [AB]. Under an isotropic-noise SDE approximation, the equilibrium distribution depends only on the ratio lr/batch, and a higher ratio favours wider minima; the scaling breaks for too-large lr or too-small batches — [Jas17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04623) [AB].
- SGD minimizes a potential that is generally *not* the training loss. Its most likely trajectories are closed loops ("out of equilibrium") because the gradient noise is highly anisotropic, with covariance rank as small as ~1% of the dimension — [Cha17c](https://doi.org/10.1109/ITA.2018.8503224) [AB].
- After convergence at constant lr, local step size stays constant while global displacement keeps growing *super*-diffusively: ‖θ_k − θ*‖² ∝ k^c with c = 1.075 (ResNet-18), 1.085 (ResNet-34), 1.37–1.62 (VGG variants) on ImageNet. c rises with trajectory length (1.069 at 20 epochs to 1.306 at 100 epochs for ResNet-18). Larger lr lowers c and can make the motion subdiffusive. The stationary state is a NESS with nonzero phase-space currents. Under the paper's assumptions, Cov_ss(θ) = (ησ²/(S(1−β)))·(H + λI)⁻¹H, i.e. ∝ η/S — [Kun21](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01626) [FT].
- In constant-lr SGD, stationary probability currents are nonzero for minibatches drawn with and without replacement, so detailed balance is broken. The cause is a violated Einstein relation (mobility ηI vs a state-dependent diffusion D). Mean entropy production is ≈ 3·10⁻⁴ per step (regression) and ≈ 3·10⁻³ per step (MNIST), read from a figure, and fluctuation theorems hold (⟨e^(−σ)⟩ within 0.02% and 2% of 1). The stationary state is not Boltzmann over the training loss. Scaling lr and batch by the same factor leaves the stationary solution unchanged within the approximation — [Adh23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.03521) [FT].
- Yaida's stationarity identities for SGD: FDR1 ⟨θ·∇f⟩ = (η/2)⟨Tr C̃⟩, and with momentum ⟨θ·∇f⟩ = ((1+μ)/(2(1−ν)))·η⟨v²⟩. These are *necessary, not sufficient* for stationarity. The relative mismatch |O_L/O_R − 1| falls to ~10⁻³ at constant lr, and equilibration is slower at smaller lr. In the harmonic approximation ⟨f − f0⟩ ≈ (η/4)·Tr C̃ — [Yai18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00004) [FT].
- In the under-parametrized regime, SGD reaches a stationary state, and an FDT effective temperature can be defined via DMFT — [Mig21](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ac841d) [AB].

**Rotational equilibrium (AdamW + weight decay)**
- For scale-invariant weight vectors, the equilibrium expected angular update per step is η̂_r = √(2ηλ(1−β1)/(1+β1)) for AdamW and √(2ηλ/(1+α)) for SGDM. The AdamW equilibrium norm is ≈ √(ηC/(2λ)). The transient lasts ~1/(ηλ) steps, depending on the initial norm. The predicted rate is the same for all such vectors (no gradient term). **Per-step rotation is not net long-term rotation, which depends on correlations between successive updates (App. L).** Scale-sensitive vectors can rotate at unbalanced rates — [Kos23c](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17212) [FT].

**Simplicity bias that continues on the plateau**
- *Stochastic collapse*: SGD noise makes invariant sets (vanishing neurons, identical neurons) attractive even when gradient flow repels them. In 1-D the attractivity rate is α = L″(0) + ½D″(0), with D ∝ η/B. Prolonged training at lr 0.1 keeps reducing the fraction of independent neurons after training loss and test accuracy have plateaued, and a later lr drop then gives better final test accuracy (VGG-16/CIFAR-10, ResNet-18/CIFAR-100, 8 replicates). Operational criteria: a neuron "vanishes" if its in/out norms are < 10% of the layer maximum; two neurons are "identical" if their normalized parameter distance is < 10% — [Che23z](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ad6429) [FT].
- With SGD + weight decay, normalized weight matrices are ε-approximable at rank ≤ m_l·B·log(2/ε)/(2μλ). The bias is minimal without weight decay. The theorem does **not** predict an indefinite decrease over time; empirically, ranks fall early and then level off — [Gal22f](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05794) [FT].
- At large step sizes, training loss "stabilizes" while SGD noise keeps sparsifying features. Example: DenseNet block-4 feature sparsity of 50–60% (small steps) vs 10–20% (large steps); CIFAR-100 test error of 60% vs 35%. This is shown for training-loss plateaus; the paper does not claim it happens after test-performance plateaus — [And22b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.05337) [FT].
- Continued training of a network that already performs the task produces slow sparsification. Learning splits into fast familiarity, slow implicit regularization, and a steady state of null drift — [Rat24c](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.90069) [AB].
- Under online SGD with L2, a two-layer linear autoencoder's representations drift by rigid rotation along the degenerate minimum-loss manifold, with bounded mean-reverting fluctuations normal to it. The drift is ordinary (Brownian) angular diffusion with D ∝ η³ (Gaussian inputs: D_sr = η³γ⁴(n+2)/(16(1−γ))). A **more frequent stimulus drifts more slowly**: D_b ≥ D_a, where a is the frequent stimulus — [Pas23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.02563) [FT].
- Dimension studies: the intrinsic dimension of representations is orders of magnitude below layer width; across layers it rises and then falls, and last-layer ID predicts test accuracy — [Ans19](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12784) [AB]. Local and global ID (not the participation ratio) show early expansion and later compression across layers. Noise-injected SGD compresses more. The comparison is before vs after training only, with no epoch trajectory — [Rec19](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00443) [FT]. Embeddings can span a lower-dimensional subspace ("dimensional collapse") even in contrastive learning — [Jin21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348) [AB].

- **Kosson check, order of magnitude (my calculation, under assumptions).** ηλ = 5·10⁻⁴ × 0.05 = 2.5·10⁻⁵. With β1 = 0.9, η̂_r = √(2·2.5·10⁻⁵·0.1/1.9) ≈ 1.62·10⁻³ rad ≈ 0.093°/step. A project note in "Przeciwne wiązki w treningu ViT" assumes ~35k images per epoch, which with B = 32 gives ≈ 1,094 steps/epoch (to be verified). Then 1/(ηλ) = 40,000 steps ≈ 37 epochs, and the e-fold time of the squared norm, 1/(2ηλ) = 20,000 steps, is ≈ 18 epochs. Both are in line with "collimation by epoch 30, constant rotation afterwards". Net rotation per epoch would be ≈ 101° if all steps added coherently and ≈ 1.62·10⁻³·√1094 ≈ 0.054 rad ≈ 3.1° for a random walk. The measured 2.2°/epoch sits just below the random-walk value, which would mean slightly anticorrelated successive updates. Caveats: W2 columns are not scale-invariant (Kosson's derivation assumes scale invariance), and the steps-per-epoch figure is unverified.
- **NC predicts our phase separation only if tool vs background acts like a class label.** The detection loss presumably supervises tool tokens (box regions) differently from background. That makes the separation a label-driven drift (the "external field" of ranks 3 and 7), not a demixing driven by token–token interactions. This favours the vocabulary of "field-driven segregation / neural collapse" over "spinodal demixing".
- **Pas23's frequency effect plausibly explains why tool tokens are more mobile.** Background tokens are the frequent "stimulus" and should drift and fluctuate less than the rarer tool tokens; we measure exponent 0.20 vs 0.34. Kob–Andersen offers a parallel physical metaphor: the minority B species is more mobile. Both are analogies; Pas23 is a linear-autoencoder model.
- **The MLP "brake" and its compressive field run against "MLPs prevent collapse".** The [Don21d](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03404) claim is about architecture and depth. Our measurement is the learned block-6 MLP field over training. Reporting the negative divergence (−0.016 → −0.025 per dimension) as an MLP-driven compression is a genuinely new observation relative to that literature.
- **The "liquid middle / solid boundary" prediction for depth.** In a Langevin-trained "thermal deep learning machine", layers near the input and output boundaries are solid-like (replica overlap ≥ 1/e, persistent) and middle layers are liquid ([Hua25t](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06789) [FT], details in Q3). By analogy, block 6 (next to the output head) should be among the most "solid" blocks. Test this by repeating the MSD/overlap analysis on blocks 2–4.

- No source predicts **caging of representations** in a nontrivial (glassy) sense.

## Q3. Which papers measured MSD, caging or aging of representations or weights over training, and what exponents did they report?

### Takeaway
All quantitative MSD and aging measurements found are in **weight space**, not representation space. They report the following. Late-training *subdiffusion* α ≈ 0.78 (ResNet-14, [Che20g](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2022.01.019)). Post-convergence *superdiffusion* c ≈ 1.07–1.6 (ImageNet CNNs, [Kun21](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01626)). Short-time weight MSD ∝ τ^0.7 with aging collapse in τ/τ_w (RNNs, [Her24e](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.111.055307)). Subaging μ = 0.709 ± 0.017 (MNIST, [Seg22](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ac8f1b)). Aging only while the loss decreases, with no MSD plateau in over-parametrized nets ([Bai18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ab3281)). **No caging** under Langevin training, weight overlap ∝ t^(−0.5), and dynamic heterogeneity and aging only at low T ([Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010)). Our token-space exponents (0.20 and 0.34) are far lower than any weight-space value. No published representation-space MSD over training time was found to compare against.

### Cited Findings
- **Baity-Jesi et al. 2018/2019** — [Bai18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ab3281) [FT]. Observable: weight MSD Δ(t_w, t_w+t) = (1/M) Σ_i (w_i(t_w) − w_i(t_w+t))². Training used SGD at **constant lr** (toy net lr 0.1 B = 100; MNIST FC lr 0.01 B = 128; small CNN on CIFAR-10 lr 0.01 B = 100; ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100 lr 0.01 B = 64). They found aging (characteristic time growing with t_w) in the loss-decreasing window t1 < t_w < t2, and nearly stationary, diffusive motion after t2. There is no intermediate MSD plateau in over-parametrized nets. Under-parametrized nets (10 hidden units) show small-lag collapse (a possible Edwards–Anderson plateau) plus aging. No raw MSD exponent was fitted; the noise-rescaled Δ/D ∝ t. Conclusion: slowdown comes from an increasing number of flat directions — "Barrier crossing does not seem to play any role."
- **Chen et al. 2022 (Neural Networks)** — [Che20g](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2022.01.019) [FT]. Time-averaged weight MSD over a window T = 1,000 steps. Early training (t_w < t0): subdiffusion at short lags (α1 < 1) and superdiffusion at long lags (α2 > 1, roughly 1.05–1.45 across settings). Late training: subdiffusion with **α = 0.78** (ResNet-14, CIFAR-10, B = 1024, lr 0.1). Crossover at t0 = 21,000 steps. The authors attribute this to a fractal-like landscape (fractal dimension ≈ 1.8 for a 2-D projection) and heavy-tailed gradients (Lévy stability index 1.09 → 1.58). The crossover is not tied to a loss or accuracy plateau, and no cage or random-environment language is used.
- **Kunin et al. 2021/2024** — [Kun21](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01626) [FT]: post-convergence superdiffusion with c from 1.075 to 1.616 (see Q2). Higher lr gives lower c.
- **Herrera Segura, Montoya, Tapias 2022** — [Seg22](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ac8f1b) [FT]. Weight MSD; t* defined by MSD(t_w, t_w+t*) = Δ* (Δ* = 10⁻² or 10⁻³). Constant lr 0.05 SGD (B = 100 for the synthetic LJAT19 task, B = 50 for MNIST). **Subaging t* ∝ t_w^μ with μ = 0.709 ± 0.017** across the under-parametrized MNIST phase; a ~t_w^0.7 guide line is drawn for LJAT19. Plateau-like low-displacement portions appear in the under-parametrized regime, but the paper does not call them caging.
- **Hertz & Tyrcha 2024/2025 (PRE)** — [Her24e](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.111.055307) [FT]. RNNs on Bach chorales, SGD with B = 300. Weight MSD D(τ_w, τ_w+τ), noise-corrected to Δ. Short times: Δ ∝ τ^α with **α ≈ 0.7** for widths 30, 68 and 90 (a fit gives 0.66 for w = 30). Aging collapse in τ/τ_w is clearest when under-parametrized (w = 30), is transient near and above the interpolation transition, and is compatible with log(τ/τ_w) at large ratios. No χ4 was measured and no quantified caging plateau was found.
- **Kerr Winter & Janssen 2025 (PRR)** — [Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) [AB]. MNIST and CIFAR-10 nets trained by overdamped Langevin dynamics. They find a topology-trivialization transition and the jamming-like transition. **No diverging relaxation times at nonzero T and no caging.** The weight overlap follows a power law with exponent ≈ −0.5, in agreement with mode-coupling theory, and time–temperature superposition holds. **Dynamic heterogeneity and aging appear only at low T.**
- **Huang et al. 2025 ("thermal deep learning machine")** — [Hua25t](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06789) [FT]. Weights *and* hidden representations are dynamical variables under Langevin MD at k_BT = 10⁻⁵ (L = 10, binary MNIST 0/1). A layer is liquid if its replica overlap q_l* < 1/e and solid otherwise. The profile is solid–liquid–solid, with solid penetration depth ξ_s ~ ln α. Middle-layer relaxation times plateau for t_w ≳ τ_th ≈ 10⁴. Between τ0 ≈ 10³ and τ_th there is aging, and accuracy rises roughly logarithmically. Boundary layers stay out of equilibrium. **No caging regime is identified.**
- **Yaida 2018** — [Yai18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00004) [FT]: FDR-based stationarity diagnostics at constant lr (see Q2).
- **Hoffer et al. 2017** — [Hof17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08741) [AB]: distance of the weights from initialization ∝ log(number of updates) in the early high-lr phase.
- **Pashakhanloo & Koulakov 2023** — [Pas23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.02563) [FT]: the only representation-level diffusion result found. It is a theory with simulations: linear angular MSD on the solution manifold with D ∝ η³.
- **Zhang et al. 2025** frame grokking as "computational glass relaxation": memorization resembles a fast quench into a glass, and generalization a slow relaxation, with no entropy barrier — [Zha25cr](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.11411) [AB].
- The deep-search summary notes that direct quantitative MSD exponents, van Hove functions and χ4 measurements on networks remain sparse, and that constant-step SGD can be stationary without obeying equilibrium FDT — [DS summary, glassy-dynamics deep search] [DS].

### Inferences
- **Our exponents are lower than all weight-space exponents (0.70–0.78 subdiffusive; 1.07–1.6 superdiffusive).** Three non-exclusive explanations: (i) the Procrustes alignment and normalization by R remove the diffusive rotational and scale modes (cf. [Pas23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.02563)); (ii) token states are a projection of weights through a fixed input onto a nearly constrained (low-loss) set, so most weight-space diffusion is invisible in function space (the flat directions of [Bai18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ab3281)); (iii) genuine caging. The weight-space papers cannot be cited as *support* for our token caging. They are context only.
- **Late-training regimes in the literature are near-stationary in the over-parametrized case** ([Bai18](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ab3281); [Her24e](https://doi.org/10.1103/physreve.111.055307) aging breaks down at long times; [Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) aging only at low T). This matches our constant speed after epoch 30. Whether the small ViT on the surgical-tool data is under- or over-parametrized matters for the expected presence of aging. If it reaches ~zero training loss it is over-parametrized, and aging should be weak.
- **Concrete numbers to quote as a contrast.** An aging test on our token MSD should be compared with μ ≈ 0.71 ([Seg22](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ac8f1b)), μ ∈ [0.5, 1] in polymer glasses ([Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587)), and the colloidal-glass fixed-lag decay a ≈ 0.23–0.31 ([Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349)).

### Gaps
- The full text of [Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) was not accessible (abstract only). Its MSD shapes, χ4 values and aging exponents were therefore not extracted.
- No paper was found that measured MSD, α2, van Hove or χ4 of **token or activation states** across training checkpoints. The representational-drift literature (neuroscience-motivated) uses angular diffusion, not glass diagnostics.

## Q4. Which 3–5 physics diagnostics would most convincingly confirm or refute "colloidal glass with phase separation", what do they need, and how should this be framed?

### Takeaway
Five diagnostics separate the three live hypotheses: (A) OU/NESS tracers in harmonic cages, (B) a glass with cage escape and dynamic heterogeneity, and (C) a slowly drifting structure (continuing collapse) under (A). The diagnostics are: the cage-anticorrelation curve c(Δt); the non-Gaussian parameter and van Hove function with a static-heterogeneity control; a two-time aging test on uniformly spaced checkpoints out to epoch 400; χ4 plus bond-breaking dynamic heterogeneity; and a "temperature" (lr/batch) branching test. The first four use only existing token positions plus 20 new checkpoints and cost minutes of CPU. The fifth needs a few short training branches.

### Cited Findings
(The formulas and expected behaviours below are from the sources indicated. How they apply to our data is in Inferences.)
- α2 is ≈ 0 at short and long lags and peaks at the cage-rearrangement time Δt*, which marks the end of the MSD plateau — [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5) [FT]. The 3-D definition is α2 = 3⟨r⁴⟩/(5⟨r²⟩²) − 1 — [Kob95](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.51.4626) [FT].
- Cage anticorrelation via ⟨x12⟩(r01): linear with slope −c for caged particles, breaking down at r_cage. c ≈ 0.5 mid-plateau and decreasing at large Δt — [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5) [FT].
- Aging: τ(t_w) ~ t_w^μ; the subaging exponent is extracted at fixed Δ* — [Seg22](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ac8f1b) [FT], [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT]. In colloids, fixed-lag MSD ∝ t_w^(−a) — [Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349) [FT].
- χ4(t) = N[⟨C²⟩ − ⟨C⟩²] peaks near τ_α — [Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587) [FT]. The overlap order parameter Q(t) and its self part give χ4, and S4(q,t) gives ξ4 — [Lac03](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1605094) [AB]. Bond-breaking uses two thresholds A1 and A2; τ_α ≈ 0.1·τ_b — [Yam98b](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.3515) [FT].
- Stationary covariance ∝ η/S for SGD(M) — [Kun21](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01626) [FT]. Rescaling lr and batch together leaves the stationary state unchanged — [Adh23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.03521) [FT], [Jas17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04623) [AB]. DNN Langevin dynamics obey a form of time–temperature superposition — [Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) [AB]. The colloidal cage size is roughly lag-independent (a static property), while cage lifetimes grow strongly approaching φ_g — [WW02cp](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0104(02)00667-5) [FT], [WW02prl](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.89.095704) [AB].
- Low-rank collapse needs weight decay (minimal without it) — [Gal22f](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05794) [FT]. Long high-lr phases increase collapse, and an lr drop slows it — [Che23z](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ad6429) [FT].

### Inferences

#### Diagnostics to compute next (ranked by discriminating power per unit cost)

**Data prerequisites.** Add the 20 checkpoints at uniform spacing (e.g., every 10 epochs from 210 to 400), and ideally fill 40–200 to a uniform 10-epoch grid if spare checkpoints exist. Two-time analyses need many (t_w, Δt) pairs with equal Δt. Compute everything (i) with and without Procrustes, (ii) with and without normalization by R, and (iii) in "cage-relative" coordinates Δr_i − (1/k) Σ_{j ∈ kNN_i(t_w)} Δr_j, which removes collective drifts (no citation retrieved for this definition). Treat the 12 frames as independent samples (bootstrap over frames for error bars). Report background and tool separately throughout.

**D1. Cage-anticorrelation curve c(Δt) and conditional response ⟨x12⟩(r01)** (the strongest single discriminator; existing data suffices on the uniform part of the grid)
- c(Δt) = −⟨Δr_i(t, Δt)·Δr_i(t+Δt, Δt)⟩ / ⟨|Δr_i(t, Δt)|²⟩, averaged over tokens i, origins t ≥ 40 epochs, and frames.
- ⟨x12⟩(r01) = ⟨Δr12·Δr̂01⟩ binned by r01 = |Δr01|. A linear part with slope −c and a breakdown radius r_cage defines the cage size.
- Consistency check: γ_est(Δt) ≈ 1 + log2(1 − c(Δt)) should reproduce the measured local MSD slopes (0.20 → c ≈ 0.43; 0.34 → c ≈ 0.37).
- **Verdict rule:** if c(Δt) rises monotonically toward 0.5 and stays there → harmonic/OU cages, no escape (hypothesis A). If c(Δt) peaks and then falls toward 0 at the largest lags while γ(Δt) rises back toward 1 → cage escape, a glass/supercooled signature (B). If c ≈ 0 while the MSD stays subdiffusive → the subdiffusion comes from a slowing drift (C) or from alignment artefacts.

**D2. Non-Gaussian parameter and self van Hove function, with a static-heterogeneity control**
- For an effective dimension d, α2(Δt) = d⟨|Δr|⁴⟩/((d+2)⟨|Δr|²⟩²) − 1 (the general-d form of the Kob–Andersen 3-D definition). Because the cloud is anisotropic (PR ≈ 3–5), prefer per-principal-axis 1-D values α2^(k) = ⟨Δx_k⁴⟩/(3⟨Δx_k²⟩²) − 1 on the top-k PCs of the cloud, or work on displacements whitened by the stationary displacement covariance.
- G_s(Δx, Δt) histograms on log scale for Δt = 10, 20, 40, 80 epochs, compared with a Gaussian of equal variance and with an exponential tail exp(−|Δx|/λ).
- Static-heterogeneity control: repeat D2 on per-token-normalized displacements Δr_i/⟨|Δr_i|²⟩^(1/2)_t (each token rescaled by its own RMS over time). A glass keeps α2 > 0 with a peak at Δt*, while purely static heterogeneity drops toward α2 ≈ 0.
- **Verdict rule:** a peak of α2 at a finite Δt* that survives the per-token control, together with fat tails, supports B. A flat α2 ≈ 0 (or one explained by static heterogeneity) supports A.

**D3. Two-time aging test** (needs the extra checkpoints to epoch 400)
- MSD(t_w, Δt) = ⟨|r_i(t_w+Δt) − r_i(t_w)|²⟩ for t_w ∈ {40, 80, 120, 160, 200} epochs and Δt up to 200 epochs. Alternatively use the overlap Q(t_w, Δt) = (1/N) Σ_i Θ(a − |Δr_i(t_w, Δt)|), with a set near the plateau value of √MSD.
- Characteristic time t*(t_w) from MSD(t_w, t_w+t*) = Δ*, fitted as t* ∝ t_w^μ (as in [Seg22](https://doi.org/10.1088/2632-2153/ac8f1b)). Also fit the fixed-lag mobility MSD(t_w, Δt = 10) ∝ t_w^(−a) (as in [Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349)).
- **Verdict rule:** collapse in Δt alone (μ ≈ 0, a ≈ 0) means time-translation invariance, i.e. a stationary NESS (A) or an equilibrated supercooled liquid. μ ≈ 0.5–1, or a ≈ 0.05–0.5, means an aging glass. Our constant short-lag speed already suggests a ≈ 0.

**D4. Dynamic heterogeneity: χ4 and bond-breaking**
- χ4(Δt) = N[⟨Q(Δt)²⟩ − ⟨Q(Δt)⟩²], averaged over origins t_w within each frame, then over frames. Subtract per-frame means to remove frame-to-frame static differences. A glass predicts a peak near τ_α, where c(Δt) decays in D1. Stationary OU tracers give a featureless χ4 near the independent-particle value.
- Bond-breaking (an upgrade of the current neighbour persistence): the bond set at t0 is the 10 nearest neighbours within distance ≤ A1·d_10. A bond breaks when the distance exceeds A2·d_10 with A2 > A1 (hysteresis, so vibrations within the cage do not count, as in [Yam98b](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.3515)). Measure N_bond(Δt)/N_bond(0), τ_b, and the stretching exponent.
- Mobility persistence (my proposal, no citation retrieved): Spearman ρ between per-token |Δr| in [t, t+Δt] and in [t+Δt, t+2Δt]. Dynamic heterogeneity makes ρ decay over ~τ_α, while static input heterogeneity keeps ρ ≈ const.
- Image-space clustering of the 10% most mobile tokens. Do they form contiguous patch clusters (e.g., at the tool boundary, i.e. "interface" tokens), as fast colloids do ([Wee00](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.287.5453.627), [Cou02](https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/15/1/349))?

**D5. "Temperature" branching test (requires training; the decisive test for the NESS picture)**
- From the checkpoints at epochs 100 and 200, run 15–20 epoch branches with (η, B) ∈ {(η/2, B), (2η, B), (η, 2B), (2η, 2B)}. Save every 1–2 epochs, plus one short step-resolved segment (every 10 steps for ~1 epoch) for the short-lag regime and the Kosson per-step angle.
- NESS/OU predictions ([Kun21](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01626), [Jas17](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04623), [Adh23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.03521)): the MSD plateau height scales ≈ ∝ η/B; (2η, 2B) ≈ (η, B); the relaxation time in steps scales ∝ 1/η. AdamW changes the exact form, so treat this as an empirical scaling test.
- Glass prediction: the cage size stays nearly fixed (structural), while the escape time and the α2 peak position are strongly T-sensitive. Check for time–temperature superposition (curves collapse after rescaling time), which [Win25](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevresearch.7.023010) reports for Langevin-trained DNNs.
- Kosson check: the measured per-step angle of W1 rows and W2 columns vs √(2ηλ(1−β1)/(1+β1)) ≈ 1.6·10⁻³ rad, and the net angle per epoch vs √(steps)·η̂_r.
- Add-ons: (a) a 5–10-epoch lr→0 cosine "quench" probe from epochs 40, 100 and 200. The token positions after the quench define "basin centres"; the distance between instantaneous and quenched positions measures the cage amplitude; and changes of basin centre between t_w values measure cage escape. This is an inherent-structure-style analysis, for which no citation was retrieved. (b) A weight-decay-0 rerun or branch to test whether the PR collapse is the weight-decay low-rank bias ([Gal22f](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05794)) or stochastic collapse ([Che23z](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ad6429)). (c) The fraction of independent MLP neurons over epochs, using the Che23z criteria (vanishing: in/out norm < 10% of the layer max; identical: normalized distance < 10%). Collimation from 154 to 31 effective directions should correspond to a falling independent fraction.

**Also cheap, and needed for the phase-separation claim:** the NC1-type separation metric for tool (t) vs background (b) per checkpoint, S(t) = Tr(Σ_W Σ_B⁺)/2. For two classes this reduces to the within-class variance along the mean-difference axis divided by the squared mean difference, up to a class-weight factor; for equal weights it is 2·(û^T Σ_W û)/|μ_t − μ_b|² (my derivation; compute it directly with a pseudo-inverse). Plot it on a log axis against epoch, marking the IoU plateau (~40) and the brake onset (~80). Repeat across blocks 1–6 to get the depth law ([Var25](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3602733), [He22g](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2221704120)).

**Compute and data budget:** D1–D4 and the NC metric need only the stored token positions (46 checkpoints × 12 × 600 × 192 floats ≈ 64 M floats ≈ 0.25 GB in float32), at O(T²·N·d) cost — seconds to minutes on a CPU. D5 needs about 8 branches × 15–20 epochs of training plus one step-resolved epoch. Error bars come from bootstrapping over the 12 frames; with only one seed, a second seed for D1–D3 would strengthen any glass claim considerably.

#### How to phrase this in an article
- Safe framing: "We describe token states as tracer particles whose 'time' is training time. After an initial relaxation (≈30 epochs), their short-lag mobility is stationary at constant learning rate, consistent with a non-equilibrium steady state of AdamW/SGD [Kun21, Adh23, Kos23c]. Token displacements are strongly subdiffusive (MSD ∝ Δt^0.20–0.34), indicating confinement ('caging') relative to the training noise."
- Say "caging" only in the operational sense (sublinear MSD with a measured anticorrelation c(Δt)), and call it "glass-like" only if D1, D2 and D4 pass. Example wording: "glass-like caging with cage escape (α2 peak at Δt* ≈ … epochs, χ4 peak …)", or else "harmonic confinement without detectable cage escape".
- For phase separation, say "label-driven segregation of tool and background tokens, analogous to within-class collapse and between-class separation in neural collapse [Pap20], here at the patch-token level within images". Avoid "spinodal demixing" unless an interaction-driven mechanism is shown.
- For dimensional collapse on the plateau, say "continued simplification at constant learning rate, consistent with noise-driven collapse toward simpler invariant sets [Che23z] and the low-rank bias of weight decay [Gal22f]". Then state which ablation (lr-decay probe or wd = 0) was run.
- Name the analogies explicitly as one of two kinds: (i) quantitative models with tested predictions (NESS scaling, rotational equilibrium, NC metrics, the cage-anticorrelation relation), and (ii) metaphors used only for intuition (lasers, lane formation, optical binding, active matter). Keep the metaphors in the discussion section, not the results.
- Cite the contrasting DNN-glass evidence directly: "Langevin-trained DNNs show no caging [Win25], and over-parametrized SGD becomes stationary and diffusive after the loss decreases [Bai18]. Our token-space subdiffusion is therefore not expected a priori from weight-space results."

#### Reviewer risks
1. **Alignment artefact.** Procrustes and R-normalization can manufacture subdiffusion by removing the rotational and scale diffusion modes ([Pas23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.02563) predicts exactly a rotational drift). Mitigation: report unaligned, aligned and cage-relative MSDs.
2. **Distinguishable particles with quenched disorder.** Each token has its own fixed input, so α2 > 0 or χ4 can come from static heterogeneity. Mitigation: the per-token normalization control and mobility persistence (D2, D4).
3. **"Temperature" is not a temperature.** SGD/AdamW noise is anisotropic, state-dependent and violates detailed balance ([Adh23](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2306.03521), [Cha17c](https://doi.org/10.1109/ITA.2018.8503224)), and constant speed does not imply equilibrium ([Ber10b](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.83.587)). Use "effective noise level set by lr/batch" and show the D5 scaling.
4. **No interactions in the measured force field.** The block-6 MLP acts on each token separately, so physically "caging by neighbours" is not mechanistically possible within the measured field. Interactions come only through attention and through the shared weight updates. Be explicit that the cage is a training-dynamics confinement, not a neighbour cage, unless shown otherwise.
5. **Sparse time sampling and a single seed.** 26 irregular checkpoints make two-time functions and exponent fits fragile; 0.20 vs 0.34 may not be significant. Mitigation: a uniform grid to epoch 400, bootstrap over frames, and a second seed.
6. **Depth-time conflation.** The Geshkovski-type clustering theorems are about depth and exclude MLPs ([Ges23b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10794), [Ges24b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.06833)). Citing them as predicting *training-time* clustering would be an error.
7. **The neural collapse scope.** NC is defined for last-layer features of class-labelled samples with lr decay ([Pap20](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2015509117)). Patch tokens inside images, at block 6 under constant lr, are a different setting. Phrase as "NC-like metrics" and report the actual Tr(Σ_W Σ_B⁺) curve.
8. **The plateau may be an lr noise floor.** IoU "saturation" at constant lr can hide progress that an lr decay would reveal ([Che23z](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ad6429), [And22b](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2210.05337)). Run the lr-decay probe before claiming that dimensional collapse continues "without functional benefit".
9. **Kosson applicability.** The rotational-equilibrium formulas assume scale-invariant weights. MLP weights in a pre-LN block are only partly scale-invariant ([Kos23c](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17212) notes that scale-sensitive vectors can rotate unevenly), and the steps-per-epoch figure is unverified. Present the 2.2°/epoch match as order-of-magnitude only.

### Gaps
- I found no published application of D1–D4 (α2, van Hove, cage anticorrelation, χ4) to **token or activation trajectories over training time**. The protocol therefore has to be justified from colloid and simulation practice rather than from ML precedent.
- I found no AdamW-specific fluctuation–dissipation relation or stationary-covariance formula. The Yaida FDRs and the Kunin covariance are for SGD/SGDM.
- I did not retrieve a source for inherent-structure (quench) analysis or for cage-relative displacements, so both are proposed without citation.
- The steps per epoch for the seed-3047 run were not verified against the training config (the 35k images/epoch figure comes from an earlier project note in "Przeciwne wiązki w treningu ViT").
