## Q1. Optical tweezers: why the force field around a trapped particle has a rotational (curl) part, how it is measured, and how big it is relative to the restoring stiffness

### Takeaway
Optical trap fields are non-conservative because radiation pressure (the scattering force) has the form "intensity × fixed propagation direction". Its strength varies across the beam, perpendicular to the direction it pushes, so its curl is nonzero. Phase gradients, spin/orbital angular momentum and multi-beam asymmetry add more rotational terms. The standard local tool is the linearised stiffness (Jacobian) matrix split into a symmetric (conservative) part and an antisymmetric (rotational, Ω) part. This split is measured with cross-correlation asymmetries, probability currents or maximum-likelihood fits. In ordinary single-beam tweezers the rotational part is only about 1–4 % of the radial stiffness. In vortex/OAM beams and many-particle optical matter it can be comparable to the restoring part or larger, which produces complex eigenvalues, spirals and even instability.

### Cited Findings
**Physical cause of the curl**
- Gradient (intensity-gradient) forces are conservative; radiation pressure is not. In a static tweezer the probability flux of a trapped sphere traces a steady toroidal vortex, the "fountain of probability". Model: a harmonic well plus radiation pressure along the optical axis proportional to the local intensity, F = −k r + f1 I(r) ẑ, with small parameter ε = f1/(kσ). The fit gave ε = 0.11 ± 0.04 for 2.2 µm silica spheres. One net cycle took about 9 s, with drift speed about 150 nm/s at 7.5 mW. The circulation rate scales as P0^½ and T^½, so the particle would not circulate without thermal noise. [FT] — [Roichman et al. 2008, PRL 101, 128301](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.128301)
- Mechanism in words: the scattering force falls off strongly away from the optical axis, and "this inhomogeneity of the scattering force subsequently generates a nonconservative contribution". 3-D force maps of a Rayleigh particle (10 nm resolution, fN precision) show a curl that grows radially away from the axis, with a vortex-like curl pattern. The transverse stiffnesses differed by k_x/k_y = 1.7, and the curl is smaller along the polarisation axis because intensity falls off less steeply there. For large particles (geometric optics) the curl pattern can reverse sign. [FT, excerpts] — [Wu, Huang, Tischer, Jonas, Florin 2009, PRL 103, 108101](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.108101)
- Phase gradients in the light field also produce forces in traps, separate from intensity gradients. [ABS] — [Roichman et al. 2008, "Optical forces arising from phase gradients", PRL 100, 013602](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.013602)
- Spin angular momentum in elliptically polarised tweezers adds to radiation pressure. Its curl contributes forces and torques even on isotropic spheres. A single sphere shows stochastic Brownian-vortex circulation that maps out the force field. [ABS] — [Ruffner & Grier 2012, PRL 108, 173602](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.173602)
- Optical vortex (LG) beams can have complex force constants, so trapping must be stabilised by ambient damping. Depending on damping, the particle shows stable trapping, periodic or aperiodic orbits, or escape. The 3×3 matrix K_jk = ∂f_j/∂x_k at the zero-force point has transverse eigenvalues (a+b ± √((a−b)² + 4dg))/2. These are complex iff −4dg > (a−b)², i.e. when the torque-producing antisymmetric coupling beats the transverse stiffness anisotropy. For a cylindrically symmetric vortex, K_trans = a ± i d. The simulations report a supercritical Hopf bifurcation as damping decreases. [FT] (The read_pdfs agent flagged that the printed critical-damping formula, Eq. I.17, looks dimensionally inconsistent.) — [Ng, Lin, Chan 2010, PRL 104, 103601](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.103601)
- In purely azimuthal LG beams the dynamics is locally non-conservative. Some equilibria are only "conditionally stable", i.e. unstable zero-force points that damping can stabilise. The regimes are classified by the stiffness-matrix spectrum and the damping constant. [ABS] — [Kiselev & Plutenko 2016, PRA 94, 013804](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.94.013804)
- Refractive (ray-optics) trapping forces can be non-conservative near a stable equilibrium even without radiation pressure. Near equilibrium, a generic real 3×3 matrix has one real eigenvalue and a complex pair; the imaginary part sets a single circulation plane. Equipartition of potential energy still holds. Dissipated power is ⟨dW/dt⟩ = 4k_BT ξ²/(κ_x+κ_y), proportional to temperature. [FT] — [Saberi & Gittes 2011, JOSA B 28, 2369](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.28.002369)
- Non-spherical particles feel first-order (linear-term) non-conservative effects that do not shrink as beam power increases. For spheres the effects are second order and only seen at low power. [ABS] — [Simpson & Hanna 2010, PRE 82, 031141](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.82.031141) (has an erratum)
- In vacuum traps, birefringence-induced rotation–translation coupling can cause a Hopf bifurcation: the trapping point destabilises and limit cycles appear. Before the bifurcation, probability currents swirl around the stable point. [ABS] — [Simpson, Arita, Dholakia, Zemánek 2021, PRA 104, 043518](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.104.043518); transverse-spin forces and non-equilibrium orbits: [Svak et al. 2018, Nat. Commun. 9, 5453](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07866-8) [ABS]
- Optical force on a sphere of any size can be split analytically into a gradient of a scalar and a curl of a vector potential (multipole theory). [ABS] — [Jiang, Chen, Ng, Lin 2016, arXiv:1604.05138](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.05138)
- Review of non-conservative optical forces. [ABS; abstract unavailable] — [Sukhov & Dogariu 2017, Rep. Prog. Phys. 80, 112001](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6633/aa834e)

**Many-particle optical matter (the closest analogue to a 192-D Jacobian)**
- For N bound particles the 3N×3N force-constant matrix K_ij = ∂F_i/∂ΔX_j is real but asymmetric (non-Hermitian). Write K = S + A with S = (K+Kᵀ)/2 and A = (K−Kᵀ)/2. Eigenvalue pairs coalesce at exceptional points and then become complex conjugates. Complex modes appear for N < 10, "most modes are complex by N > 50", and all clusters with N > 70 have complex modes. The minimum spacing of S's eigenvalues shrinks with matrix size. In random-matrix tests, the asymmetry needed for the first complex pair correlates with that spacing (r ≈ 0.75 uniform, 0.65 Gaussian) and goes to 0 as size grows. So even slight asymmetry makes complex eigenvalues in large matrices. Ambient damping can restore stability. [FT] — [Li, Liu, Lin, Ng, Chan 2021, Nat. Commun. 12, 6597](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26732-8)
- A non-Hermitian trapping force can turn a saddle potential into a stable state. Equipartition fails under non-Hermitian forces. [ABS] — [Li, Cao, Ng 2024, Nat. Commun. 15, 1963](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46058-5)

**How the rotational part is measured**
- 2-D linear model: with the coordinate system chosen so that ∂_y f_x = −∂_x f_y, J/γ = [[−φ−Δφ, Ω], [−Ω, −φ+Δφ]] (as reconstructed). Here φ is the mean restoring rate, Δφ the stiffness anisotropy and Ω the rotation rate. The eigenvalues are λ1,2 = −φ ± √(Δφ² − Ω²), so they are complex iff Ω > Δφ. The rotational term "is invariant under a coordinate rotation". For Ω = 0 the point becomes unstable once Δφ ≥ φ; adding rotation keeps it stable. "For values of Ω ≥ φ, the rotational component … becomes dominant over the conservative one." The cross-correlation difference r_xy(Δt) − r_yx(Δt) and the sum of autocorrelations are coordinate-invariant; the difference is nonzero only if Ω ≠ 0. [FT, excerpts] — [Volpe, Volpe, Petrov 2007, PRE 76, 061118](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.061118)
- Torque detected from the auto- and cross-correlations of a bead in a harmonic trap driven by an OAM beam. [ABS] — [Volpe & Petrov 2006, PRL 97, 210603](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.97.210603)
- Saddles and centres in microflows were measured with an optically trapped probe. Four spinning vaterite spheres were placed around the probe. For an incompressible flow tr J = 0, so only saddles (det J < 0, e.g. det J = −4/ρ for two spheres) or centres (det J > 0, e.g. 2/ρ for four spheres) can occur. The CCF was zero for the saddle and nonzero for the centre. The measured rotational component was γΩ⟨x²+y²⟩ = 5.7–6.4 fN·µm in a k = 78 fN/µm trap. [FT] — [Volpe, Volpe, Petrov 2008, PRE 77, 037301](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.77.037301)
- FORMA, a maximum-likelihood fit of F ≈ F0 + J0 r from local displacements. It splits J0 = J_c + J_r with J_c = (J0+J0ᵀ)/2 (principal stiffnesses k1*, k2*, orientation θ*) and J_r = (J0−J0ᵀ)/2 = [[0, −γΩ*], [γΩ*, 0]], with Ω* = (J0,21 − J0,12)/(2γ). It identifies stable, unstable and saddle equilibria in speckle fields, including "a rotating unstable point". In LG beams, Ω* ≈ 1.8·l s⁻¹ (linear polarisation) and 1.8·(l±1) s⁻¹ (circular), with k ≈ 0.56–0.85 pN/µm read from the figure. It needs about 10× less data than earlier methods, and the neighbourhood must be small enough for the field to be linear. [FT] — [Pérez García et al. 2018, Nat. Commun. 9, 5166](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07437-x)
- Brownian-vortex theory uses a Helmholtz split F = −∇U + ∇×A. The probability current is j = µρF − D∇ρ, and circulation is read from the streamlines of j (toroidal rolls). The first-order correction is driven by p(r) = ∇U·(∇×A), the projection of the solenoidal force onto the conservative one, which controls flux reversals and topology changes. No single typical ε is given; fitted ε depends on particle radius and can change sign. [FT] — [Moyses, Bauer, Grosberg, Grier 2015, PRE 91, 062144](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.91.062144)
- A Brownian vortex is "not simply a deterministic response to the solenoidal component". It comes from the interplay of advection and diffusion, and its circulation can reverse with temperature or laser power. [ABS] — [Sun, Lin, Darby, Grosberg, Grier 2009, PRE 80, 010401](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.80.010401); discrete minimal models: [Sun, Grier, Grosberg 2010, PRE 82, 021123](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.82.021123) [ABS]
- Exact or perturbative steady states and currents for overdamped and underdamped particles in non-conservative trap models. [ABS] — [Mangeat et al. 2019, PRE 99, 052107](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.99.052107); [Mangeat, Guérin, Dean 2021, J. Stat. Mech. 113205](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/ac3907)
- An external solenoidal field (drag near a liquid–air interface) on an optically trapped bead produces a tunable Brownian vortex. [FT, excerpts] — [Khan 2011, PRE 83, 041408](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.83.041408)
- Force-field vortices, optical vortices and Brownian vortices are distinct things; the paper reviews the mechanisms of circulation. [ABS] — [Lenton et al. 2020, Proc. SPIE 11297](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2550418)

**Typical rotation-to-restoring ratios (measured)**
- Linear model f_ρ = −k_ρ ρ + ε k_ρ z, f_z = −ε k_ρ ρ − η k_ρ z, with ε estimated from the slope at τ = 0 of DCCF_ρz(τ) = ⟨ρ(t+τ)z(t)⟩ − ⟨z(t+τ)ρ(t)⟩. The paper's Table 1: for 0.45 µm beads, ε = 1.0 ± 0.1 % (6 mW), 1.7 ± 0.2 % (3.2 mW) and 2.4 ± 0.2 % (1 mW). For 1.25 µm beads, ε = 0.7 ± 0.1 % (4 mW), 1.2 ± 0.1 % (1.4 mW) and 3.8 ± 0.3 % (1 mW). Axial/radial ratio η ≈ 12–16 %. Relative to the weaker axial spring the cross term is about 5–32 % (derived by the read_pdfs agent, not a table column). At tens of mW the DCCF cannot be told apart from zero, so the authors conclude non-conservative effects are negligible except at very low power. [FT] — [Pesce et al. 2009, EPL 86, 38002](https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/86/38002)
- Disagreement in the literature: Roichman et al. 2008 stress that non-conservative effects matter for calibration, while Pesce et al. 2009 find them negligible at usual powers. A formal Comment on Roichman et al. was also posted (Huang, Wu, Florin, arXiv:0806.4632); I did not read its content. [ABS for the comment] — [Roichman et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.128301); [Pesce et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/86/38002)

### Inferences
- **The exact mathematical analogue in our app (derived here).** Laser c contributes f_c(x) = (z_c(x) − z̄_c) v_c, where z_c = GELU(w_c·LN(x) + b_c). This is an "intensity" z_c(x) times a fixed "propagation direction" v_c, the same form as Roichman's scattering term f1 I(r) ẑ. Its Jacobian is the rank-1 matrix J_c = g_c v_c w̃_cᵀ, with g_c = GELU′(z_c) and w̃_c = ∇_x z_c / g_c = (∂LN/∂x)ᵀ w_c. Its antisymmetric part ½ g_c (v_c w̃_cᵀ − w̃_c v_cᵀ) is a rotation in the plane span{v_c, w̃_c}, with Frobenius norm |g_c|·‖v_c‖‖w̃_c‖·|sin θ_c|/√2, where θ_c is the angle between v_c and w̃_c. So **a laser's curl comes entirely from the misalignment between its "push direction" v_c and its "intensity-gradient direction" w̃_c**. This mirrors how the optical curl comes from ∇I ⊥ k̂ (Wu et al. 2009). A laser with v_c ∥ w̃_c is the analogue of a pure gradient force: it is conservative, with energy ∫GELU (the tied-weight case of Im et al. 2016, see Q3).
- **Every misaligned laser is a "saddle element" (derived here).** The symmetric part of a rank-1 matrix g v w̃ᵀ has eigenvalues (g/2)(v·w̃ ± ‖v‖‖w̃‖), which have opposite signs unless θ_c ∈ {0, π}. So a superposition of many randomly oriented lasers produces a sign-indefinite symmetric part, i.e. many restoring and many expanding strain directions. This is a likely mechanistic reason for the earlier "half restoring / half expanding" observation. Note: the observation itself is close to the random null; see Q4.
- **Divergence as summed alignment (derived here).** tr J = Σ_c g_c (v_c · w̃_c) = Σ_c g_c ‖v_c‖‖w̃_c‖ cos θ_c. A net compressive divergence (tr J < 0) that grows during training means the active lasers (g_c > 0) are increasingly *anti*-aligned (cos θ_c < 0). Lasers in the negative-slope GELU region (z_c ≲ −0.75, where GELU′ < 0) contribute with flipped sign. This is the analogue of the optical trap's total stiffness tr K = −(k_x+k_y+k_z).
- **Translating the optics ratios.** In tweezers the relevant dimensionless number is (rotation)/(restoring), e.g. Ω/φ or ε. A natural ViT analogue is ρ_rot = ‖A‖_F/‖S‖_F of the full J, or |Im λ|/|Re λ| for each eigenpair. Conventional tweezers sit at ≈ 0.01–0.04, while vortex beams and large optical-matter clusters reach O(1). A 192-D non-symmetric matrix behaves like a large optical-matter cluster (Li et al. 2021), so complex eigenvalues are expected generically.
- **Terminology warning.** "Brownian vortex" in the optics sense needs thermal noise and a non-zero steady probability current (Sun et al. 2009 stress it is *not* the deterministic solenoidal part). The ViT field has no diffusion, so what the app shows is the deterministic solenoidal part of a linearised field. Call it "rotational (solenoidal) component" or "focus/spiral", not "Brownian vortex", unless you add a noise model (e.g. token dropout or augmentation) and measure currents.

## Q2. Classifying 3-D fixed points (node, focus, saddle, saddle-focus) and which invariants are slice-independent

### Takeaway
At a zero of a 3-D field, the standard classification uses the invariants of the 3×3 velocity-gradient (Jacobian) matrix: P = −tr, Q, R = −det and the discriminant D (Chong, Perry & Cantwell 1990). D > 0 means a complex pair (focus type), D < 0 means three real eigenvalues (node/saddle type). Every one of these quantities depends on which 3-D slice you take. Only invariants of the full 192-D Jacobian are slice-independent: its spectrum, trace, Frobenius norms of the symmetric and antisymmetric parts, Henrici departure, numerical range, and the spectrum of its symmetric part.

### Cited Findings
- Chong, Perry & Cantwell: the trajectory geometry of three coupled linear ODEs "can be related and classified using three matrix invariants". The scheme is "valid for any smooth three-dimensional vector field", not only velocity. [ABS] — [Chong, Perry, Cantwell 1990, Phys. Fluids A 2, 765](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.857730)
- P, Q, R are defined through the characteristic polynomial of the 3×3 matrix, and the discriminant of that polynomial separates the topologies. [FT, excerpts; the excerpts drop the equations] — [Bechlars & Sandberg 2017, JFM 815, 223](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2017.40)
- For a trace-free tensor, the discriminant line is D_s = 27R_s² + 4Q_s³ (citation snippet). The quadrant names in the R–Q map are standard: the lower right (R > 0, D < 0) is "Unstable-Node/Saddle/Saddle (UN/S/S)", and "Stable-focus/Stretching (SF/S)" is the focus region. [FT, citation snippets] — [Khashehchi et al. 2021, J. Turbul. 22, 640](https://doi.org/10.1080/14685248.2021.1955121)
- The named topological regions are unstable focus/compressing, stable focus/stretching, unstable node/saddle/saddle and stable node/saddle/saddle. [ABS] — [Wan, Liu, Lu 2022, JFM 934](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2021.1038); compressible extension: [Suman & Girimaji 2010, J. Turbul. 11, N2](https://doi.org/10.1080/14685241003604751) [ABS; no abstract]
- "Spiralling compactness" of orbits (an imaginary/real-part measure) is proposed as a vortex-core criterion and related to the Q, Δ and λ2 criteria. [ABS] — [Chakraborty, Balachandar, Adrian 2005, JFM 535, 189](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112005004726)
- In optics, the same local classification is done on the 2-D/3-D stiffness matrix: stable vs unstable vs saddle (FORMA), and complex force constants (Ng). [FT] — [Pérez García et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07437-x); [Ng, Lin, Chan 2010](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.103601)
- Each 3×3 antisymmetric matrix acts as a cross product with one axis vector, so local non-conservative 3-D motion reduces to circulation in one plane. [FT] — [Saberi & Gittes 2011](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.28.002369)

### Classification scheme (formulas; standard linear algebra, stated here because the retrieved excerpts drop equations)
For M = P3 J P3ᵀ (3×3) at u*:
- Characteristic polynomial λ³ + Pλ² + Qλ + R = 0 with **P = −tr M**, **Q = ½(P² − tr M²)** (= sum of principal 2×2 minors), and **R = −det M**.
- Discriminant (Chong et al. convention): **D = 27R² + (4P³ − 18PQ)R + 4Q³ − P²Q²**. D > 0 gives one real eigenvalue plus a complex pair (focus family). D < 0 gives three real eigenvalues (node/saddle family). D = 0 is the degenerate boundary. For P = 0 this reduces to D = 27R² + 4Q³, which matches the D_s line quoted above.
- Split M = S_M + A_M (symmetric "strain" + antisymmetric "rotation"). Then tr M² = ‖S_M‖_F² − ‖A_M‖_F², so for trace-free M, **Q = ½(‖A_M‖_F² − ‖S_M‖_F²)** (Q > 0 means rotation dominates strain; this is the "Q-criterion" logic). The 3×3 antisymmetric part acts as ω × r with rotation axis ω, ‖ω‖ = ‖A_M‖_F/√2.
- Classes (sign convention: f is a force field, so the overdamped velocity is proportional to f and Re λ < 0 means attracting):

| Eigenvalues of M | D | Name (ours) | Chong et al. name (trace-free case) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 real, all < 0 | < 0 | stable node (pure trap) | — (needs P ≠ 0) |
| 3 real, two < 0, one > 0 | < 0 | saddle (index 1) | stable node/saddle/saddle (R < 0) |
| 3 real, one < 0, two > 0 | < 0 | saddle (index 2) | unstable node/saddle/saddle (R > 0) |
| 3 real, all > 0 | < 0 | unstable node | — |
| complex pair Re < 0, real λ < 0 | > 0 | stable focus (spiral trap, "attracting vortex") | — (needs P ≠ 0) |
| complex pair Re < 0, real λ > 0 | > 0 | saddle-focus (spiral in-plane, escapes along axis) | stable focus/stretching (R < 0) |
| complex pair Re > 0, real λ < 0 | > 0 | saddle-focus (spiral out, captured along axis) | unstable focus/compressing (R > 0) |
| complex pair Re > 0, real λ > 0 | > 0 | unstable focus | — |

  The two "saddle with vortex" cases should be reported as **saddle-focus of index 1 or 2**. The 2-D intuition from Volpe et al. 2007 carries over: rotation makes eigenvalues complex once it exceeds the in-plane strain anisotropy, and rotation adds +ω² to the 2×2 determinant, so it can turn a strain saddle into a focus. This is the same as the "non-Hermitian force converts a saddle into a stable state" result of Li et al. 2024.
- **Non-hyperbolicity warning (derived here).** LayerNorm's Jacobian (γ/σ)(I − 11ᵀ/d − x̂x̂ᵀ/d) kills the mean direction 1 and the radial direction x − μ. So the full J has at least two zero eigenvalues (right null space span{1, x−µ}). If P3 overlaps those directions, M gets eigenvalues near 0 and the class can flip under tiny perturbations. Report the smallest |λ(M)|/‖M‖ as a "hyperbolicity margin".

### Slice-independent vs slice-dependent quantities
- **Slice-dependent:** P, Q, R, D, the eigenvalues of M, the class label, the rotation axis, and the location u* (all depend on P3).
- **Slice-independent (full 192-D J at x̃_j):**
  - tr J (divergence) and the full spectrum {λ_i(J)}: fraction of complex pairs, spectral abscissa max Re λ, count of Re λ > 0.
  - ‖S‖_F, ‖A‖_F and **ρ_rot = ‖A‖_F/‖S‖_F** (invariant under orthogonal change of basis).
  - Spectrum of S: the inertia (numbers of negative, positive and zero eigenvalues), the numerical abscissa λ_max(S), and λ_min(S).
  - **Henrici departure from normality** δ(J) = √(‖J‖_F² − Σ|λ_i|²)/‖J‖_F, as used by Fernando & Guitchounts 2026 ([arXiv:2605.14258](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.14258), [FT]).
  - The numerical range W(J).
  - det J is unusable because of the LN null space; use the pseudo-determinant on the range if needed.
- Bridge between the two (derived here, standard Haar-measure moments; verify by Monte Carlo): for a Haar-random orthonormal k×d frame P, E[tr(PJPᵀ)] = (k/d)·tr J and E‖PAPᵀ‖_F² = k(k−1)/(d(d−1))·‖A‖_F². For a trace-free symmetric part S₀ with k = 3, E‖PS₀Pᵀ‖_F² = 6(2d−1)/(d(d−1)(d+2))·‖S₀‖_F². So for d = 192 a random 3-slice inflates the symmetric-to-antisymmetric energy ratio by about (2d−1)/(d+2) ≈ 1.97. **Random 3-D slices are therefore biased toward looking more strain-dominated (more saddle/node-like) than the full matrix by roughly a factor √2 in ‖A‖/‖S‖.** The app's P3 is not random, so calibrate with random slices (see Q4).

### Inferences
- Report the app's per-token class label alongside full-J invariants (ρ_rot, tr J, fraction complex, inertia of S). Without them a reader cannot tell whether a "vortex" in the slice reflects the full field.
- Chong et al. explicitly say the scheme applies to any smooth 3-D vector field, so using it for a 3-D *field restricted to a slice* is formally legitimate. But the restricted field P3 f(x̃ + P3ᵀu) is itself a 3-D vector field whose Jacobian is M, not J. The classification is exact for that projected field and only approximate for the 192-D one.

### Gaps
- The Chong et al. 1990 full text was not accessible (closed access). The discriminant formula and region names above come from standard algebra plus citation snippets, not a direct read of the paper.
- I found no optics paper applying PQR invariants to optical force fields. Optics uses the 2×2/3×3 stiffness-matrix eigenvalues directly.

## Q3. Rotational (non-gradient) components in learned neural vector fields, and whether anyone has characterised vortex/saddle topology of transformer MLP fields around tokens or tracked it during training

### Takeaway
The symmetric/antisymmetric split of a Jacobian is standard in ML (differentiable games, GAN numerics, conservativeness of auto-encoders and score models). For a one-hidden-layer MLP (exactly our block), Im et al. give the necessary and sufficient condition for the field to be conservative. Since 2025–2026, several preprints treat the transformer residual stream as a dynamical system. They report rotation-dominated, non-normal Jacobians with ~98 % complex eigenvalues (LLMs), rotational remainders of 4–45 % of drift (Pythia, 16-D PCA space), and saddle-dominated zeros of projected FFN fields (~83 % saddle-like). I found **no paper that does what the app does**: Newton-located zeros of a ViT MLP field in a 3-D slice, classified by PQR/eigenvalues and tracked over training. The closest are Mudarisov et al. 2026 (projected-field zeros, saddle labels, LLMs), Fernando & Guitchounts 2026 (full-Jacobian rotation vs training), Quemy 2026 (rotational share of depth flow) and Aubry et al. 2024 (ViT block Jacobians over training, SVD only).

### Cited Findings
**Symmetric/antisymmetric (potential/Hamiltonian) decomposition**
- The game Jacobian splits into a symmetric part (potential games, i.e. gradient descent on an implicit function) and an antisymmetric part (Hamiltonian games, which obey a conservation law). This motivates Symplectic Gradient Adjustment. [ABS] — [Balduzzi et al. 2018, ICML, arXiv:1802.05642](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05642); [Letcher et al. 2019, JMLR 20(84), arXiv:1905.04926](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04926)
- GAN training convergence suffers from Jacobian eigenvalues with zero real part and from eigenvalues "with big imaginary part". [ABS] — [Mescheder, Nowozin, Geiger 2017, arXiv:1705.10461](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10461)
- A Newton method on the Helmholtz split of multi-agent dynamics into irrotational (potential) and solenoidal (Hamiltonian) parts; it is attracted to stable fixed points and repelled by strict saddles. [ABS + citation snippets] — [Ramponi & Restelli 2021, AAAI 35, 11325](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i13.17350)

**Conservativeness of an MLP-shaped field (directly our block)**
- For r(x) = R·h(Wᵀx + b) + c, the field r(x) − x is conservative on a simply connected domain iff the Jacobian R·D_h′(x)·Wᵀ is symmetric for every x, where D_h′ = diag(h′(Wᵀx+b)). Tied weights (R = W) are sufficient but not necessary. In the tied case the energy is F(x) = Σ_k ∫h(u_k)du_k − ½‖x−c‖² (the RBM free energy for sigmoid units). Measured "symmetricity" of the Jacobian rises during training, curl falls on 2-D spirals, and contractive training is locally conservative near fixed points. [FT] — [Im, Belghazi, Memisevic 2016, AAAI 30, arXiv 2015](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v30i1.10268)
- Unconstrained score models are not conservative, which can hurt performance; the authors propose quasi-conservative regularisation via Hutchinson trace estimation. [ABS] — [Chao et al. 2022, ICML, arXiv:2209.12753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12753)
- A conservative + orthogonal (gauge) decomposition of diffusion-model vector fields. Conservativeness is neither necessary nor sufficient for exact sampling, but is desirable for local manifold information. [ABS] — [Horvat & Pfister 2024, arXiv:2402.03845](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.03845)

**Fixed points, saddles and rotations in trained networks**
- Find fixed and slow points of trained RNNs by minimising q(x) = ½‖F(x)‖², then linearise around them. Stable and unstable points reveal the mechanism. [ABS] — [Sussillo & Barak 2013, Neural Comput. 25, 626](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00409)
- Trained sentiment RNNs show approximate line attractors in the fixed-point topology. [ABS] — [Maheswaranathan et al. 2019, NeurIPS, arXiv:1906.10720](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10720)
- Non-normal (Schur-split) recurrent connectivity gives transient dynamics with unit-norm spectra. [ABS] — [Kerg et al. 2019, NeurIPS, arXiv:1905.12080](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12080)

**Transformers as vector fields around token representations**
- **Full-Jacobian eigendecomposition of production LLM blocks.** About 98 % of the mean-Jacobian eigenvalues are complex-conjugate pairs. Self-alignment ‖V_kᵀU_k‖_F²/k (1 means normal; ≈ k/d means a pure rotator) rises from ≈ 0.04 (layer 2) to ≈ 0.70 (layer 29) in Llama 3.1 8B. Henrici departure falls from 0.91 to 0.47 across depth. At initialisation (OLMo step 0) layers are nearly symmetric (self-alignment 0.635 / 0.951 / 0.979 for early / mid / late). After training these drop to 0.428 / 0.653 / 0.859, so rotation and non-normality are *installed by training*. The paper analyses the whole block, without separating MLP from attention, and has no shuffled-weight control for these statistics. Preprint (May 2026). [FT] — [Fernando & Guitchounts 2026, arXiv:2605.14258](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.14258)
- **Rotational share of the depth flow.** A quadratic drift fit to Pythia token flows is decomposed into the best cubic-potential gradient plus a remainder. The remainder ("rotational component") is 4–45 % of the explainable drift (held-out); one example transition L4→L5 has 89 % potential and 11 % remainder. At linear order the symmetric share of the fitted map is 0.53–0.84, versus a **shuffle-null of 0.50–0.54**. The analysis is done in a 16-D PCA space, not the full 768-D space. The author warns that "in a single plane most of the circulation is invisible". Preprint (Aug 2026). [FT] — [Quemy 2026, arXiv:2608.29706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29706)
- **Projected-field zeros of FFN steering fields.** The paper defines g_α(u) = P_u(Vu + αΦ(u)). Zeros are "nonlinear projective equilibria", classified by the eigenvalues of the tangent-space Jacobian as attractor, saddle or repeller. With the full field, 91/111 candidates are saddle-like, 11 repelling and 9 attractor-like. In the ablation study, 116/140 (82.9 %) are saddle-like for the full field and 118/140 (84.3 %) for the FFN-only field. Spectra often have positive real parts and nonzero imaginary parts ("saddle/rotational or transport-like"). Keeping only the tangential FFN component raises loss by 0.07–1.72, versus 7.19–12.96 for radial-only. The authors stress that these are "approximate-zero, local-spectrum diagnostics", not refined exact equilibria. Preprint (Aug 2026). [FT] — [Mudarisov, Burtsev, State 2026, arXiv:2608.02071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02071)
- **Self-attention Jacobians without an energy function.** Complex eigenvalues are identified with oscillatory components. Normalisation (RMSNorm) bounds the Jacobian spectral norm and suppresses oscillatory components. High-accuracy models sit near criticality (max Lyapunov exponent ≈ 0.1; 0.196 ± 0.016 in a language-modelling run). Energy-based symmetric self-attention has negative exponents and lower accuracy. [FT] — [Tomihari & Karakida 2025, arXiv:2505.19458](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.19458)
- **Block-Jacobian coupling over training, including ViTs.** Three CIFAR-10 ViTs (d = 192, depth 24, 3 heads, i.e. the same width as ours) were analysed. Coupling of the top singular vectors rises from ≈ 0.60–0.62 to ≈ 0.69–0.70 over training and correlates with test accuracy (R² = 0.93). **No eigenvalue or symmetric/antisymmetric analysis is reported**, only SVD. [FT] — [Aubry, Meng, Sugolov, Papyan 2024, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2407.07810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07810)
- Energy and gradient-flow readings of attention hold under structural assumptions: modern Hopfield networks [ABS] — [Ramsauer et al. 2020, arXiv:2008.02217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217); doubly-stochastic attention as a Wasserstein gradient flow [ABS] — [Sander et al. 2021, arXiv:2110.11773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11773); interacting-particle / clustering theory [ABS] — [Geshkovski et al. 2023, arXiv:2312.10794](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.10794), [Castin et al. 2025, arXiv:2501.18322](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.18322).
- In a reduced transformer model with a symmetric value matrix plus an aligned MLP, the dynamics is a gradient flow: only fixed points, no limit cycles, and saddles are unstable. With a non-symmetric V whose dominant eigenvalue is a complex pair, the point attractor is replaced by a great-circle limit cycle. [ABS] — [Volpert 2026, Mathematics 14, 3350](https://doi.org/10.3390/math14183350)
- Training an FFN in a mean-field transformer can make tokens leave the clustered regime near the final layers. [ABS] — [Isobe, Inoue, Imaizumi 2026, arXiv:2605.07772](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.07772)

### Inferences
- **Our block is literally an untied auto-encoder-shaped field (derived here).** f(x) = W2·GELU(W1·LN(x) + b) has the same structure as Im et al.'s r(x) with R = W2 and W = (W1·∂LN/∂x)ᵀ. So their condition applies directly: *the MLP field is conservative iff W2·diag(GELU′)·W1·∂LN/∂x is symmetric at every token state*. With per-neuron alignment v_c ∥ w̃_c (tied-like), the field would be the gradient of Σ_c ∫GELU. This gives a concrete, testable, mechanistic statement: **"curl" = departure from tied-like alignment between each neuron's read-in direction w̃_c and write-out direction v_c.**
- The literature supports these expectations for our ViT:
  - complex eigenvalues are generic (≈ 98 % in LLM blocks; Ginibre-type null ≈ 94 % for d = 192, see Q4);
  - saddle-type zeros dominate projected fields (≈ 83 %, Mudarisov 2026);
  - training increases non-normality in early and mid blocks (Fernando 2026). Block 6 of a 12-block ViT would be "mid", but this is an extrapolation from LLMs.
  
  A claim that "vortices and saddles appear" is therefore *expected*, not novel. The novelty must lie in (i) the per-laser mechanism (alignment), (ii) dimensionless trends over training compared with nulls, and (iii) a link to function, such as occlusion robustness, token classes or tracking.
- Mudarisov et al. use the same epistemic stance as the app (zeros of a projected field, local spectrum labels) and phrase results cautiously. Their wording ("approximate-zero, local-spectrum diagnostics") is a good model for our article.

### Gaps
- No found work classifies MLP-only (not whole-block) Jacobians of a ViT by eigenvalue topology, or tracks vortex/saddle labels over training epochs. The 2026 papers analyse LLM blocks or attention.
- Neural-ODE / residual-as-Euler-step literature (e.g. Chen et al. 2018) did not appear in my retrieved results, so I have no citation for it from the tools.
- "Bifurcations and loss jumps in RNN training" (Eisenmann et al. 2023) appeared in the deep-search list, but I did not read it. It may support "topology changes during training = bifurcations".

## Q4. Slicing: when a vortex or saddle in a 3-D linear slice reflects the full 192-D Jacobian, and which controls to run

### Takeaway
For a non-symmetric J, the eigenvalues of the compression M = P3 J P3ᵀ (Ritz values) need not approximate any eigenvalue of J. They are only guaranteed to lie inside the numerical range W(J), and their real parts lie between λ_min(S) and λ_max(S) of the symmetric part. Cauchy interlacing applies to the symmetric part of the slice, P3 S P3ᵀ, against S, not to J itself. So a slice saddle certifies only that S is indefinite, and a slice vortex certifies only that the antisymmetric part has weight in that plane (equivalently, that W(J) is not a real interval). Neither certifies an unstable or complex eigenvalue of J. The slice is exact only when P3 spans an invariant subspace of J (e.g. real Schur vectors).

### Cited Findings
- Rayleigh–Ritz estimates for Hermitian matrices obey Cauchy interlacing; the paper studies how far this localisation extends to non-Hermitian matrices. [ABS] — [Carden & Embree 2012, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 33, 1320](https://doi.org/10.1137/120872693). The Undermind summary (abstract-based) adds: "For non-normal operators, projected Ritz values lie within the numerical range but need not approximate eigenvalues of the full matrix."
- The field of values W(A) = {x*Ax : ‖x‖ = 1} is compact and convex and contains σ(A). For a principal submatrix A(α), W(A(α)) ⊆ W(A) (this extends to any orthonormal compression by unitary invariance, W(U*AU) = W(A)). Further: W(A) ⊂ ℝ iff A is Hermitian; Re W(A) = W(H(A)) = [λ_min(H(A)), λ_max(H(A))] with H(A) = (A+A*)/2; and if A is normal, W(A) is the convex hull of the eigenvalues. [FT, excerpt] — [Loisel 2018, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 39, 1726](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1148608)
- For non-normal A, "W(A) can be much larger than the convex hull of the spectrum". Low-dimensional projections (orthonormal V, VᵀAV) inherit W(VᵀAV) ⊆ W(A). [FT, excerpt] — [Frommer, Jacob, Kahl et al. 2019, arXiv:1912.10765](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10765)
- For non-normal iteration matrices, spectral information alone predicts only asymptotic behaviour; spectra may be highly sensitive to perturbation; the field of values is an intermediate tool. [ABS] — [Eiermann 1993, Linear Algebra Appl. 180, 167](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(93)90530-2)
- Random real Gaussian n×n matrices have few real eigenvalues. The expected number grows only like √n (the main asymptotic result, quoted from memory as E_n ≈ √(2n/π), because the retrieved abstract had the formula stripped), and the real fraction → 0 as n → ∞. [ABS] — [Edelman, Kostlan, Shub 1994, J. AMS 7, 247](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1231689-0)
- In large asymmetric matrices, the asymmetry needed to create complex eigenvalues goes to 0 as matrix size grows (level spacing of S shrinks). [FT] — [Li et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26732-8)
- Shuffle nulls for the symmetric share of a fitted linear map give 0.50–0.54, against 0.53–0.84 observed. A single 2-D plane hides most circulation. [FT] — [Quemy 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29706)
- At initialisation, transformer block Jacobians are nearly symmetric with eigenvalues clustered near Re λ = 1, so the init checkpoint is a natural baseline. [FT] — [Fernando & Guitchounts 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.14258)

### Inferences (derived here unless cited)
- **What a slice saddle means.** Sym(M) = P3 S P3ᵀ, so its eigenvalues interlace with those of S (Cauchy, valid because S is symmetric). Re λ(M) ∈ [λ_min(P3SP3ᵀ), λ_max(P3SP3ᵀ)] ⊆ [λ_min(S), λ_max(S)] (Loisel's property (g) applied to M plus W(M) ⊆ W(J)). A slice eigenvalue with Re > 0 therefore implies λ_max(S) > 0 (S indefinite, some locally expanding strain), but **not** that J has an eigenvalue with Re > 0. A non-normal J can have every Re λ(J) < 0 and still show slice saddles, through transient-growth directions.
- **What a slice vortex means.** A complex pair in M requires a non-zero antisymmetric part P3 A P3ᵀ in the slice, which is large enough relative to the slice's strain anisotropy (2-D analogue: Ω > Δφ, Volpe 2007). It does not imply that J has complex eigenvalues near that Ritz value. For d = 192, J almost surely has many complex pairs anyway (≈ 98 % in LLM blocks; Ginibre-type expectation ≈ 1 − √(2/(π·192)) ≈ 94 % complex, from the Edelman et al. asymptotic). So **the presence of complex eigenvalues is the null expectation**. Only their weight relative to a null is informative: ρ_rot, |Im|/|Re| distribution, Henrici δ.
- **"Half restoring / half expanding" is the null.** For a zero-mean random non-symmetric matrix, eigenvalues spread symmetrically about Re = 0 (circular law) and S has a symmetric spectrum, so ≈ 50/50 signs are expected without any learning. The informative signals are (i) the shift of the whole spectrum, tr J / d and its growth with training, and (ii) asymmetry in the inertia of S relative to the w–v shuffle null.
- **When the slice is faithful.** Eigenvalues of M equal eigenvalues of J iff P3 spans an invariant subspace of J, i.e. J P3ᵀ = P3ᵀ M. Measure the invariance residual η_inv = ‖(I − P3ᵀP3) J P3ᵀ‖_F / ‖J P3ᵀ‖_F. A small η_inv means the slice topology is a faithful piece of J. A real Schur basis of J gives exactly invariant 3-D slices (one real eigenvector plus a complex-pair plane, or three real), i.e. "faithful slices" by construction.
- **Why the app's P3 is special.** P3 comes from PCA of normalised output columns v_c, so it is aligned with the *range* (left side) of J but not its input side. M = Σ_c g_c (P3v_c)(P3w̃_c)ᵀ, so the slice sees the full push directions but only the in-slice part of each laser's gradient direction. This can inflate apparent misalignment, and hence rotation, or deflate it. Hence the need for slice controls.

### Controls we must run
1. **Full-J spectrum per token (slice-free).** Report the fraction of complex pairs, spectral abscissa, numerical abscissa λ_max(S), inertia of S, tr J, ρ_rot = ‖A‖_F/‖S‖_F, and Henrici δ. Do this per token, per frame, per checkpoint.
2. **Null models for J:**
   - (a) **w–v pairing shuffle**: permute output columns v_c across neurons. This keeps the marginals of W1 and W2 and the GELU′ statistics but destroys learned alignment. It is the key null for tr J and ρ_rot.
   - (b) **Initialisation checkpoint** (epoch 0) of the same architecture, as in Fernando 2026.
   - (c) **Frobenius-matched Gaussian matrices** projected through the same LN null space (Ginibre baseline for complex fraction and Re-sign balance).
   - (d) **Positive "no-curl" control**: replace J by S, or replace each v_c by ±‖v_c‖·w̃_c/‖w̃_c‖ (tied-like model, conservative by Im et al. 2016). The app should then show no vortices. If it still does, there is a bug or numerical artifact.
   - (e) **Positive "pure rotation" control**: add a known antisymmetric A_test and check that the app recovers its axis and rate.
3. **Slice controls:**
   - (a) ≥1000 Haar-random 3-frames per token: the distribution of class labels and of slice ρ_rot. Compare the app's P3 label frequencies with this distribution, correcting for the ≈ √2 strain bias derived in Q2.
   - (b) Faithful slices from real Schur vectors of J (top-|λ| or top-Re invariant subspaces).
   - (c) Slices from top-3 left / right singular vectors of J, from PCA of the 600-token cloud, and from top eigenvectors of S (pure strain) and of A (top rotation planes; the canonical form of A gives ⌊d/2⌋ = 96 rotation planes with rates ±iω_k).
   - (d) Report η_inv for each slice.
4. **Validity of the "trap" itself.** P3 f(u*) = 0 does **not** make u* an equilibrium of the 192-D field. Report the off-slice residual ‖(I − P3ᵀP3) f(x̃+P3ᵀu*)‖ / ‖f(x̃)‖. Run Newton from many starts (multiplicity, basins, convergence), report the hyperbolicity margin min|λ(M)|/‖M‖, and report the distance of u* from the token (is it inside the cell for a reason?).
5. **Convention robustness.** Vary the centering z̄_c (frame mean vs dataset mean vs none), the cell definition, and the LN-null-space overlap of P3 (component of P3 rows along 1 and x̃_j − μ).
6. **Discrete-map check.** A residual block applies x ↦ x + f(x) once, so local stability of the map needs |1 + λ| < 1, i.e. Re λ < −|λ|²/2. That is stricter than Re λ < 0. Report both, or justify the flow convention (small-step / ODE view).
7. **Nonlinear circulation check (analogue of Wu et al.'s curl maps).** Compute ∮ f·dl around small loops in the slice and compare with Stokes' prediction 2·area·(P3AP3ᵀ)_ij. Larger loops reveal nonlinear rotation beyond the linearisation.
8. **Per-laser attribution.** Compute cos θ_c between v_c and w̃_c, the contributions g_c v_c·w̃_c to tr J and each laser's share of ‖A‖_F. Track the cos θ_c histogram over training. Separately count lasers in the GELU′ < 0 regime (z_c ≲ −0.75).
9. **Numerics.** Compare the autograd Jacobian (exact GELU) with central finite differences, and check eigenvalue conditioning (pseudospectra / eigenvector condition numbers). Non-normal eigenvalues may be ill-conditioned (Eiermann 1993).
10. **Statistics.** Show distributions over tokens, frames, seeds and checkpoints, not single examples. Use dimensionless quantities (ρ_rot, tr J/‖J‖_F, |Im|/|Re|) so that trends are not artifacts of weight-norm growth during training.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Carden & Embree full text, so the precise non-Hermitian localisation theorems (how freely Ritz values can be placed within W(A)) are not quoted.
- I found no paper giving Haar-slice distributions of 3×3 topology classes for structured non-normal matrices. The bias factor above is my own derivation and must be checked by Monte Carlo.

## Q5. What is publishable and defensible about vortex/saddle "traps" in a ViT latent field, and what reviewers will challenge

### Takeaway
A defensible paper states results about the **local linear response of the MLP block at token states** using slice-free invariants of the full Jacobian, compared against explicit nulls. Examples: rotation/strain ratio, divergence, inertia of the symmetric part, and per-neuron read/write alignment. The 3-D slice "traps" should be presented as an **interpretive visualisation of zeros of a slice-projected field**, validated by faithful-slice and random-slice controls. They should not be presented as equilibria or dynamics of the network. The optical analogy is mathematically exact at the level of "intensity × fixed direction" force terms and is worth stating precisely. Physical vocabulary such as "Brownian vortex" or "trap" should be defined or avoided.

### Cited Findings (anchors for the claims)
- Rotation and non-normality in transformer Jacobians are learned (absent at init) and vary with depth. [FT] — [Fernando & Guitchounts 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.14258)
- The rotational share of the token flow is non-negligible, and the symmetric share exceeds the shuffle null. [FT] — [Quemy 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29706)
- Saddle-like labels dominate zeros of projected FFN fields; such labels are "descriptive" diagnostics. [FT] — [Mudarisov et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02071)
- Normalisation suppresses complex-eigenvalue (oscillatory) components and Lipschitzness. [FT] — [Tomihari & Karakida 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.19458)
- Conservativeness condition for MLP-shaped fields. [FT] — [Im et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v30i1.10268)
- Physical precedent for classifying traps by the symmetric (stiffness) and antisymmetric (Ω) parts of the local Jacobian, and for saddles vs centres. [FT] — [Volpe et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.061118); [Volpe et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.77.037301); [Pérez García et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07437-x)
- Physical origin of curl as "intensity × fixed direction" with transverse intensity gradient. [FT] — [Roichman et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.128301); [Wu et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.108101)
- Large non-symmetric force matrices generically have complex modes. [FT] — [Li et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26732-8)

### How to phrase it in an article (templates)
- "We linearise the MLP sub-block of block 6 at each token state and decompose its Jacobian J = S + A into a symmetric (strain, potential-like) and an antisymmetric (rotational, solenoidal) part, following the stiffness-matrix analysis of optical traps [Volpe 2007; Pérez García 2018] and differentiable games [Balduzzi 2018; Letcher 2019]."
- "Each hidden unit c contributes a rank-one term g_c v_c w̃_cᵀ, formally identical to a radiation-pressure force (intensity z_c along a fixed direction v_c) [Roichman 2008]. Its rotational part is proportional to |sin∠(v_c, w̃_c)|, and the field is conservative iff the summed Jacobian is symmetric [Im 2016]."
- "Relative to a read–write shuffle null, trained units become [anti-]aligned (median cos θ_c = …), which makes the local divergence tr J negative and growing over training, while the rotation-to-strain ratio ‖A‖_F/‖S‖_F stays at … (null …)."
- "For visualisation we restrict the field to a 3-D subspace and locate zeros of the *projected* field. Because J is non-normal, eigenvalues of the compressed 3×3 Jacobian are Ritz values constrained only to the numerical range of J [Loisel 2018; Carden & Embree 2012]. We therefore report slice labels together with (i) the invariance residual of the slice, (ii) label frequencies over random and Schur-aligned slices, and (iii) slice-free invariants."
- Wording: use "zero of the slice-projected field" or "projected trap" instead of "trap". Use "rotational (solenoidal) component" or "focus" instead of "vortex", or define "vortex" once as a complex eigenpair of the projected Jacobian. Use "saddle-focus of index k" instead of "saddle with vortex". Use "local linear response" instead of "dynamics". Do not use "Brownian vortex".

### Reviewer risks (expected challenges, with mitigation)
1. **Slice artifact.** "Your vortices/saddles come from the 3-D compression of a non-normal matrix." Mitigate with controls 3, 4 and 7 of Q4 and Ritz/numerical-range language.
2. **Not an equilibrium.** "P3 f = 0 does not mean f = 0; tokens never sit at u*." Report the off-slice residual and call u* a projected zero (Mudarisov et al. 2026 use the same caveat).
3. **No dynamics.** "A residual block is applied once with block-specific weights; there is no flow to converge to a fixed point." Frame the work as the geometry of a one-step update map. If you use the flow language, justify it and give the discrete-map stability criterion |1+λ| < 1.
4. **Genericity.** "Complex eigenvalues and saddles are generic for random non-symmetric matrices." Mitigate with the Ginibre/Edelman baseline, the w–v shuffle and init nulls, and dimensionless effect sizes. The finding is the *shift relative to null* and its training trend.
5. **"Half restoring / half expanding" is the null.** Present the divergence shift and the inertia asymmetry against the null, not the 50/50 split itself.
6. **Centering and LN conventions.** z̄_c subtraction moves the zero, and the LN null space makes J singular (non-hyperbolic points). Show robustness to centering and report hyperbolicity margins.
7. **Choice of P3.** It is built from W2 only and ignores W1, LN and the token distribution. Compare with Schur, SVD, random and token-PCA slices.
8. **Over-reaching physics analogy.** "Optical words without physics." Restrict the analogy to the exact mathematical correspondence (intensity × direction force; S/A split of stiffness; PQR classification). Explicitly disclaim noise-driven phenomena such as Brownian vortices and probability currents.
9. **Scale confounds over training.** Weight norms grow, so use normalised ratios and per-checkpoint nulls.
10. **Functional relevance.** "So what?" Tie the metrics to behaviour: e.g. correlation of ρ_rot or tr J with occlusion robustness, token class, tracking accuracy, or generalisation (as Aubry et al. 2024 did for coupling, R² = 0.93), or run interventions that symmetrise J (project out A) and measure the effect.
11. **Numerical reliability.** Eigenvalues of non-normal matrices are perturbation-sensitive [Eiermann 1993]. Report conditioning and check autograd against finite differences.
12. **Novelty.** Cite Fernando 2026, Quemy 2026, Mudarisov 2026 and Tomihari 2025 as close prior work. Claim novelty for (i) a ViT MLP-only analysis, (ii) the per-neuron alignment mechanism, (iii) training-time tracking of slice-free invariants with nulls, and (iv) the interactive 3-D projected-zero visualisation with faithfulness diagnostics.

### Per-paper notes (most useful first)
- **Volpe, Volpe & Petrov 2007, PRE 76, 061118** [FT excerpts]: the canonical 2-D linear model J/γ = −φI ± Δφ (anisotropy) + Ω (rotation), with λ = −φ ± √(Δφ²−Ω²). Coordinate-invariant observables are the CCF difference and the ACF sum. This is the template for a "rotation vs restoring" ratio. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.061118
- **Pérez García et al. 2018, Nat. Commun.** [FT]: FORMA. Local MLE fit of F0 + J0r, explicit J_c/J_r split, Ω* = (J21−J12)/(2γ); it finds stable, unstable and saddle points (including rotating ones). The recipe maps directly onto the per-token Jacobian split. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07437-x
- **Pesce et al. 2009, EPL** [FT]: the only paper found with tabulated rotation-to-restoring ratios (ε = 0.7–3.8 %, rising at low power). It uses the DCCF slope. https://doi.org/10.1209/0295-5075/86/38002
- **Roichman et al. 2008, PRL** [FT]: radiation pressure ∝ I(r)ẑ gives toroidal probability currents, ε = f1/(kσ) ≈ 0.11, circulation rate ∝ P^½ T^½. Its "intensity × direction" form matches each GELU neuron's output term. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.101.128301
- **Wu et al. 2009, PRL** [FT excerpts]: a direct 3-D curl map. Curl grows away from the axis, polarisation causes anisotropy (k_x/k_y = 1.7), and the curl can reverse for large particles. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.108101
- **Volpe, Volpe & Petrov 2008, PRE 77, 037301** [FT]: saddles vs centres in microflows, with tr J = 0 forcing saddle or centre, classified by det J. Shows that saddle and centre classification by a trapped probe is experimentally meaningful. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.77.037301
- **Ng, Lin & Chan 2010, PRL** [FT]: complex force constants in vortex beams; complex iff −4dg > (a−b)²; damping-stabilised; Hopf bifurcation. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.103601
- **Li et al. 2021, Nat. Commun.** [FT]: large asymmetric force matrices make complex modes inevitable via exceptional points; level-spacing argument; relevant scale (3N ~ 150–270 ≈ our d = 192). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26732-8
- **Moyses et al. 2015, PRE** [FT]: Helmholtz split −∇U + ∇×A; current j = µρF − D∇ρ; p = ∇U·(∇×A) controls flux reversal. Useful if a noise model is ever added. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.91.062144
- **Saberi & Gittes 2011, JOSA B** [FT]: 3-D linear non-conservative forcing reduces to circulation in one plane (the imaginary part of the complex pair); equipartition of potential energy survives. https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.28.002369
- **Im, Belghazi & Memisevic 2016, AAAI** [FT]: iff-condition for an MLP-shaped field to be conservative; tied weights give energy Σ∫h; symmetricity rises in training. The single most directly applicable ML result. https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v30i1.10268
- **Fernando & Guitchounts 2026, arXiv** [FT; preprint]: about 98 % complex eigenvalues; self-alignment and Henrici δ across depth; training (not architecture) installs rotation. A whole-block Jacobian, LLMs only. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.14258
- **Quemy 2026, arXiv** [FT; preprint]: rotational remainder 4–45 % of drift in a 16-D PCA space; symmetric share 0.53–0.84 vs shuffle null 0.50–0.54; "in a single plane most of the circulation is invisible". https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29706
- **Mudarisov, Burtsev & State 2026, arXiv** [FT; preprint]: zeros of a projected FFN field, ~83 % saddle-like, spectra with positive Re and nonzero Im; explicit "approximate-zero, local-spectrum diagnostics" caveat. The closest methodological twin of the app. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02071
- **Tomihari & Karakida 2025, arXiv** [FT]: complex eigenvalues = oscillatory modes; RMSNorm bounds the Jacobian norm and suppresses oscillations; good models are near-critical. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.19458
- **Aubry et al. 2024, ICLR 2025** [FT]: ViT (d = 192) block-Jacobian singular-vector coupling grows over training and correlates with accuracy (R² = 0.93). No eigen/curl analysis, which leaves a gap our work can fill. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07810
- **Balduzzi et al. 2018 / Letcher et al. 2019** [ABS]: symmetric (potential) vs antisymmetric (Hamiltonian) Jacobian split; the standard ML citation for the decomposition. https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05642 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04926
- **Mescheder et al. 2017** [ABS]: large imaginary parts of the Jacobian hurt convergence, i.e. rotation matters functionally in learning dynamics. https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10461
- **Sussillo & Barak 2013** [ABS]: fixed- and slow-point finding by minimising ½‖F‖², then linearisation. A methodological precedent for Newton-based trap finding. https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00409
- **Chong, Perry & Cantwell 1990** [ABS]: the PQR classification of 3-D critical points, valid for any smooth 3-D field. https://doi.org/10.1063/1.857730
- **Loisel 2018; Frommer et al. 2019; Carden & Embree 2012; Eiermann 1993** [FT excerpts / ABS]: numerical-range facts needed to state what slice eigenvalues can and cannot certify. https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1148608 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10765 ; https://doi.org/10.1137/120872693 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(93)90530-2
- **Edelman, Kostlan & Shub 1994** [ABS]: few real eigenvalues for random real matrices; the null baseline for the complex-eigenvalue fraction. https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1231689-0

### Inferences
- **A strong, defensible core claim, if the data support it:** "Training aligns or anti-aligns the read-in and write-out directions of MLP units, producing a compressive local divergence and a rotation-to-strain ratio distinct from read–write-shuffled and initial networks. Zeros of the field projected onto output-PCA slices are predominantly saddle-foci, a pattern that persists [does not persist] in invariant (Schur) slices." Everything in this claim can be checked against nulls and does not depend on one particular slice.
- **Weak claims to avoid:** "tokens are trapped", "the ViT forms vortices around colloids", "Brownian vortices in latent space", and any stability or dynamics claim based only on 3×3 slice eigenvalues.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed (non-preprint) study of eigen-topology of transformer MLP fields over training was found. The closest evidence is 2025–2026 preprints, so cite them as preprints.
- The functional relevance of rotation/strain metrics for vision transformers (beyond the LLM correlations) is not established in the found literature.
